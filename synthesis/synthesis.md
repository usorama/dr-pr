### The strategy in five sentences

4. The lighthouse is built **one flagship at a time**: **Patients for life** in his own practice first, peer teaching behind a doctors-only wall second, and a measured **Heart School** cohort third. Each one counts and publishes its results, including disappointing ones.
5. His **AI edge** is visible responsibility, not gadgets. It consists of doctor-verified Telugu patient explanations, an audited own-practice registry, a published AI charter and teaching peers how to judge AI. There is no avatar, no voice clone and no public chatbot.

### The three things to do first (this fortnight)

### The three biggest risks

## 3. The playing field

### 3.1 The rules, in their own words

**Conclusion:** the October 2026 text plus the binding 2002 Regulations leave one wide lane open: education, scholarship, teaching and factual information. Owning a practice relaxes nothing on promotion. It only gives him control of a factual footprint [11b, 30].

**Legal status (text).** The document is a Public Notice of **Guidelines** (No. R-13014/01/2024-Ethics, 6 October 2026), "read with" the 2002 Regulations. Where the two conflict irreconcilably, "the applicable statutory provisions and operative regulations shall prevail" (cl. 4.2). It is not a gazetted regulation. The State Medical Council enforces it, and the penalty ladder is one that "may be contemplated" (cl. 10.1) [30 A8].

### 5.7 The AI edge

- **What it is.** Workflows and visible responsibility, in order [18]:
  1. The **two-pipeline rule**.
  2. **Verified Telugu patient explanations** (15–20 condition templates; 2–3 minutes per patient; footer "Prepared with AI assistance and checked by Dr …").
  3. The **published charter** (ten points, ending with "No artificial me": no AI copy of his face or voice).
  4. **Teaching peers** "How to judge an AI tool in cardiology", using EAGLE (positive) against TRICORDER (negative) and FAVOR III Europe [31 1.17–1.19].
  5. An **audited own-practice registry**, with AI extraction validated against manual abstraction on 100 records.
  6. **Year 2:** a Telugu medical-AI evaluation with IIIT-H, and a reviewed Telugu Q&A library. Any retrieval-only assistant comes only after user testing and a lawyer's sign-off.
- **Why.**
  - Tools are no edge; anyone can buy them [18].
  - Telugu speech recognition has 25.1–28.9% word error across four systems [31 2.11], so a human Telugu reviewer is mandatory.
  - Independent doctors' cholesterol videos had 29% misinformation, and GPT-3.5 fabricated 47–55% of references in 2023 studies (GPT-4: 18%) [31 1.21–1.22].
  - Identical advice was rated less reliable and less empathetic when **labelled** as AI or human + AI (effect size about 0.26–0.29) [31 1.23]. So he shows the method and the checked work, and never leads with "AI" in self-description.
- **Cost.**
  - AI subscription about Rs 25,000 a year (estimate) [28].
  - Him: 10 one-off hours for templates and 12 for the peer talk.
  - The IIIT-H course (Rs 30,000, about 3 hours a week) is deferred to the 6-hour cap or year 2 [31 3.17].
- **Not doing.** An avatar, voice clone or dub of him; a public chatbot; patient data in consumer AI tools; AI on Apollo patients without permission; vendor advisory roles; "AI-powered cardiologist" claims; screening without a confirmatory pathway [18].

### 6.3 The approval and verification workflow

Every item passes these gates in order. Any "No" stops it.

1. **Topic** from the calendar. The friend proposes; he approves quarterly.
2. **Draft** from his recorded words or notes. AI is given the sources; it is never asked to recall facts [22 §4.5].
3. **Claims ledger.** Each claim is listed, the source is opened by a human, and the figure is pasted in. A source that will not open means the sentence is deleted.
4. **Compliance checklist** (file 11 §9(i) with the 11b additions): subject, words, patients, money, solicitation, credential line, AI.
5. **Telugu review and back-translation** for any instruction.
6. **Second reader** for Tier A.
7. **His sign-off**, logged with date, platform, sources, approver and time.
8. **Publish**, with the "Medically checked / next review" stamp and sources.
9. **Monitor comments to script.**

### 6.4 The quality standard

- **Source hierarchy** [22 §4.1]: guidelines (Indian first where they exist) > systematic reviews > named trials > Indian registries > observational studies ("associated") > his opinion, labelled. Press releases, preprints, other doctors' videos and AI output are not sources.
- **Numbers.** Absolute before relative, the population named, no "up to".
- **Review cadence.** 12 months for evergreen pieces; 6 months for anything naming a drug class, target or device.
- **Corrections policy** [22 §4.4]:
  - S1 (could cause harm): corrected within 24 hours, with a dated note and an e-mail to subscribers.
  - S2 (wrong fact): within 72 hours.
  - S3 (typo or broken link): within 7 days.

  The corrections log is public.
- **Listener test** on every flagship piece: 3–5 lay listeners, including one over 60 and one with schooling to Class 8 or less. Pass = 4 of 5 give the main message and the action.
- **Accessibility.** Checked captions, website transcripts, alt text, audio-only versions, large type [22 §4.7].
- **Boundary wording.** The honest version in file 22 §4.6, in Telugu and English, not a ritual disclaimer.

### 6.5 Governance and stop rules

**Decision rights** [22 §6.2]:

- He decides every clinical claim and opinion.
- The friend decides wording and format within the standard.
- The lawyer must read anything naming a company, product, practitioner, hospital or system of medicine.
- Either may invoke a stop; only he lifts it.

**The 13 stop rules** [22 §6.3] pause publishing on:

1. An S1 error.
2. A council notice.
3. A legal notice or police contact.
4. A written employer objection.
5. A confidentiality incident.
6. An account compromise or impersonation.
7. A data breach.
8. An energy rating of 2 or below for two weeks, or four weeks over the hours cap.
9. No second reader available (Tier A only).
10. An incomplete claims ledger (that piece only).
11. An unreviewed rule change.
12. A public tragedy or the death of a public figure (24–72 hours).
13. The friend unavailable for more than two weeks with no backup. He then drops to the **minimum cadence**: one Telugu card a fortnight, which he can hold alone in about 60 minutes a week.

**Reviews and reporting.**

- **Quarterly:** the one-page review [22 §5.4], plus a check of the NMC and TGMC notices and the court cases.
- **Each January:** one public note covering impact, corrections, complaints, money and interests, and AI use. This merges file 22's annual note with file 28's annual statement. The lawyer reads it first.
