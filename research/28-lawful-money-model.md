# A sustainable and lawful money model: who pays for this work, and what income the teaching may lawfully produce (R4-5)

**Executive summary**

1. **The rules bar being paid to promote; they do not bar being paid to teach.** The only sentence in the NMC Guidelines of 6 Oct 2026 that uses the word is cl. 5.1: education is allowed if it is "not monetized through promotional marketing". The term is undefined. No NMC, TGMC or court statement on AdSense, memberships, paid courses, books or ad revenue routed to charity was found (12 Nov 2026).
2. **Green:** society and hospital teaching honoraria, book royalties, courses for doctors, employer-paid education contracts (with conditions), tele-consultation and second-opinion fees as clinical income, grants and donations that go to an institution or an existing NGO. **Amber:** AdSense, memberships, paid newsletters, fee-paying public courses, honoraria paid directly by drug or device companies, health-tech advisory equity. **Red:** sponsorships, affiliate links, endorsements, industry money for public content, industry-paid delegate travel, anything tied to patient flow.
3. **Tax is the neglected half.** Clinical fees are GST-exempt, but speaking, consulting, course and ad income are not, and the Rs 20 lakh registration threshold counts exempt clinical receipts. A consultant cardiologist is therefore probably liable to register for GST as soon as he earns any taxable side income. The Income-tax Act, 2025 has applied since 1 April 2026; the old s.194R freebie TDS is now s.393(1) (10% above Rs 20,000).
4. **Do not form a charity in year 1.** CSR Rule 4(1)(d) still requires 12A + 80G + a three-year track record (the July 2025 amendment changed only Form CSR-1). A programme for a company's own employees is not CSR at all. Run community work under an existing NGO or Rotary umbrella; a trust later costs about Rs 5,000–20,000 and 2–4 months to set up.
5. **Three year-1 budgets:** lean about Rs 3 lakh, standard about Rs 15.5 lakh, ambitious about Rs 33.5 lakh. Content cannot lawfully pay for itself. By year 3, hosts, an NGO partner and teaching fees can plausibly cover programme costs, but not his time. The clean option is to treat this as an **unfunded professional duty with a fixed annual cap**, monetisation off.

### 1. What the rules say about money

#### 1.1 NMC Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication, 6 Oct 2026

**(b) Interpretation**

- The Guidelines contain **no clause on platform ad revenue, memberships, subscriptions, honoraria, royalties, course fees or grants**. Money is addressed in four ways only: (i) education must not be "monetized through promotional marketing"; (ii) no endorsement of products, paid or unpaid; (iii) no consideration linked to patients; (iv) commercial relationships in public-health communication must be disclosed. (High, full-text check; file 11 reached the same result.)
- "Monetized through promotional marketing" most naturally means earning by promoting something: sponsorship, brand integration, affiliate commission, paid placement. A fee for a lecture or a royalty on a book is payment for the teaching itself. (Medium.)
- The weak point of any income tied to public content is **Explanation I**, not 5.1. Content loses the automatic exemption if there is "any intent for commercial benefit". It then has to pass the main test in 3.2: does it promote his services or reputation? Educational content can still pass, but the comfort is gone. (Medium.)
- 8.1(xi) is a positive duty. Every paid relationship that touches a piece of public teaching (an honorarium, an employer contract, an advisory role) must be stated on that piece. (High on the text; the phrase "in accordance with applicable law" is vague.)

**(c) Unsettled**

1. Whether programmatic ad revenue is "monetized through promotional marketing".
2. Whether "promotion of ... any products" in 8.1(ii) reaches a doctor's own book or course. A literal reading does; a sensible one confines it to third-party products.
3. Whether donating the income changes anything. No authority speaks to it.

#### 1.2 Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002 (binding)

**(a) Text** (NMC PDF, "amended upto 8th October 2016"; High)

- **1.8:** "The personal financial interests of a physician should not conflict with the medical interests of patients. A physician should announce his fees before rendering service".
- **6.1.1:** "Soliciting of patients directly or indirectly ... is unethical." A physician shall not give "whether for compensation or otherwise, any approval, recommendation, endorsement, certificate, report or statement with respect of any drug, medicine, nostrum remedy, surgical, or therapeutic article, apparatus or appliance or any commercial product or article ... for use in connection with his name, signature, or photograph in any form or manner of advertising".
- **6.2 (patents and copyrights):** "A physician may patent surgical instruments, appliances and medicine or Copyright applications, methods and procedures. However, it shall be unethical if the benefits of such patents or copyrights are not made available in situations where the interest of large population is involved."
- **6.3 (open shop):** "A physician should not run an open shop for sale of medicine for dispensing prescriptions prescribed by doctors other than himself or for sale of medical or surgical appliances. It is not unethical for a physician to prescribe or supply drugs, remedies or appliances as long as there is no exploitation of the patient."
- **6.4.1:** no "gift, gratuity, commission or bonus in consideration of or return for the referring, recommending or procuring of any patient"; no "division, transference, assignment, subordination, rebating, splitting or refunding of any fee for medical, surgical or other treatment." **6.4.2** extends this to specimens and diagnostics.
- **6.8.1 (industry):**
  - (a) no gifts;
  - (b) no travel "for attending conferences, seminars, workshops, CME Programme, etc. **as a delegate**";
  - (c) no hospitality "under any pretext";
  - (d) "shall not receive any **cash or monetary grants** ... for individual purpose in individual capacity under any pretext. Funding for medical research, study etc. can only be received through approved institutions ... It shall always be fully disclosed";
  - (f) professional autonomy;
  - (g) "may work for pharmaceutical and allied healthcare industries in advisory capacities, as consultants, as researchers, as treating doctors or in any other professional capacity", provided integrity is kept and "such affiliations / employments are fully transparent and disclosed";
  - (h) "shall not endorse any drug or product of the industry publically."
- **6.8 penalty table (2016):** for gifts, travel, hospitality and cash grants alike: over Rs 1,000 up to Rs 5,000, censure; over Rs 5,000 up to Rs 10,000, removal from the register for 3 months; over Rs 10,000 to Rs 50,000, 6 months; over Rs 50,000 to Rs 1,00,000, 1 year; over Rs 1,00,000, more than 1 year. For (e) research, (f) autonomy, (g) affiliation and (h) endorsement: "First time censure, and thereafter removal of name".
- **7.11:** may "write to the lay press under his own name on matters of public health, hygienic living or to deliver public lectures, give talks on the radio/TV/internet chat for the same purpose and send announcement of the same to lay press."
- **7.12:** "An institution run by a physician for a particular purpose such as a maternity home, nursing home, private hospital, rehabilitation centre **or any type of training institution** etc. may be advertised in the lay press, but such advertisements should not contain anything more than the name of the institution, type of patients admitted, **type of training and other facilities offered and the fees**."
- **7.19:** "A Physician shall not use touts or agents for procuring patients."
- **8.1:** the list of misconduct is not exhaustive; "Every care should be taken that the code is not violated in letter or spirit."

**(b) Interpretation**

- **Reg. 6.2 expressly allows copyright.** That is the textual basis for book royalties and for owning course material. Its second sentence points the other way for core prevention content: where "the interest of large population is involved", the benefit must be made available. Reading: keep the public prevention curriculum free and openly licensed; charge, if at all, for depth, format or professional training. (Medium.)
- **Reg. 6.3 bars a shop.** No sale of BP monitors, wearables, supplements or merchandise to the public. (High.)
- **Reg. 6.4 and 7.19** are about patients and treatment fees. A revenue share with a publisher or a teaching platform is not fee-splitting. A revenue share with a tele-consult aggregator on consultation fees is closer to the line (see 2(k)). (Medium.)
- **Reg. 7.12 is the only text that describes how a doctor-run training activity may be announced:** name, type of training, facilities, fees. It is a usable template for a Heart School or CPR course notice. (Medium; the clause was written for institutions, not courses.)
- **6.8.1(d) versus (g).** A speaker fee or advisory fee under a written contract is payment for a professional service (g), not a "grant" (d). A lump sum from a company with no deliverable is a grant, whatever it is called. The rupee slabs make this a registration-level risk from Rs 5,001. (Medium; no decided case found.)

#### 1.3 UCPMP 2024 and the device code (bind companies, not doctors)

**(a) Text** (Department of Pharmaceuticals PDFs; High)

- **UCPMP 6.2:** CME abroad prohibited; events may be run by colleges, hospitals, professional associations, research institutions, or companies alone or with those bodies; organisers must "spell out the procedure followed in the selection of participants and speakers, display a statement of their funding sources and expenditures on their website"; speaker-to-participant ratio within 1:10; participants and speakers "must comply with the relevant provisions of the Income Tax Act".
- **UCPMP 7(ii):** "Engagement of healthcare professionals in consultant-advisory capacity shall be for bona-fide research services, under a consultancy agreement involving a consultancy fee or an honorarium-based payment, subject to the relevant provisions of the Income-Tax Act, 1961."
- **UCPMP 8.2 / 8.3:** no travel or hospitality "unless the person is a speaker for a CME or a CPD Program". **8.4:** no "cash or monetary grant to any healthcare professional ... under any pretext." Closing line: where the code is silent, the 2002 Regulations prevail.
- **Device code (UCMPMD, as amended 30 Apr 2026):** same structure; 8.2 and 8.3 also allow travel and hospitality for "a participant in a training program"; 6.2 permits overseas training where equipment or experts are not available in India (file 16).

**(b) Interpretation**

- **Neither code expressly authorises a speaker honorarium.** They authorise travel and hospitality for speakers (8.2, 8.3) and a fee or honorarium for "bona-fide research services" under a consultancy agreement (7(ii)). A lecture fee paid directly by a company therefore sits between 7(ii) and 8.4. The fallback is Reg. 6.8.1(g), which allows work "in any other professional capacity" if disclosed. This is defensible but grey. (Medium; this refines file 16, which recorded only that no rupee cap exists.)
- The cleaner route is for the **organising society or hospital** to pay the honorarium, with the company's support disclosed by the organiser as 6.2 requires.
- Neither code sets a cap or a "fair market value" formula. (High.)

#### 1.4 Income tax

- **The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026.** Section numbers in older commentary are out of date. (High; multiple 2026 sources.)
- **Freebies, company side.** In *Apex Laboratories v. DCIT* (Supreme Court, 22 Feb 2022) the Court refused a deduction for freebies to doctors because acceptance is barred by the 2002 Regulations: "one arm of the law cannot be utilized to defeat the other arm of law" (LKS; Mondaq; High). The Finance Act 2022 then wrote the rule into s.37(1) (Explanation 3): no deduction for a benefit or perquisite whose acceptance violates a law, rule, regulation or guideline governing the recipient **[BK for the enacted wording; Medium]**. Result: companies now want a written agreement, a deliverable and a defensible rate before they pay a doctor anything.
- **Freebies, doctor side.** The old s.194R is now **s.393(1), Table entry 8(iv)**: 10% tax deducted on benefits or perquisites above Rs 20,000 a year per recipient, in cash or kind (TDSMAN, Aug 2026; TaxGuru; High). Company-paid speaker travel and hotel can fall here. How CBDT Circulars 12/2022 and 18/2022 (hospital-attached consultants, reimbursed expenses) carry over: **could not verify**.
- **Fees.** Honoraria, advisory fees and royalties are professional income. Tax is deducted at 10% on professional fees above the annual threshold (Rs 50,000 since April 2025 **[BK, Medium]**), now also under s.393(1).
- **His own spending.** Expenses laid out wholly and exclusively for the profession are deductible if he keeps regular books (old s.37(1); the new general-deduction section is believed to be s.34: confirm). Not separately deductible if he is on the presumptive scheme for professionals (old s.44ADA), which has a receipts ceiling he very likely exceeds **[BK, Medium]**.
- **A trap in the ledger.** If content costs are booked as "advertising" or "business promotion", his own accounts describe the content as promotion. That contradicts the position he must hold before the medical council. Book them as teaching, public education and continuing professional development. And if any content were ever held to be prohibited advertising, the expense would also fail the "prohibited by law" test. (Judgment; put to both the CA and the lawyer.)
- **Donations.** Giving income away does not make it non-taxable. The 80G-type deduction (now s.354) is generally unavailable under the default new tax regime **[BK, Medium]**.
- **Authors.** The 1961 Act gave resident authors a deduction on book royalties up to Rs 3 lakh (s.80QQB), old regime only **[BK, Medium; new section number not checked]**.

#### 1.5 GST

- **Healthcare is exempt.** Notification 12/2017-CT(Rate), entry 74: "services by way of health care services by a clinical establishment, an authorised medical practitioner or para-medics". Health care services means diagnosis, treatment or care for illness, injury, deformity, abnormality or pregnancy (Patron Accounting, updated 11 Nov 2026; ClearTax; High). Consultation fees "whether from an own clinic or as a visiting consultant" are exempt (same source).
- **Teaching, consulting, wellness and ad income are not healthcare.** "Standalone wellness packages, including corporate wellness sold separately from treatment" are taxable at 18% (Patron Accounting; High). Lecture honoraria, advisory fees, course fees and sponsorship-type receipts are ordinary taxable services at 18% **[Medium; a Karnataka advance ruling of 2022 on guest-lecture income is reported to the same effect: BK, not re-verified]**.
- **Threshold.** Registration is required when aggregate turnover exceeds Rs 20 lakh and the person makes any taxable supply. "Aggregate turnover includes exempt as well as taxable supplies"; "a provider making only exempt supplies does not need GST registration, whatever its fee income" (Patron Accounting; ClearTax: "annual turnover will include all taxable, tax-free, and exempted supplies"; High).
- **Consequence for him.** His exempt clinical receipts almost certainly exceed Rs 20 lakh. On that reading, **the first rupee of taxable side income (a paid corporate talk, an advisory fee, AdSense) makes him liable to register**, charge 18% on taxable invoices and file returns. Many doctors do not know this. (Medium-High on the reading; it is the first question for the CA.)
- **AdSense.** Treated as an export of services to Google: zero-rated, but it counts toward the threshold; "the Rs 20 lakh limit applies even when the services are exported" (ClearTax; Medium).
- **Royalties.** An author's copyright licence to a publisher is taxed under reverse charge (the publisher pays) **[BK, Medium]**. Printed books carry no GST **[BK]**.
- **Charities.** Entry 1 of the same notification exempts "charitable activities" of a registered charity, and the definition includes "public awareness of preventive health" **[BK, Medium-High]**. A registered NGO running prevention education is therefore outside GST for that activity; an individual doctor selling the same course is not. Education fees are exempt only for an "educational institution" as defined (schools, recognised qualifications, approved vocational courses); a Heart School is not one **[BK, Medium]**.
- **Input tax.** No credit against exempt clinical supplies. GST on editors, software and agencies is a real cost for him.

#### 1.6 Professional tax

Telangana levies profession tax on self-employed professionals, capped by the Constitution at Rs 2,500 a year; medical practitioners with more than five years' standing pay the top slab **[BK, Medium; the state page could not be opened]**. It is trivial in amount; it matters only as a registration to have in order for the own practice (file 17 lists it as "not researched").

#### Notes and precedent on each stream

**(a) Ad revenue: the facts about the platform.**
- Full Partner Programme (ad revenue share): 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days (YouTube Help; High).
- Lower tier, available in India: 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and 3,000 watch hours or 3 million Shorts views. It unlocks memberships, Super Chat, Super Thanks and Shopping, not ad revenue (YouTube Help; High).
- **Switching monetisation off does not keep ads away.** YouTube's Terms: "You grant to YouTube the right to monetize your Content on the Service (and such monetization may include displaying ads on or within Content ...). This Agreement does not entitle you to any payments." (YouTube Terms; High.) Ads can therefore appear beside his videos whether or not he joins. Outside the programme he earns nothing and, as far as could be established, controls nothing about which ads run.
- That creates a real question for the lawyer: is it safer to stay out (no income, no control) or to join later, block sensitive ad categories and give the money away? Whether category blocking is available to an Indian health channel and how complete it is: **could not verify**.
- YouTube's health-source labels: individual licensed professionals can apply only in ten listed countries; India is not on that list, though organisations elsewhere may ask to be considered (YouTube Help; Medium, the page is internally inconsistent).
- **Regulator position on AdSense, or on AdSense routed to charity: none found.** One dedicated search and the coverage of the 6 Oct 2026 Guidelines returned nothing on ad revenue, courses or books. No disciplinary case about a doctor's platform revenue was found. Absence of evidence, five weeks after the Guidelines: Medium.
- At year-1 scale the question is academic: the channel is empty and far below either threshold.

**(b) Memberships and paid newsletters.** Whether Substack paid subscriptions can be received in India today: **could not verify** (help page refused retrieval; file 05 had the same gap). The recommendation does not depend on it.

**(c) Books: how Indian doctor-authors do it.**
- Verified examples of practising doctors published by mainstream trade houses: Dr V. Mohan, *Making Excellence a Habit* (India Viking / Penguin Random House India, Feb 2021, Rs 699); Dr Tanaya Narendra, *Dr. Cuterus* (India Penguin, Dec 2022, Rs 299) (publisher pages; High). File 03 lists the international pattern (Topol, Gawande, Mukherjee, Gulati).
- In each case the publisher, not the doctor, markets the book: catalogue page, launch, festival sessions, reviews, excerpts. That division of labour is the compliance model: the publisher promotes a book; the doctor speaks about the subject.
- No NMC or council action against a doctor for publicising his own book was found (not searched exhaustively; Low-Medium).
- Money: trade royalties are a percentage of cover price; the amounts are small at the cover prices above. A book is a credibility and reach asset, not a funding source. (Judgment; royalty percentages not researched.)
- Telugu health books and newspaper columns by Hyderabad doctors exist as a tradition, but named examples were not verified this round.

**(d) Fee-paying public courses.** No Indian precedent was found either way for a practising cardiologist charging the public for a prevention course. File 15 records a nearby cost-recovery norm: the Lodge Keys / EMRI CPR sessions ask Rs 1,000 "from those who can pay".

**(e) Employer programmes: is it "soliciting"?**
- Text: 6.1.1 and 8.1(ii) prohibit soliciting **patients** and promoting **professional (medical) services**. A proposal to an HR head to deliver a 12-lesson education programme solicits neither.
- It becomes solicitation the moment the package includes his consultations, a "priority appointment", a discount, a screening test that leads to his cath lab, or a vendor who advertises "sessions by Dr X, senior interventional cardiologist" to win accounts (8.1(ii), (x), (xi)).
- Market practice: vendors such as Indus Health Plus arrange "special talk sessions"; Apollo 24|7 prices corporate programmes per employee per month or as fixed-fee bundles (vendor pages; Medium). **No published per-session fee for a doctor's talk was found**; fees are negotiated.
- File 19's design already fits: baseline tests "by an accredited laboratory paid by the employer", written referral to participants' own doctors.

**(f) CME honoraria.** No published rate card for cardiology faculty honoraria in India was found; societies commonly pay nothing or a token. Treat honoraria as incidental, not as a funding line.

#### 3.2 Tax registration timeline (Income-tax Act, 2025)

- Old 12A/12AB registration is now registration as a **"registered non-profit organisation" under s.332**; the donor deduction (old 80G) is **s.354**, renewed separately every 5 years (Patron Accounting, March–April 2026; Medium-High).
- New entity: **Form 10A** provisional registration, order due within 1 month, valid **3 years**. Convert with **Form 10AB** at least 6 months before expiry (or within 6 months of starting activities, per other vendor sources); regular registration lasts 5 years. One source says the provisional form has been renumbered; check the portal. (Medium.)
- Then PAN, bank account, NGO Darpan (1–2 weeks). **Entity to provisional tax registration: about 2–4 months.**
- Yearly: books, CA audit, ITR-7 by 31 October, online statement of donations; at least 85% of income applied to the objects; cash donations above Rs 2,000 are not recognised for the donor's deduction. (Medium-High.)
- Payments or benefits to the founder, relatives or their concerns are "violations" under ss.351–353 (old s.13) and can cost the exemption **[BK for the content; section mapping from the same source]**.

#### 3.3 CSR eligibility: the current text

- **Rule 4(1) of the Companies (CSR Policy) Rules, 2014** (as substituted on 22 Jan 2021 and 20 Sep 2022). A company may do CSR itself or through:
  - (a) a Section 8 company, registered public trust or registered society **established by the company**, with 12A and 80G;
  - (b), (c) entities established by government or by statute;
  - (d) a Section 8 company, registered public trust or registered society with 12A and 80G "**and having an established track record of at least three years in undertaking similar activities**". (ca2013.com rule text; High.)
- **The Companies (CSR Policy) Amendment Rules, 2025** (G.S.R. 452(E), 7 July 2025, in force 14 July 2025) **only substituted e-form CSR-1**; Rule 4(1) was not changed. The new form asks about 12A/80G status and the three-year record and needs certification by a practising CA, CS or cost accountant. (TaxGuru text of the notification; High.)
- Rule 4(2): every implementing entity must register by filing CSR-1. Cost about Rs 3,500–7,500 (digital signature plus certification); 3–7 working days. A lapsed 80G breaks eligibility. (Patron Accounting; file 09; Medium.)
- **So the Round 1 finding stands, now verified against the rule text:** an independent new entity reaches its first CSR rupee about **3.5–4 years** after registration.
- **Often missed:** the CSR Rules exclude "any activity benefitting employees of the company" from CSR **[BK, High; Rule 2(1)(d)]**. An employer paying for a Heart School cohort for its own staff is buying an HR or wellness service, not doing CSR. CSR money fits only the community-facing work (CPR in apartment blocks, schools, public places), under Schedule VII item (i), "promoting health care including preventive health care" **[BK, High]**.
- A company may also run a CSR project **itself** and engage trainers as suppliers. That is a lawful route for a company that wants CPR training in its neighbourhood before any NGO of his exists.

#### 3.4 Foreign money (FCRA)

- Any foreign contribution needs FCRA registration or prior permission. Registration normally requires three years of existence and Rs 15 lakh spent on the objects over three years; a new body can seek prior permission for a specific donor and project. Money must land in the designated FCRA account at SBI New Delhi Main Branch; passing foreign funds on to another organisation is prohibited; administrative spending is capped at 20%. **[BK from the 2020 amendments and Rules; the official FAQ could not be opened; Medium.]**
- Practical reading: **take no foreign money for the first five years**, including from the diaspora, foreign alumni groups or overseas societies. Indian-resident donors only.

#### 3.5 Governance pitfalls

1. **Benefit to the founder.** Rent, fees, salary, equipment use or patient flow to the founder or his clinic puts the tax exemption at risk and is the textbook complaint to a medical council.
2. **Fee income inside a charity.** Charging employers through the charity may be treated as commercial activity. The old Act capped such receipts at 20% for "general public utility" charities; one 2026 source says the new Act relaxes this. **Unsettled; CA question.**
3. **Lapsed renewals** (80G/s.354 every 5 years) silently end CSR eligibility.
4. **Founder-dominated boards.** Donors and CSR due-diligence agencies (GuideStar India, Credibility Alliance) look for independent trustees and audited accounts.
5. **Name and branding.** A trust carrying his name or his clinic's name turns every camp into a "communication ... intended to promote ... reputation" (3.2). Use a programme name ("First 60 Minutes", "Hands that Save").
6. **Lifetime liability.** Trustees, audits and filings continue for as long as the entity exists (file 09).

#### 3.6 Conflict-of-interest safeguards: the "screening camp funnels patients to the founder" problem

The risk is specific. An interventional cardiologist's charity that finds people with chest pain, abnormal ECGs or high risk scores a few kilometres from his clinic is, to a complainant, a lead-generation scheme (Guidelines 8.1(i), (vii), (x); Reg. 6.1.1, 6.4, 7.19). Safeguards, in order of weight:

1. **Teach and measure; do not screen for procedures.** Programmes do CPR training, BP and glucose measurement and risk education. No ECG, echo, treadmill or "free angiography" camps. (8.1(i): no "unnecessary demand for medical procedures"; 8.1(x): no "free procedures".)
2. **A written no-referral rule.** Anyone with an abnormal finding receives a written note addressed "to your own doctor", plus a neutral list of nearby public and private facilities that does not include, or does not single out, the founder. He does not hand out cards, numbers or appointment slips.
3. **No money in either direction between the programme and his practice.** No rent, fees, salary, equipment hire or laboratory arrangement; the founder and his relatives are unpaid.
4. **Independent control.** The founder is one voice among at least three trustees or committee members; the majority are unrelated to him and not his employees; the chair is not him.
5. **Data wall.** Participant data is held by the host or NGO under its own consent notice (DPDP), never copied to the clinic's records or used to contact people.
6. **No branding of the practice.** Materials carry his name, qualifications and registration number only (file 11b); no clinic or hospital name, logo, address or QR code.
7. **Clean donors.** No pharma, device, diagnostics, insurer, hospital-chain or food-supplement money (stream n).
8. **Open books and an open conflict statement.** Annual income-and-expenditure statement published; a standing line: "Dr Pathuri practises cardiology in Hyderabad. The programme does not refer participants to him or to any facility in which he has a financial interest."
9. **Same fee for everyone.** If a participant later chooses him independently, the consultation is on ordinary terms: no programme discount, no priority.

#### 3.7 The alternative: an existing umbrella

- India has no legal category called "fiscal host". The working equivalent is a **programme run inside an existing registered NGO under a short MoU**: the NGO receives the money, issues receipts, owns the kit, carries the audit; he is the volunteer clinical lead and curriculum author.
- Candidates already identified (file 09, file 15): a Rotary club and its district trust; the CSI Telangana chapter; the Gandhi Medical College Alumni Association with Janahita Seva Trust; the Society for Cyberabad Security Council's healthcare forum; EMRI Green Health Services' Resuscitation Academy. Billion Hearts Beating has 12A, 80G and FCRA but is Apollo's foundation: it fails the independence test in Addendum 2.
- What to check in a host: valid s.332/12AB and s.354/80G registration, CSR-1 number, last three audited accounts, who signs cheques, and a clause that the curriculum and data-collection design remain his to take elsewhere.
- **Recommendation (confirms file 09):** partner for years 1–2; decide on an own entity only when there is a two-to-three-year record worth institutionalising. The record is needed for CSR anyway, and work done under a host counts as evidence of "similar activities" only if it is documented in the host's reports with his programme named. Whether that satisfies Rule 4(1)(d) for a later new entity is doubtful; the track record belongs to the entity. (Medium.)

#### 5.4 The other honest option: an unfunded professional duty with a cap

- He declares, once, in writing: this work is a professional duty, it takes no outside money, and he will spend no more than a fixed amount a year on it.

- What it costs: slower output, no paid coordinator, and the friend's time becomes the limiting resource.
- The two options are compatible: start with the cap; add Green income only when a specific cost (the coordinator, journal fees) needs covering and the lawyer and CA have answered.

### 6. Deliverables

#### 6.1 The one-page money rules

1. **No payment ever changes what he says.** No payer sees, edits or approves content. Stated in every contract.
2. **Public teaching is free to the public.** No paywall, membership, tip jar or subscription on prevention content.
3. **No industry money in anything the public sees.** No drug, device, diagnostics, insurer, hospital-chain, food or supplement company funds, hosts, equips or co-brands his content or community programmes.
4. **Monetisation stays off.** Platform ad revenue is not enabled until a health lawyer clears it in writing. If ever enabled, sensitive ad categories are blocked, every rupee goes to a charity he does not control, and the receipts are published.
5. **No sponsorships, affiliate links, discount codes, product reviews or endorsements.** Paid or unpaid. No brand names in content.
6. **Nothing is sold.** No devices, supplements or merchandise. Books and professional courses are the only things with a price, and a publisher or platform sells them.
7. **Money never follows patients.** No commission, referral fee, lead fee, platform ranking fee or per-patient payment, to or from anyone.
8. **Community programmes never refer to him.** Written note to the participant's own doctor; no cards, numbers or discounts; no procedure-generating tests at camps.
9. **Every honorarium, fee, grant, equity stake and paid trip is written down and disclosed.** One register; one public disclosure page updated twice a year; a disclosure slide in every talk; a line on every piece of content the relationship touches.
10. **Industry may pay for peer teaching only.** Written agreement, his own slides, no brand recommendation, never as a delegate, never repeated to the public. Prefer the society to pay.
11. **Advisory roles are disclosed and silent.** The company may not use his name or face; he does not post about its product or category.
12. **Grants and donations go to institutions, not to him.** Research money through an approved institution with ethics approval; programme money to the host NGO.
13. **Clinical income and teaching are kept apart.** Tele-consultation and second opinions appear only on the factual clinic page with the fee; never in or under educational content.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Taking the Amber streams as Green.** AdSense, memberships, paid public courses and company honoraria are unsettled; a first complaint would be decided by the Telangana Medical Council with no precedent.
- **Believing "monetisation off" means "no ads".** YouTube can still run ads on his videos. The rule he can keep is that he earns nothing from them.
- **Unregistered taxable income.** A single paid corporate talk without a GST position is a compliance breach that has nothing to do with the NMC and is easy to find from the payer's records.
- **Company money dressed as something else.** "Educational grants", "unrestricted support", paid "advisory boards" with no deliverable, a company buying 500 copies of his book, a device firm funding CPR manikins. Reg. 6.8 penalties start at censure above Rs 1,000 and reach removal above Rs 5,000.
- **The camp funnel.** Any event that screens, then hands out his card. This is the single most likely source of a complaint from a competitor.
- **A charity that pays the founder** in any form, or carries his name.
- **Booking content as marketing** in the practice accounts.
- **Vendors using his name.** Wellness companies, CME platforms and start-ups will put "Dr X, senior interventional cardiologist" in their sales material unless the contract forbids it (8.1(ii), (xi)).
- **Foreign money**, however small, without FCRA clearance.
- **Letting income targets set the agenda.** The moment a cohort, course or talk is chosen for its fee, rule 1 has been broken in spirit.
- **Treating this file as advice.** Sections marked (b) and every Amber rating are readings, not rulings. Tax points marked [BK] were not re-verified against the 2025 Act.
