# 30 — Fact-check and quotation annex: legal and regulatory claims

**Purpose.** The final plan rests on a set of legal claims. This file re-opens each one at its primary source, quotes the operative words with clause numbers, and marks it Confirmed, Corrected or Could not verify. Nothing here is taken from files 01–29.

**How to read it.**
- Every source was fetched on **12 November 2026**.
- Quotations are verbatim. Spelling and grammar slips inside quotation marks are in the originals.
- "Primary" means the regulator's, court's or gazette's own document. Where only a law-firm note or news report could be opened, the entry says so.
- Web search was limited to 12 queries; the rest was direct fetching. Sites that could not be reached are listed in each section.

**The five corrections that matter most**
1. The 2026 NMC document is a set of **Guidelines issued by public notice**, read with the 2002 Regulations. Its penalty ladder is one the State Medical Council "may" contemplate. It is not a gazetted regulation.
2. Several clause citations in circulation are wrong: the review rule is **Explanation V to cl. 3.2**; cl. **8.3 is about hospitals**, not doctors; the government-campaign exemption is an **Explanation under cl. 7.3**; registration-number display in the 2002 Regulations is **1.4.1**, not 1.4.2.
3. "No paid reach" is **not a rule in the text**. It is an inference from cl. 3.2 and 5.1.
4. *MIOT Hospitals* is a single-judge Madras High Court order and one legal journal reports a **Supreme Court appeal with a status quo order**. It is not settled law.
5. The Clinical Establishments Act penalty for running unregistered is **tiered** (up to Rs 50,000, then Rs 2 lakh, then Rs 5 lakh), and Telangana Act 21 of 2025 only **repeals** the 2002 state Act; adoption of the central Act was by 2017 resolutions.

## A. NMC Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication, 6 October 2026

**Source status: primary, confirmed.** The NMC's own website lists the document under "What's New" as "06/10/2026-Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication by Hospitals/Medical Institutions and Registered Medical Practitioners". The official PDF (https://nmc.org.in/whats-new/download/1874) is a 13-page scan. Its page images are byte-identical to those in the Medical Dialogues copy, which carries a text layer. Key pages (4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11) were also read as images to check the text layer.

**Identity of the document**

> No.: R-13014/01/2024-Ethics   Dated: 06/10/2026
> PUBLIC NOTICE
> Subject: Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication by Hospitals/Medical Institutions and Registered Medical Practitioners.

Signed digitally by Dr Raghav Langer, Secretary, National Medical Commission, 06-10-2026. Issuing body on the letterhead: National Medical Commission, Ethics & Medical Registration Board.

### A1. Definition of "advertisement"; scope and applicability

> **3.2** "Advertisement" mean any form of communication, promotion, publicity, announcement, representation or dissemination of information in any medium, which directly or indirectly solicits, promotes or is intended to promote the professional services, reputation, skills, qualifications, achievements, facilities or commercial healthcare services of an RMP or hospital/medical institution.
>
> For the purposes of these Guidelines, digital, social-media, sponsored, targeted, influencer-led, search-engine, platform-based, audio-visual, AI-generated or AI-assisted promotional communications shall be treated as advertising where their content or manner of dissemination has a promotional character.
>
> **Explanation I** - The discourse of information in the normal course of discharge of information to the public at large, without same being promotional and without any intent for commercial benefit shall be exempted from the above definition of "Advertisement".
>
> **Explanation II** - Information regarding availability or installation of equipment, technology, diagnostic facilities or services may be disseminated as factual information, provided that such communication does not contain claims of superiority, guaranteed accuracy, guaranteed outcomes, comparative claims or other misleading or unverifiable representations.

> **3.5** "Medium of advertisement" means and includes but is not limited to print media, Television, Radio, Social media platforms, websites and blogs, messaging platforms (WhatsApp, Telegrams, Facebook etc.), Podcasts, Influencer marketing, Digital marketing campaigns, sponsored posts, artificial intelligence generated promotion, public interviews, news articles intended for promotion, online healthcare platforms, any audio-visual or digital communication by whatsoever name called.

> **3.6** "Social Media" shall mean and include all forms of publications and sharing through internet-based websites, apps, etc. by whatsoever name called, which allows users to create, share and join online communities, participate in social networking, etc.

> **4.1** These Guidelines shall apply to the professional conduct of the RMPs in relation to advertising, publicity, digital communication and promotional activities and to address the gap created due to advent of electronic media. These Guidelines are sought to be more comprehensive, specific and aligned to the needs of the present-day society.
>
> **4.2** In relation to RMPs, these Guidelines shall be read with the IMC (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002, as amended from time to time, and the NMC Act, 2019. In case of any irreconcilable conflict, the applicable statutory provisions and operative regulations shall prevail.
>
> **4.3** For Hospitals/ Medical Institutions, these guidelines shall be read in conjunction with the applicable Clinical Establishment Act or Rules framed thereunder/ any other State Act as applicable and in case of any irreconcilable conflict between the two, the provisions of the applicable Clinical Establishment Act or Rules framed thereunder/ any other State Act as applicable shall override the provisions of these Guidelines.

**Verdict: Confirmed.** "Reputation" is in the definition. AI-generated and AI-assisted communications are covered "where their content or manner of dissemination has a promotional character". Note "public interviews" and "news articles intended for promotion" in cl. 3.5.

### A2. Educational content (cl. 5.1)

> **5. Public Awareness: Public health education**
>
> **5.1** Medical Practitioners may participate in health awareness programmers, public health campaigns, academic discussions, educational content, provided that such activities do not promote personal practice, do not solicit patients and are not monetized through promotional marketing.
>
> **5.2** Hoardings/Billboards in public space that are let out by concerned municipal or other authorities like state transport authority, railways, airports etc., may be used by hospitals/medical institutions and RMPs only for dissemination of factual information in the nature of infrastructure, facilities etc. Unverified or unverifiable claims shall be construed as for furtherance of commercial gains, and are prohibited.

**Verdict: Confirmed verbatim.** The cited phrases are exact. "Personal practice" is not defined.

### A3. Testimonials, success stories, reviews, patient images, before/after

> **3.2, Explanation V** - No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall solicit, purchase, procure, manipulate or cause to be published fake, paid or misleading patient reviews, ratings, testimonials, endorsements or recommendations for the promotion of medical services. An RMP shall not request or share patient testimonials, recommendations, endorsements or reviews for professional promotion on social media.

> **6. Patient Privacy and Consent**
>
> **6.1** Patient information, photographs, videos, medical records, clinical images, treatment details, testimonials or other identifiable information shall not be used for advertising purposes except where such use is permitted by applicable law and the necessary consent and other safeguards have been duly complied with.
>
> **6.2** Patient consent shall not, by itself, make permissible any testimonial, endorsement, promotional patient story, before-and-after depiction, success claim or other advertising practice that is otherwise prohibited under these Guidelines or applicable law.
>
> **6.3** Where consent is legally required, it shall be specific, informed, voluntary, documented and capable of verification, and shall comply with applicable privacy and data-protection law. The form and mode of recording consent shall be determined in accordance with the applicable law and the nature and purpose of the proposed use.
>
> **6.4** Even where disclosure is lawfully permitted, reasonable safeguards shall be adopted by the hospital/medical institution and RMP shall be duty bound to prevent unnecessary disclosure of names or to crop, blur, black out, birth marks/ identification marks, or other information through which the patient may be identified.

> **7.4** RMP/ Clinical establishment shall not share any patient data, such as names, faces, anomalies, etc., which can be identified publicly.

> **8.1(v)** Shall not publish cases for promotional purposes, advertise surgical results, display "before and after" photographs, publicize celebrity patients, promote personal success stories unless published strictly for scientific or educational purposes with anonymized patient consent.

> **7.2(b)** AI shall not be used to create or manipulate a patient's image, testimonial, voice or clinical outcome, or to create a synthetic endorsement or representation purporting to be that of an actual patient, RMP or other person.

See also 8.1(xi) and 8.1(xii) in A4.

**Verdict: Corrected.** All cited clauses exist. Three points of precision:
- The "no requesting or sharing reviews" rule is **Explanation V to cl. 3.2**, not a numbered clause, and its second sentence is limited to "for professional promotion on social media".
- Cl. 6.2 does not say testimonials are banned "even with consent" in those words. It says consent "shall not, by itself, make permissible" a practice "that is otherwise prohibited". The prohibition itself comes from Explanation V, 8.1(ii), 8.1(v) and 8.1(xi).
- Cl. 8.1(v) has an exception: "unless published strictly for scientific or educational purposes with anonymized patient consent". The phrase "anonymized patient consent" is the original wording.

### A4. Prohibited practices (8.1), exceptions (8.2), hospital advertising (8.3), cl. 9.1–9.4

> **8.1 Advertising Restrictions and Permitted Communications for RMPs.**
>
> i. Shall not engage in advertisement/ activities intended to create unnecessary demand for medical procedures, promote unnecessary diagnostics and promote fear-based marketing.
>
> ii. Shall not solicit patients directly or indirectly, promote his/her professional services through self-promotion, engage any third party for marketing medical services, permit the use of their name, image, voice, testimonial or endorsement for promotion of services or any products, participate in promotional campaigns giving an impression of commercialization of medical practice.
>
> iii. Shall not advertise either personal achievements or professional success rates or number of patients treated with illustrative assertions such as "Guaranteed cures", "Best doctors", "No.1 Specialist", "100% success", "painless treatment", "Miracle treatment", "Exclusive cure" or any other unverifiable or exaggerated claims by whatsoever name called.
>
> iv. An RMP shall not give, directly or indirectly, any approval, recommendation, endorsement, certificate, report or statement concerning any drug, medicine, remedy, therapeutic article, apparatus, appliance, medical device, diagnostic product, health product or commercial product for use in advertising or publicity in connection with his/her name, signature, photograph, voice or professional status, whether or not monetary or other consideration is received.
>
> v. Shall not publish cases for promotional purposes, advertise surgical results, display "before and after" photographs, publicize celebrity patients, promote personal success stories unless published strictly for scientific or educational purposes with anonymized patient consent.
>
> vi. Shall not publish any statement that creates unrealistic expectations, misrepresents treatment outcomes, conceals risks, promote unproven therapies, secret remedy or treatment whose composition, scientific basis, safety or efficacy is not adequately established or which is otherwise prohibited under applicable law.
>
> vii. No RMP shall, directly or indirectly, offer, pay, solicit, receive or participate in any commission, rebate, bonus, gift, gratuity, referral fee, lead-generation fee or other consideration in relation to the promotion or advertisement of medical services, where such consideration is connected with the referral, recommendation or procurement of patients, specimens or materials for diagnosis, treatment, surgery or other medical services.
>
> viii. The engagement of advertising agencies, digital marketing agencies, influencers or online platforms shall not be structured in a manner that results in payment or consideration being linked to the procurement or referral of individual patients.
>
> ix. No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall make comparative claims such as "best", "No.1", "leading", "most trusted", "top", "number one", "unmatched" or similar superiority claims unless the claim is objectively verifiable, based on a transparent and independently ascertainable methodology and is otherwise permissible under applicable law.
>
> Provided, Any ranking or award relied upon in advertising shall be capable of independent verification, and material conditions, methodology and relevant date, details of award giving organization or firm shall be disclosed where necessary to avoid misleading the public.
>
> x. No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall use discounts, limited-period offers, contests, coupons, gifts, cashbacks, referral benefits, free procedures or similar inducements in a manner that is likely to encourage unnecessary medical consultation, diagnostic investigation or treatment or otherwise amounts to solicitation of patients.
>
> Any lawful disclosure of charges, packages or fees shall be factual, transparent and not misleading.
>
> xi. No RMP shall use or permit any celebrity, influencer, patient, employee or third party to promote professional services through prohibited testimonials, endorsements or recommendations. Any material commercial relationship in lawful public-health or informational communication shall be transparently disclosed in accordance with applicable law. No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall use a third party as an intermediary to do indirectly what the RMP or institution is prohibited from doing directly.
>
> xii. No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall procure, manipulate or cause any third party to procure or manipulate fake followers, likes, reviews, ratings, testimonials, comments, views or other digital engagement, or manipulate search rankings, visibility or algorithms to create a misleading impression of professional standing.

> **8.2 Exceptions from Prohibitions for RMP –**
>
> i. Only research-based communication, wherein they have published interesting case studies in reputed medical journals shall be allowed.
>
> ii. A medical practitioner is however permitted to make a formal announcement regarding the following :
> a) On starting practice.
> b) On change of type of practice.
> c) On changing address.
> d) On temporary absence from duty.
> e) On resumption of another practice.
> f) On succeeding to another practice.
> g) Public declaration of charge.
>
> iii. Medical Practitioners may deliver lectures or talks on public health and diseases for public awareness through television, radio and electronic media in their own name and designation, without promoting their employer organization.
>
> iv. Medical Practitioners can issue health education and general information pamphlets/ brochures to the patients visiting their clinic/ hospital but these should not contain guarantee of cure or superiority over others or unsubstantiated claims or misleading information
>
> v. Greeting on days of national importance or related to medical profession, etc. of National Day, etc. can be given by Medical Practitioners, however same should not contain advertisement of a professional gains.

> **8.3 Hospital/Medical Institution Advertising**
>
> i. Hospitals/Medical Institutions may disseminate factual, objective and verifiable information concerning their name, location, contact details, departments, facilities, equipment, services, emergency services, accreditation status, charges and other institutional information permitted by applicable law.
>
> ii. Such communication shall not contain patient solicitation, inducement, comparative superiority claims, guarantees of cure or treatment outcome, unverifiable or exaggerated claims, or promotional portrayal of an individual RMP. The form, medium or frequency of communication shall not convert otherwise factual information into solicitation or promotional self-aggrandizement.
>
> iii. Where advertising is permitted under applicable law, the communication shall remain within the scope permitted by such law and these Guidelines.

> **9. Responsibilities of Hospital/Medical Institutions and RMP**
>
> **9.1** Clinical Establishments may advertise only factual information, where it is limited to objective and verifiable particulars concerning the establishment, including the name of the establishment, contact details, departments functioning, diagnostic facilities, emergency services, accreditation status, other facilities offered and applicable fees. A hospital website containing a directory of its doctors, containing the names, recognised qualifications, specialties, registration details and availability of its doctors, that serves a patient-information function; shall be regarded materially distinct from a paid or promotional advertisement or inducement.
>
> **9.2** Use of the photograph/image of an RMP shall not be permitted; where such use constitutes self-advertisement, solicitation or promotional publicity. In particular, use of self-photograph or similar publicity material on the letterhead or signboard of a consulting room or clinical establishment shall be governed by Regulations 6.1.1 and 6.1.2 of the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002, and regarded as unethical conduct.
>
> **9.3** RMP can issue a formal announcement which are factual, non-promotional and limited in scope regarding starting of practice, change of address, change of practice type, temporary absence, resumption of practice, public declaration of consultation fees.
>
> **9.4** All healthcare platforms hosting RMP shall ensure compliance with NMC ethical advertising norms, prohibition of paid ranking of doctors, maintain transparency in listing practices.

**Verdict: Corrected.**
- 8.1 has twelve sub-clauses, all quoted above.
- 8.2 is the only list of exceptions for doctors. It has five heads.
- **8.3 is not a doctor's permission.** It governs hospitals and bars "promotional portrayal of an individual RMP". That matters for anything Apollo publishes about him.
- 9.1 speaks of "Clinical Establishments" and of "a hospital website containing a directory of its doctors". It does not mention a doctor's personal website. A single-doctor clinic is a clinical establishment under the central Act (see D4), so 9.1 can attach to his own clinic's factual particulars; that step is an inference, not text.
- 8.2(ii) drops the words "in press" that appear in Regulation 6.1.1 of 2002. Neither 8.2(ii) nor 9.3 names a permitted medium.
- 9.3 lists six occasions and omits "succeeding to another practice", which 8.2(ii)(f) includes.
- 8.2(i) is narrowly and awkwardly worded: "Only research-based communication, wherein they have published interesting case studies in reputed medical journals shall be allowed."

### A5. Third parties, agencies, influencers

> **4.4** The fact that an advertisement or promotional communication is published through a digital platform, third party, advertising agency, influencer or other intermediary shall not, by itself, absolve an RMP or medical institution from responsibility for content which it has authorized, commissioned, sponsored, adopted or knowingly permitted. The disciplinary or statutory consequences shall, however, be determined by the competent authority under the applicable law.

Also 8.1(ii) ("engage any third party for marketing medical services"), 8.1(viii), 8.1(xi), 8.1(xii) and 9.4, quoted in A4, and:

> **11.5** … Where an RMP has personally authorized, participated in, endorsed or otherwise been responsible for the prohibited communication, the professional conduct of such RMP may separately be examined by the concerned State Medical Council, Ethics and Medical Registration Board or National Medical Commission, as applicable.

**Verdict: Confirmed.** The responsibility test in 4.4 is "authorized, commissioned, sponsored, adopted or knowingly permitted". The Guidelines do not define "third party" or "marketing medical services", and say nothing about unpaid helpers, editors or drafting tools. Whether a friend who edits educational content falls inside 8.1(ii) is a legal judgment the text does not answer.

### A6. AI (cl. 7.2) and government campaigns

> **7.2** AI Generated promotional campaign for furtherance of commercial interest is prohibited. Any AI generated promotional contentconforming to the provisions of these guidelines, the Regulations, and applicable Clinical Establishment Act and rules framed thereunder/ any other State Act as applicable, shall compulsorily carry the source mark, explicitly stating that the origin of the content is AI.
>
> a) AI-generated or AI-assisted content shall not be used by an RMP or hospital/medical institution to create or disseminate misleading, deceptive or unverifiable representations concerning diagnosis, treatment, clinical outcomes, professional qualifications, patient experiences or healthcare services.
>
> b) AI shall not be used to create or manipulate a patient's image, testimonial, voice or clinical outcome, or to create a synthetic endorsement or representation purporting to be that of an actual patient, RMP or other person.
>
> c) Where AI-generated or materially AI-altered content is used in a manner in which its artificial nature may reasonably be material to the audience's understanding of the communication, appropriate disclosure shall be made.
>
> d) Any use of patient information as input for an AI system shall comply with applicable privacy, confidentiality, data-protection and professional-ethics requirements.

> **3.3** "Artificial Intelligence" or "AI" means a self-learning automated process, program, machine or computer built for doing task which usually require human brain.

> **7.3** The prohibited practices relating to advertising, publicity, solicitation, endorsements, testimonials, patient images, misleading claims and digital manipulation shall be governed by Chapter III of these Guidelines. RMPs and clinical establishments shall also comply with the specific requirements relating to patient privacy, digital communication and AI contained in pre-paras of this Chapter.
>
> **Explanation** - All campaigns on programmes/schemes of Government of India and the state Governments, which arein larger public interest, shall be exempted from these guidelines.

**Verdict: 7.2 Confirmed. Government-campaign citation Corrected:** the exemption is the unnumbered Explanation that follows 7.3. Cl. 7.2(b) bars a synthetic representation "purporting to be that of an actual … RMP", which covers an AI avatar or voice clone of the doctor himself when used in a communication that counts as advertising.

### A7. Explanation III — registration number

> **Explanation III** - RMP while publishing any Electronic media posts should transparently disclose their name, qualifications, registration status and SMR/NMR Registration number.
>
> **Explanation IV** - Clinical Establishments while publishing any Electronic media posts should transparently disclose the names of the RMP with qualifications, registration status and SMR/NMR Registration number.

**Verdict: Confirmed, with a wording caveat.** The verb is "should". Four items are required: name, qualifications, registration status, and SMR/NMR number. The Explanations sit under the definition of "Advertisement" (cl. 3.2); the text does not say whether they apply to posts that are not advertisements.

### A8. Commencement, legal basis, enforcement, penalties, appeal

**Legal basis as stated in the notice**

> In view of the Writ Petition (Civil) No.1160/2023 filed in the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India and provisions of Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002; the subject guidelines have been framed with the following objectives: …
>
> 2. Accordingly, the National Medical Commission (NMC) hereby informs all Registered Medical Practitioners and other concerned stakeholders, to ensure compliance with the provisions of the annexed Guidelines.
>
> The Guidelines shall come into force with immediate effect.

> **1.2** They shall come into force on the date of their publication by the National Medical Commission.

> **2.1(f)** provide an operational framework consistent with the National Medical Commission Act, 2019, the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002, as applicable, and other applicable laws, rules and regulations, without altering, overriding or derogating from any existing statutory or regulatory requirement.
>
> **2.2** Nothing contained in these Guidelines shall be construed as permitting any act which is prohibited under the applicable law or the aforesaid Regulations.

**Penalties**

> **10.1** In case of violation of the above guidelines, actions will be taken by the concerned SMC against the erring RMP in accordance with the provisions ofthe NMC Act and Regulations made thereunder. The following graded action **may be contemplated** by the SMC concerned;
>
> i. **First Violation**: Warning and mandatory ethics training.
> ii. **Second Violation:** Censure and monetary penalty.
> iii. **Third Violation:** Suspension of registration (3-6 months).
> iv. **Serious Violation** (Misleading cure claims, inducement for patients, digital mass solicitation, etc.) Suspension of 6-12 months.
> v. **Repeated Violations:** Removal from medical register for 1-3 years.
>
> Any violation of these Guidelines by an RMP may, where applicable, constitute professional or ethical misconduct and shall be dealt with by the competent authority in accordance with the NMC Act, 2019, the applicable Ethics Regulations and the principles of natural justice.
>
> The competent authority may, where legally permissible, impose such disciplinary or corrective measures, including censure, direction for corrective compliance/ethics sensitization, restriction or suspension of registration, removal from the register for a specified period or otherwise, and monetary penalty, as may be authorized under the NMC Act and applicable Regulations.
>
> Explanation: Hospital/medical institutionsengaging in unethical advertising shall be dealt with in accordance with applicable Clinical Establishment Act and Rules framed thereunder/ any other State Act as applicable.
>
> **10.2** The penalties shall be imposed only after issuing a show-cause notice to the defaulting RMP and providing an opportunity to explain or clarify the alleged violation, in accordance with the applicable laws.
>
> **10.3** Where an RMP is found to be in violation of these Guidelines, the SMC shall issue a reasoned and speaking order setting out: i. the specific violation identified; ii. the contentions of the defaulting RMP/ and reasons for acceptance / rejection of the same; iii. the reasons and basis for imposing the penalties.

(Bold in 10.1 is in the original.)

**Appeal**

> **11.1** Any RMP aggrieved by the findings or decision of the SMC may prefer an appeal before the Appellate Authority i.e, EMRB,NMC, within 60 days under Section 30 (3) of NMC Act, 2019.
>
> **11.4** The decision on the first appeal may be contested by the RMP through second appeal, in accordance with Section 30 (4), NMC Act, 2019, within sixty days of communication of such decision.

> **12. Power to Issue Clarifications** The National Medical Commission may issue such clarifications, advisories, standard operating procedures or procedural instructions, consistent with these Guidelines and the National Medical Commission Act, 2019, as may be necessary for their effective and uniform implementation.

**Verdict: Confirmed.** In force from 6 October 2026. The enforcing body for him is the State Medical Council. Two limits are in the text itself: the ladder "may be contemplated", and monetary penalty is available only "as may be authorized under the NMC Act and applicable Regulations".

## B. Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002

**Source: primary.** NMC web page (headed "AMENDED UPTO 8th OCTOBER 2016") and NMC-hosted PDF with amendments incorporated.

### B1. Regulation 6.1.1 and 6.1.2 — advertising and formal announcements

> **6.1.1** Soliciting of patients directly or indirectly, by a physician, by a group of physicians or by institutions or organisations is unethical. A physician shall not make use of him / her (or his / her name) as subject of any form or manner of advertising or publicity through any mode either alone or in conjunction with others which is of such a character as to invite attention to him or to his professional position, skill, qualification, achievements, attainments, specialities, appointments, associations, affiliations or honours and/or of such character as would ordinarily result in his self aggrandizement. A physician shall not give to any person, whether for compensation or otherwise, any approval, recommendation, endorsement, certificate, report or statement with respect of any drug, medicine, nostrum remedy, surgical, or therapeutic article, apparatus or appliance or any commercial product or article with respect of any property, quality or use thereof or any test, demonstration or trial thereof, for use in connection with his name, signature, or photograph in any form or manner of advertising through any mode nor shall he boast of cases, operations, cures or remedies or permit the publication of report thereof through any mode. A medical practitioner is however permitted to make a formal announcement in press regarding the following:
> On starting practice.
> On change of type of practice.
> On changing address.
> On temporary absence from duty.
> On resumption of another practice.
> On succeeding to another practice.
> Public declaration of charges.

> **6.1.2** Printing of self photograph, or any such material of publicity in the letter head or on sign board of the consulting room or any such clinical establishment shall be regarded as acts of self advertisement and unethical conduct on the part of the physician. However, printing of sketches, diagrams, picture of human system shall not be treated as unethical.

**Verdict: Confirmed.** Seven occasions. The medium named is "in press".

### B2. Regulations 7.11–7.13

> **7.11** A physician should not contribute to the lay press articles and give interviews regarding diseases and treatments which may have the effect of advertising himself or soliciting practices; but is open to write to the lay press under his own name on matters of public health, hygienic living or to deliver public lectures, give talks on the radio/TV/internet chat for the same purpose and send announcement of the same to lay press.

> **7.12** An institution run by a physician for a particular purpose such as a maternity home, nursing home, private hospital, rehabilitation centre or any type of training institution etc. may be advertised in the lay press, but such advertisements should not contain anything more than the name of the institution, type of patients admitted, type of training and other facilities offered and the fees.

> **7.13** It is improper for a physician to use an unusually large sign board and write on it anything other than his name, qualifications obtained from a University or a statutory body, titles and name of his speciality, registration number including the name of the State Medical Council under which registered. The same should be the contents of his prescription papers. It is improper to affix a sign-board on a chemist's shop or in places where he does not reside or work.

**Verdict: Confirmed.** 7.12 is the own-institution rule: an institution "run by a physician" may be advertised in the lay press with name, type of patients, facilities and fees only.

### B3. Regulations 7.14 and 7.17 — confidentiality and patient images

> **7.14** The registered medical practitioner shall not disclose the secrets of a patient that have been learnt in the exercise of his / her profession except – i) in a court of law under orders of the Presiding Judge; ii) in circumstances where there is a serious and identified risk to a specific person and / or community; and iii) notifiable diseases. In case of communicable / notifiable diseases, concerned public health authorities should be informed immediately.

> **7.17** A registered medical practitioner shall not publish photographs or case reports of his / her patients without their permission, in any medical or other journal in a manner by which their identity could be made out. If the identity is not to be disclosed, the consent is not needed.

**Verdict: Confirmed.**

### B4. Regulation 3.7.1 — fees; 1.4 — registration number

> **3.7.1** A physician shall clearly display his fees and other charges on the board of his chamber and/or the hospitals he is visiting. Prescription should also make clear if the Physician himself dispensed any medicine.
>
> **3.7.2** A physician shall write his name and designation in full along with registration particulars in his prescription letter head.

> **1.4 Display of registration numbers:**
> **1.4.1** Every physician shall display the registration number accorded to him by the State Medical Council / Medical Council of India in his clinic and in all his prescriptions, certificates, money receipts given to his patients.
> **1.4.2** Physicians shall display as suffix to their names only recognized medical degrees or such certificates/diplomas and memberships/honours which confer professional knowledge or recognizes any exemplary qualification/achievements.

**Verdict: 3.7.1 Confirmed. 1.4.2 citation Corrected:** the registration-number rule is **1.4.1**. 1.4.2 limits the suffixes after his name.

### B5. Regulation 6.4 — rebates and commission

> **6.4.1** A physician shall not give, solicit, or receive nor shall he offer to give solicit or receive, any gift, gratuity, commission or bonus in consideration of or return for the referring, recommending or procuring of any patient for medical, surgical or other treatment. A physician shall not directly or indirectly, participate in or be a party to act of division, transference, assignment, subordination, rebating, splitting or refunding of any fee for medical, surgical or other treatment.
>
> **6.4.2** Provisions of para 6.4.1 shall apply with equal force to the referring, recommending or procuring by a physician or any person, specimen or material for diagnostic purposes or other study / work. Nothing in this section, however, shall prohibit payment of salaries by a qualified physician to other duly qualified person rendering medical care under his supervision.

**Verdict: Confirmed.**

### B6. Regulation 6.8 — pharmaceutical and allied health sector industry

Inserted by notification published 14.12.2009; title and 6.8.1(b) amended and penalty table added by notification published 01.02.2016.

> **6.8 Code of conduct for doctors in their relationship with pharmaceutical and allied health sector industry**
>
> **6.8.1** In dealing with Pharmaceutical and allied health sector industry, a medical practitioner shall follow and adhere to the stipulations given below:-
>
> a) Gifts: A medical practitioner shall not receive any gift from any pharmaceutical or allied health care industry and their sales people or representatives.
>
> (b) Travel Facilities : A medical practitioner shall not accept any travel Facility inside the country or outside, including rail, road, air, ship, cruise tickets, paid vacation, etc. from any pharmaceutical or allied healthcare industry or their representatives for self and family members for vacation or for attending conferences, seminars, workshops, CME Programme, etc. as a delegate.
>
> c) Hospitality: A medical practitioner shall not accept individually any hospitality like hotel accommodation for self and family members under any pretext.
>
> d) Cash or monetary grants: A medical practitioner shall not receive any cash or monetary grants from any pharmaceutical and allied healthcare industry for individual purpose in individual capacity under any pretext. Funding for medical research, study etc. can only be received through approved institutions by modalities laid down by law / rules / guidelines adopted by such approved institutions, in a transparent manner. It shall always be fully disclosed.
>
> g) Affiliation: A medical practitioner may work for pharmaceutical and allied healthcare industries in advisory capacities, as consultants, as researchers, as treating doctors or in any other professional capacity. In doing so, a medical practitioner shall always: (i) Ensure that his professional integrity and freedom are maintained. (ii) Ensure that patients interest are not compromised in any way. (iii) Ensure that such affiliations are within the law. (iv) Ensure that such affiliations / employments are fully transparent and disclosed.
>
> h) Endorsement: A medical practitioner shall not endorse any drug or product of the industry publically. Any study conducted on the efficacy or otherwise of such products shall be presented to and / or through appropriate scientific bodies or published in appropriate scientific journals in a proper way.

Penalty table (2016), same bands for gifts, travel, hospitality and cash grants:

> … more than Rs. 1,000/- upto Rs. 5,000/-: Censure
> … more than Rs. 5,000/- upto Rs. 10,000/-: Removal from Indian Medical Register or State Medical Register for 3 (three) months.
> … more than Rs. 10,000/- to Rs. 50,000/-: Removal … for 6 (six) months.
> … more than Rs. 50,000/- to Rs. 1,00,000/-: Removal … for 1 (one) year.
> … more than Rs. 1,00,000/-: Removal for a period of more than 1 (one) year …

**Verdict: Confirmed.** The travel bar in (b) is worded "as a delegate". The hospitality bar in (c) is "under any pretext" and has no speaker exception.

## F. IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026

**Source: secondary.** The gazette was not opened and the G.S.R. number is unverified. Two law-firm notes were read in full; the Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas note sets old and new rule text side by side.

Dates (Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas; Khaitan & Co): notified 10 February 2026; in force 20 February 2026.

**Three hours** — amended rule 3(1)(d), as reproduced by Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas (the note's table labels it "Rule 3(2)(d)"):

> … shall, upon receiving the actual knowledge under clause (b) of sub-section (3) of Section 79 of the Act on such information, remove or disable access to such information within three hours of the receipt of such actual knowledge …

The old text read "within thirty-six hours". Khaitan: "reduce the timeline to act upon a court order or government reasoned intimation for takedown of unlawful content from 36 hours to 3 hours".

**Two hours** — amended rule 3(2)(b), as reproduced:

> (b) The intermediary shall, within two hours from the receipt of a complaint made by an individual or any person on his behalf under this sub-rule, in relation to any content which is prima facie in the nature of any material which exposes the private area of such individual, shows such individual in full or partial nudity or shows or depicts such individual in any sexual act or conduct, or is in the nature of impersonation in an electronic form, including artificially morphed images of such individual, take all reasonable and practicable measures …

The old period was twenty-four hours.

**Thirty-six hours** — amended rule 3(2)(a), grievance officer, as reproduced:

> … acknowledge the complaint within twenty-four hours and resolve such complaint within a period of seven days from the date of its receipt: Provided that the complaint in the nature of request for removal of information or communication link relating to clause(b) of sub-rule (1) of Rule 3, except sub-clauses (i), (iv) and [(xi)], shall be acted upon as expeditiously as possible and shall be resolved within thirty-six hours of such reporting

The old periods were fifteen days and seventy-two hours.

**Labelling of synthetically generated information (SGI)** — law-firm summaries, not rule text:

> Khaitan & Co: "Visual SGI must now carry labels that are prominent, easily noticeable, and adequately perceivable, while audio SGI are required to carry a prominently prefixed audio disclosure."
>
> Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas: "SSMIs that enable the display, upload, or publication of information must, before such content is made available, require users to declare whether the content is synthetically generated information."

Both notes record that the draft's 10 per cent surface-area watermark was dropped, and that routine editing, transcription, translation, subtitling and accessibility features done in good faith fall outside the SGI definition.

**Verdict: Confirmed in substance; gazette text not seen.** All three timelines are duties of **intermediaries** (platforms). For an individual user the practical points are two: large platforms must ask the uploader to declare synthetic content, and a person who is impersonated can invoke the two-hour route in rule 3(2)(b).

## G. Case law

### G1. *MIOT Hospitals Private Limited v. Dr Balaraman Palaniappan*

**Source: the judgment** (copy hosted by Verdictum, carrying the Madras High Court's judis footer). Neutral citation 2026:MHC:733; Arb.O.P.(Com.Div.) No. 708 of 2025; Justice N. Anand Venkatesh; reserved 18.2.2026, delivered 23.2.2026. Petition under section 11(6) of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, on a "professional agreement dated 08.9.2022".

The clauses in dispute:

> 8.2. Non-solicitation: The Contractee agrees and undertakes that during the term of this Agreement and for a period of 3 (three) years thereafter, the Contractee shall not directly or indirectly solicit with a view to offer employment to or contract with any of the Contractor's staff/ member(s)/worker(s), in order for them to leave the employment/working arrangement of the Contractor.
>
> 8.3. Non-compete: The Contractee agrees and undertakes that for a period of 3 (three) years after the termination of this Agreement, the Contractee shall not join any rival hospital or set up a practice, in the vicinity of 15 km from the Contractor's Hospital in Chennai.

Holding:

> A doctor is an independent professional, who cannot be stopped from rendering his services wherever he wants to and also cannot be stopped from attending to patients just because those patients were earlier taking treatment in the petitioner hospital.

> By no stretch, one hospital can treat another hospital as a rival and consequently, the non-compete clause can never form part of an agreement between a hospital and a doctor.

> 18. … even assuming that a client, who was earlier seeking for the professional services of the law firm, later decides to approach the advocate, who earlier served in the law firm, that can never be treated as a violation of a non solicit clause. Ultimately, whether it is a patient or a client, he will engage a firm/ hospital or an advocate/doctor, with whom, he has confidence.

> 22. An agreement entered into by a doctor with a hospital, which contains a non solicitation and/or non compete clause, is certainly opposed to public policy and such an agreement is squarely hit by Section 23 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. Consequently, it must be held to be unlawful, unenforceable and void ab initio to that extent.
>
> 23. The above clauses also go against Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 since a doctor is restrained from practising his profession and more so, when such restraints are sought to be enforced even after the expiry of the contract.

> 32. Accordingly, the above original petition stands dismissed with costs of Rs.1,00,000/- (Rupees one lakh only) payable by the petitioner hospital to the respondent doctor.

**Verdict on the holding: Confirmed.** Limits: one judge of the Madras High Court; a section 11 petition; the non-compete was post-termination; the written non-solicit clause concerned staff, and the court's remarks on patients are in paragraph 18. The judgment does not rule on exclusivity during the term of a contract. It is persuasive, not binding, in Telangana.

## What the synthesis must not say

Some of these statements are in the brief or in files 01–29 in these or similar words (for example items 3, 4, 8, 13, 15, 16, 17, 23 and 24). The rest are easy over-readings of the same material. The files were not audited line by line. The checks above do not support any of them as worded.

1. **"The NMC regulations of October 2026."** They are Guidelines issued by public notice and "read with" the 2002 Regulations. Where they conflict irreconcilably, "the applicable statutory provisions and operative regulations shall prevail" (cl. 4.2).
2. **"A first violation brings a warning, a second a fine, a third suspension."** The ladder is what the State Medical Council "may" contemplate; monetary penalty is only "as may be authorized under the NMC Act and applicable Regulations".
3. **"No paid reach" or "boosting is banned", as a quoted rule.** No clause says this. Cl. 3.2 treats "sponsored, targeted" communications as advertising "where their content or manner of dissemination has a promotional character", and cl. 5.1 requires education not to be "monetized through promotional marketing". Say it is a cautious reading, not text.
4. **"Cl. 6.2 bans testimonials even with consent."** Cl. 6.2 says consent does not "by itself" make an otherwise prohibited practice permissible. Cite Explanation V to 3.2, 8.1(ii), 8.1(v) and 8.1(xi) for the prohibition.
5. **"He may not ask for or share reviews" without the qualifier.** Explanation V bars requesting or sharing them "for professional promotion on social media", and bars soliciting "fake, paid or misleading" reviews outright. Whether a routine request for a Google review is "professional promotion on social media" is an interpretation; cl. 3.6 defines social media broadly.
6. **"Cl. 8.3 permits doctors to …".** 8.3 governs hospitals and bars "promotional portrayal of an individual RMP". Doctors' exceptions are in 8.2 only.
7. **"Cl. 7.3 exempts government campaigns."** It is the Explanation after 7.3.
8. **"Registration number must appear on every post."** Explanation III says "should", covers "Electronic media posts", and also asks for name, qualifications and registration status. Recommend doing it; do not call it a "must" in quotation marks.
9. **"Formal announcements may be made on social media."** The 2026 text names no medium; the 2002 Regulation says "in press". Either describe the medium as unsettled or keep announcements to the press and to factual notices.
10. **"Owning a clinic lets him present its particulars on his personal website under cl. 9.1."** 9.1 speaks of clinical establishments and of a hospital's doctor directory. Applying it to his own clinic's site is an inference from the central Act's definition; applying it to a personal site has no textual support.
11. **"Public talks may mention his own clinic because only the employer is excluded."** 8.2(iii) says "without promoting their employer organization"; 5.1 separately says "do not promote personal practice". Both apply.
12. **"8.2(i) lets him share his research on social media."** The words are: "Only research-based communication, wherein they have published interesting case studies in reputed medical journals shall be allowed."
13. **"Regulation 1.4.2 requires display of the registration number."** It is 1.4.1.
14. **"The TGMC circular of 17 April 2025 invalidates Andhra Pradesh numbers" or "sets a fee or penalty".** It does neither. The consequence of non-renewal comes from section 15C of the Registration Act.
15. **"Telangana Act 21 of 2025 adopted the central Clinical Establishments Act."** It repealed the 2002 state Act with effect from 27.03.2017. Adoption was by resolutions in March 2017.
16. **"Running an unregistered clinic costs Rs 50,000 to Rs 5 lakh."** Up to Rs 50,000 on first contravention; up to Rs 2 lakh on second; up to Rs 5 lakh thereafter.
17. **"DPDP duties begin on 13 May 2027" stated as beyond doubt.** One leading firm reads the date as 14 May 2027. Use 13 May as the working deadline and say why.
18. **"The IT Rules require him to label AI content within set hours."** The 3-hour, 2-hour and 36-hour duties and the labelling duty bind intermediaries. His exposure is the platform's uploader declaration and, as a possible victim, the two-hour impersonation route.
19. **"Non-compete and non-solicit clauses for doctors are void in India."** One Madras High Court single-judge order says so for post-termination clauses in a section 11 petition; a legal journal reports a Supreme Court appeal with status quo. It does not cover exclusivity during the term, and it does not bind Telangana courts.
20. **Any statement of the current position in *Himalaya Wellness v. Dr Philips*.** Nothing after October 2023 was verified. The April 2026 Kerala order concerns a different company.
21. **"The Supreme Court has approved the Guidelines" or "W.P.(C) 1160/2023 has been disposed of."** Status unknown.
22. **"UCPMP allows speaker honoraria."** It allows travel and hospitality for a CME or CPD speaker and consultancy fees or honoraria for bona-fide research services. It is silent on speaker fees, and Regulation 6.8 still binds the doctor.
23. **"Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020, cl. 5.4 (or 5.7)" with a verbatim quotation.** The 2020 text was not opened. Describe the rule in substance and cite it as "Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020 (official text to be re-checked)".
24. **"TGMC charges Rs 1,000 per missing CPD hour."** Not verified in this pass; no primary document was opened for it.
25. **"CDSCO's July 2026 guidance exempts clinical decision support" or any transition date.** Neither secondary source says so.
