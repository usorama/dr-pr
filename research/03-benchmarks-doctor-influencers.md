# Benchmarks: how doctors actually became publicly known and trusted — and the repeatable playbooks

**Research date:** 12 November 2026. **Method note:** the shared web-search budget ran out after about 24 searches, so the second half of this work used direct page fetches (Wikipedia, vidIQ channel pages, news articles, Substack/blog "about" pages). X/Twitter, Instagram and YouTube profile pages could not be loaded directly, so several follower counts are from third-party trackers or cached snapshots and are flagged.

## Executive summary

1. Doctor fame comes by three different routes: **institution and mission** (Shetty, Manjunath, Trehan), **writing and evidence interpretation** (Gawande, Verghese, Topol, Mandrola), and **platform teaching or performance** (Dr. Sudhir Kumar, Dr. Pal, Doctor Mike). Only the third needs an on-camera personality.
2. The closest live precedent is in his own hospital group: **Dr. Sudhir Kumar, neurologist, Apollo Hyderabad**, built 100K+ X followers with text-only weekly threads on prevention, with his Apollo 24/7 consult link in his bio. No video, no dancing, no feuds.
3. Median outcomes are modest. Of the 20 "top cardiology YouTube channels" in a Sept 2024 list, only 3 had over 100K subscribers; most individual cardiologists sat at 5–16K after years. Plan for a 24-month build, not a viral break.
4. Almost every breakout had one of four triggers: a single resonant case story picked up by news, a crisis (COVID), a long-form piece in a major outlet, or a mission the public could repeat in one sentence ("treatment first, payment next").
5. The backlash pattern is equally repeatable: naming companies or other systems of medicine (Liver Doc: account withheld by court order), drifting outside one's expertise (Aseem Malhotra), and private conduct surfacing (Peter Attia, Feb 2026). An introverted, evidence-first cardiologist who never names brands and stays in cardiology avoids most of it.

### 2. Archetypes of doctor reputation

| Archetype | What the public says about them | Examples | Main asset | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Mission-builder** | "He made heart care affordable" | Shetty, Manjunath, Trehan, Panda | Institution plus a one-line promise | Needs decades and capital; politics |
| **2. Master craftsman-teacher** | "Other cardiologists learn from him" | Ashok Seth | Volume, firsts, live cases, trainees | Invisible to the public unless translated |
| **3. Public-health statesman** | "Government listens to him" | Srinath Reddy, Gulati (guidelines) | Committees, guidelines, policy papers | Slow; needs institutional base |
| **4. Preventive teacher / explainer** | "He explains it so I understand" | Sudhir Kumar, York Cardiology, Kongara, Kancherla | Consistency and clarity | Plateau; content treadmill |
| **5. Evidence interpreter** | "He tells me what the new study really means" | Topol, Mandrola, Rohin Francis | Reading and judgement | Contrarian drift; being wrong in public |
| **6. Myth-buster / crusader** | "He fights quackery" | Liver Doc, Doctor Mike (debunks), ZDoggMD | Conflict drives reach | Lawsuits, harassment, tone backlash |
| **7. Writer-humanist** | "His essay stayed with me" | Gawande, Verghese, Mukherjee, Kalanithi | One piece can last decades | Patient confidentiality; slow output |
| **8. Entertainer** | "He's fun to watch" | Dr. Pal, Doctor Mike, Dr. Cuterus | Reach | Credibility dilution; personality-dependent |
| **9. Curator / community builder** | "He brought us together" | KevinMD, Sensible Medicine, #FITTweetup (Briana Costello) | Amplifies others; low self-display | Moderation burden |

### 3. Which archetypes fit an introverted, humble interventional cardiologist in Hyderabad

Ranked fit (my judgement, built on the cases above):

1. **Preventive teacher (archetype 4), written-first, Telugu plus English.** Direct precedent at Apollo Hyderabad (Sudhir Kumar). No camera needed at the start. A verified large Telugu cardiology channel was not found, so the lane looks open (L, needs a proper channel audit).
2. **Evidence interpreter (5), for doctors.** A weekly "one trial, what it changes in my cath lab" note for GPs, physicians and fellows. Mandrola built a national voice this way from a private practice. A study of 1,500 US cardiologists found only 16.3% had a Twitter handle, and that social influence tracked posting activity, not citation count (2021; stale but directionally useful).
3. **Master craftsman-teacher (2).** Case-based teaching for fellows and at CSI/state chapter meetings. This is how Ashok Seth's peer reputation was built, and peer reputation drives referrals.
4. **Writer-humanist (7), occasionally.** One de-identified, consented essay a quarter in a newspaper or journal. Kalanithi and Gawande show one piece can outweigh 500 posts.
5. **Small-scale mission (1).** A single repeatable promise he can keep personally, for example a STEMI door-to-balloon commitment or CPR training for apartment communities. Manjunath's four-word promise did more than any campaign.

Poor fit: **crusader (6)** and **entertainer (8)**. Both reward traits he does not have and carry the legal and credibility risks documented above.

### 4. Common failure modes and backlash stories

| Failure mode | Case | What happened | Rule it implies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naming companies or other medical systems | Liver Doc | Ex parte injunction; entire X account withheld (Sept 2023); multiple council complaints, an FIR, and a police summons from another state | Criticise claims and ingredients with citations; never name a brand or call a system fraud without legal review |
| Tone | Liver Doc vs. Samantha (2024) | Had to apologise for "health illiterate" wording | Correct the claim, not the person |
| Drift outside expertise | Malhotra; Shetty's COVID role | Professional bodies disowned him; Shetty criticised | Stay inside cardiology and general prevention |
| Private conduct and associations | Attia (Feb 2026) | Lost two roles within weeks | Reputation is the whole person; no shortcut partnerships |
| Being wrong in public | Mukherjee (2016); Sanjay Gupta (2013) | Both admitted error openly and kept their standing | Publish corrections quickly and visibly |
| Fame for the wrong thing | Doctor Mike (looks); Deepak Krishnamurthy (pay grievance story) | News framed them on a side issue | Decide the one thing to be known for; decline the rest |
| Superlative claims | "World's safest surgeon", "99.8% success" | Flagged as promotional | No superlatives or unaudited outcome numbers |

- Mandrola: blog 2009; national column followed; Substack co-founded 13 years later.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| X, English, 4–5 posts and 1 thread a week | 300–1,500 | 1,000–4,000 | 3,000–12,000 | 10,000–40,000 |

How to read this: the lower bound is what cardiologists in the Sept 2024 list reached after years; the upper bound assumes one news pick-up of a case story, which is how Sudhir Kumar's and Deepak Krishnamurthy's accounts jumped. Growth is stepwise, not smooth: long flat stretches, then a jump after a trigger. Telugu video has the highest ceiling (Kongara, Kancherla) and the highest effort.

## So what for Dr. Avinash

Prioritised; effort is his time unless marked "friend".

1. **Pick one sentence to be known for (one-off, 2 hours).** Model: "On a mission to prevent people from becoming patients" (Sudhir Kumar) or "Treatment first, payment next" (Manjunath). Everything published is tested against it.
2. **Start written-first on X and LinkedIn in the Sudhir Kumar format (60–90 min a week; friend schedules and formats, 60 min a week).** One numbered thread a week: one question patients ask, the evidence, what he does. Two or three short posts drawn from the same thread.
3. **Bank de-identified case stories (15 min a week).** The breakout trigger for both Hyderabad and Bengaluru benchmarks was a single case story with a social hook (young patient, overwork, smoking). Keep a private log; publish only with consent and altered details.
4. **Weekly "one trial, one takeaway" note for referring doctors (45 min a week).** Mandrola model. Send by WhatsApp broadcast and email to GPs and physicians in west Hyderabad. This builds peer trust, which is what Ashok Seth's reputation rests on.
5. **Telugu video from month 4, low production (2 hours a fortnight, batch-recorded).** York Cardiology format: seated, calm, one topic, 6–8 minutes. Test the open lane in Telugu cardiology before investing more.
6. **One guest slot a month on someone else's audience (1–2 hours a month).** Telugu aggregator and podcast channels (the TGV Telugu and SumanTV type) lend reach, but always point viewers to his own channel.
7. **One long-form essay a quarter (4–6 hours a quarter).** Newspaper op-ed or journal perspective. Writer-humanist pieces compound for years.
8. **One teachable asset a year (one-off 20–30 hours).** For example a STEMI or ECG teaching series for junior doctors, presented at a state cardiology meeting. This is the craftsman-teacher route.
9. **Measure trust, not followers (friend, 30 min a month).** Track referrals mentioning content, invitations to speak, journalist queries, and saves and shares. Expect about 3,000–12,000 X followers at 12 months; treat anything more as a bonus.

Total steady-state load: about 3–4 hours a week for him, 2–3 hours for the friend.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Do not name brands, hospitals, colleagues or other systems of medicine.** The Liver Doc record shows what follows: injunctions, account loss, out-of-state police summons.
- **No superlatives or self-reported outcome numbers** ("best", "safest", success percentages). They read as promotion and are the kind of claim Indian medical conduct rules restrict; the regulatory agent's file should govern the exact wording.
- **No patient-identifying detail, images or testimonials** without written consent and hospital clearance.
- **Do not chase trending fights or grievance topics** (doctor pay, work hours of other professions). They travel, but they define you.
- **Stay inside cardiology and prevention.** Every documented collapse of trust involved drift (vaccines, diets, epidemiology).
- **Do not depend on one platform or one hospital.** Deepak Krishnamurthy changed hospitals three times in public view; the account carried over. An X account can be withheld by a single court order.
- **Avoid sponsored content and product tie-ups** for at least the first two years. Attia's and Malhotra's cases show how fast commercial or campaign associations turn.
- **Check Apollo's and Citizens' social media policy before the first post.** Topol's case shows institutional retaliation is real; Sudhir Kumar's shows Apollo can live with a personal account.
- **Cap the time.** A cardiologist needing a sabbatical for his own health is the wrong outcome.
