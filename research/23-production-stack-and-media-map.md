## Executive summary

1. **The whole kit is cheap; the scarce input is his 2 hours a week.** A phone, a ₹1,000 wired lapel microphone (or a ₹7,850–11,950 DJI Mic Mini set), a ₹1,200 tripod and one soft light are enough. No benchmark for Telugu medical vocabulary exists that I could find. Descript does not transcribe Telugu at all. YouTube auto-captions cover Telugu and YouTube auto-dubbing does Telugu→English, but a dub cannot be edited. So: record Telugu and English natively, and have a human correct every Telugu caption against a glossary.

#### 1.3 A quiet-room set-up inside a clinic (practitioner guidance, Medium)

- Record in the consulting room after the last patient, door closed, a "recording" card on the handle. Switch the air-conditioner and fan off for each take (takes are short), phones on silent, reception told.
- Hard walls echo. A curtain, the examination couch with a blanket on it, and a bookshelf behind him do more than any software. The lapel microphone a hand's width below the chin matters more than the room.
- One soft light at 45° to the face, slightly above eye level; face a window if recording by day; never have the window behind him.
- **Frame check before every recording (this is the compliance step):** no patient files, prescription pads, appointment screens, whiteboards, monitors, name lists or visitors in frame or audible (NMC cl. 7.4: no identifiable patient data); no hospital logo wall (cl. 8.2(iii): no promoting the employer); and no clinic signboard, fee board or phone number either, because owning the practice relaxes nothing (cl. 5.1; report 11b). A plain wall or bookshelf is the background.
- Sit, do not stand. He is answering a friend's question, not presenting.

#### 1.4 Screen-and-voice explainers: the low-social-energy format

He draws while talking; his face need not appear. Three workable set-ups:

1. **iPad + Apple Pencil** (₹57,800 as listed): draw in any notes or whiteboard app, use the iPad's built-in screen recording with the lapel microphone plugged in or paired. One device, no editing needed beyond trimming the ends.
2. **Android tablet with bundled pen** (₹26,199–34,999): same method.
3. **Laptop + pen tablet + USB microphone**: record the screen with the free OBS Studio or the operating system's recorder [P].

Why this suits the rules as well as his temperament: a heart, a coronary tree or an ECG strip **drawn by him** is his own copyright and contains no patient data, which removes both the image-licensing question and the patient-image prohibition in one move.

**How to read the numbers.** The IISc table (SPIRE Lab and ARTPARK, IISc Bangalore; arXiv 2608.08235) is the only like-for-like, non-vendor comparison found: 17.77 hours of Telugu audio across six data sets, same text normalisation for all systems. On it, the best systems get roughly **one word in four wrong**. Three caveats apply. First, Telugu is agglutinative, so one wrong suffix counts as a whole wrong word; Sarvam's own evaluation note argues character error rate is fairer for Telugu and that word error rate over-penalises code-mixed speech where an English term may validly be written in either script [V]. Second, a single speaker with a lapel microphone in a quiet room is easier than these test sets, so his real error rate should be lower [E]. Third, and decisive: **no benchmark of Telugu medical vocabulary was found**, and drug names, test names and English terms spoken inside Telugu sentences ("ఎకో", "troponin", "ejection fraction") are exactly where errors concentrate and where an error matters most. Confidence in the overall picture: Medium.

**Working rule.** Machine transcript → the friend or a Telugu reviewer corrects it against a standing glossary (his preferred Telugu rendering of about 150 terms, built once) → he approves. Shorts are recorded **natively in each language**; nothing is dubbed. YouTube's Telugu→English auto-dub can be switched on for long videos only with "Publish manually" and only if someone listens to the whole dub, because a wrong dose or a wrong drug name in his apparent voice cannot be corrected, only withdrawn.

#### 2.3 WhatsApp Channels — official rules and features [V from WhatsApp's own pages unless marked]

- **What it is:** "a one-way broadcast rather than a conversation". Updates sit in the Updates tab; followers "can't reply to updates or message admins". Notifications are muted by default.
- **Privacy:** people viewing a channel cannot see who follows it; a follower's name, number and photo are not visible to other followers. An admin sees a follower's **profile name**, and the profile photo next to reactions or poll votes depending on the follower's settings. Admins who already have the follower as a contact can see the full profile including the phone number. So a channel does not hand him a list of patients' numbers, and patients do not see each other.
- **Admins:** "You can invite up to 16 additional channel admins." Admins can change channel info and settings, create, edit and delete updates, and see all channel metrics. Admins are visible to each other.
- **Sharing:** each channel has a link and a QR code that can be shared to chats, Status and other apps.
- **Features:** text, links, photos, videos; emoji reactions; polls.
- **Verification:** WhatsApp's help page says "Channel verification isn't publicly available yet."
- **Rules (Channels Guidelines):** four heads — no illegal content; nothing that could seriously harm people; no fraud, impersonation or wilful misrepresentation; age-appropriate content, including no offering or transacting in "regulated or restricted goods". Admins are also asked not to flood followers or post low-quality content and to use a title that reflects the content. **There is no section on health, medical advice or misinformation.** Enforcement ranges from a warning, through removal from the directory and revoking the invite link, to suspending the channel and banning admins. The Supplemental Terms make admins responsible for content.
- **Not confirmed on the help pages this session:** the 30-day retention of updates and the 30-day metrics window reported in report 05; the exact metrics shown. Check in the app.
- **Health content restrictions:** none from WhatsApp. The constraints are Indian: the NMC guidelines list "messaging platforms (WhatsApp …)" as an advertising medium (cl. 3.5), so the channel must stay educational, carry the credential line (Explanation III) and never carry appointment prompts, fees or offers.

### 3. A weekly production workflow

**Design choices.** One recorded conversation of 20–30 minutes in which the friend asks six to eight agreed questions; at the end, two "clean answers" of 60–90 seconds each, one in Telugu and one in English, to the single most useful question of the session. The shorts are those two clean answers, recorded natively, not excerpts hunted out of the long recording and not dubs. Everything written is drafted from the transcript.

**Two pipelines, never mixed** (report 18): the content pipeline holds only what he said for the public; it has its own cloud folder and its own AI workspace. No patient names, images, reports, dates, bed numbers or recognisable stories enter it. Illustrative scenarios are composites and are labelled as such on screen and in text.

### 5. What "being a source" looks like under the 2026 rules

**The frame.** Permitted: speaking on public health "in their own name and designation, without promoting their employer organization" (cl. 8.2(iii)); writing in the lay press under his own name on public health (2002 Reg. 7.11). Not permitted: anything with "the effect of advertising himself", patient stories, superiority claims, facility talk, paid or sponsored appearances, a third party marketing him (cl. 8.1). The friend arranging logistics for genuinely educational comment is defensible; the friend "pitching the doctor" is not. So the note below offers a public service and is sent once per reporter, not as a campaign.

**5.1 A sample "expert availability" note** (WhatsApp or email, sent by him or by the friend from the media line; adjust facts before use)

> Dear Ms/Mr ___,
> I am Dr Avinash Pathuri, MD (General Medicine), DM (Cardiology), a cardiologist in Hyderabad. [Registration: council and number — to be filled once the Telangana/Andhra Pradesh position is settled.]
> If you ever need a plain-language explanation of a heart-health story — a new study, heart attacks in younger people, cardiac arrest and CPR, blood pressure and cholesterol, life after a stent — I am glad to help, in English or Telugu (తెలుగులో కూడా వివరించగలను).
> How I work: I explain the medicine and the evidence. I do not comment on any named person's illness, I do not discuss my own patients, and I do not speak about hospitals or their services. Please identify me by name, qualification and "cardiologist, Hyderabad".
> Messages to this number between ___ and ___ are answered within the hour; outside those hours, by the next morning. If I am not the right person for a subject I will say so quickly.
> I have no payment or sponsorship arrangement with any company relevant to these subjects. [Or: list them.]

**5.2 Press-kit outline that stays factual** (one page on his own site plus a shared folder; extends report 08 s.6)

1. Name as registered and as used publicly; qualifications with year and university (MD 2016, MAHE; DM Cardiology 2020, WBUHS, per the national register); council and registration number.
2. A 50-word and a 150-word biography containing only verifiable facts: training, current role described generically, teaching and society memberships, publications. No procedure counts, no "leading", no outcomes.
3. Subjects he will explain (ten lines) and subjects he will not (individual cases, named people, hospital comparisons, product endorsements).
4. Languages.
5. Two plain photographs (no logo wall, no cath-lab hero shot) with a credit line.
6. Three sample explanations: two short videos and one written piece.
7. Disclosures: industry relationships, if any; statement that he accepts no payment for media comment and gives none.
8. How to credit him; contact route and hours; a line that comment is general education and not individual medical advice.
9. Date last updated.

### 6. Public-interest tools: an RTI on the 2023 CPR/AED plan

**6.1 The law and the timelines** (RTI Act 2005; standard provisions [P, High]; the Wikipedia summary read this session confirms the 30-day and 48-hour limits): any citizen may apply to the Public Information Officer (PIO) of a public authority, without giving reasons (s.6); reply due within 30 days (s.7(1)); if the request belongs to another authority the PIO must transfer it within 5 days (s.6(3)); no reply in time is a deemed refusal (s.7(2)) and information supplied late is free (s.7(6)); first appeal to the designated senior officer within 30 days (s.19(1)); second appeal to the State Information Commission within 90 days (s.19(3)).

**6.2 Telangana specifics**

- **Online portal: rti.telangana.gov.in exists but is not dependable** [V, news reports]. Siasat (30 Jul 2026), reporting a public-interest petition in the Telangana High Court: 3,323 public authorities have been added since the December 2022 launch but only 17 use it to reply; from December 2023 to 2025 it received 17,865 applications and disposed of 377 (2.1%); 2,502 first appeals were filed through it and none resolved; the State was given a week to respond. Deccan Chronicle (31 Jan 2026) gave near-identical figures from a Hyderabad NGO, said the vendor's ₹2.07 crore contract had expired in 2025, and reported OTP failures blocking registration. Telangana Today (8 May 2025): the IT department had to circulate an instruction telling departments to clear pending online applications. The portal returned an error to me on 12 Nov 2026. Whether the Health department is among the responding authorities: not verified.
- **Therefore file on paper.** Address: The Public Information Officer, Office of the Commissioner of Health and Family Welfare, Government of Telangana, Hyderabad (street address to be taken from the department's own notice board or website; the site was unreachable). Send by Registered Post with acknowledgment due or Speed Post, or hand-deliver and get a dated stamp on a copy. Optionally lodge the same text on the portal as a second track.
- **Fee:** ₹10 for a state-level office, by Indian Postal Order or demand draft; a guide site and the High Court's own RTI portal both show ₹10. The state fee rules as I recall them (the 2005 Andhra Pradesh rules carried over: nil at village level, ₹5 at mandal level, ₹10 otherwise; copies at ₹2 a page) [P, Medium] — **I could not open the rules themselves this session.** Below-poverty-line applicants pay nothing.
- **Second appeals:** Telangana Information Commission, tgic.telangana.gov.in; the State portal lists the Chief Information Commissioner as Dr G. Chandrashekar Reddy, office telephone 040-24740107 [V].

**6.3 Wording** (ask for records, not explanations; one subject; numbered; dated)

> To: The Public Information Officer, O/o the Commissioner of Health and Family Welfare, Government of Telangana, Hyderabad.
> Subject: Request for information under section 6(1) of the Right to Information Act, 2005 — CPR training and Automated External Defibrillators (AEDs) announced in March 2023.
> I am a citizen of India. Please provide the following information held on record:
> 1. A copy of the order(s) or proceedings sanctioning the procurement of AEDs announced in March 2023, showing the number sanctioned and the budget.
> 2. The number of AEDs purchased up to the date of this application, with purchase-order dates, supplier names and unit cost.
> 3. The list of places where these AEDs are installed (institution or location name, district), with the date of installation of each.
> 4. The number of people trained in CPR under the programme, by district and by financial year from 2022–23 to date, and by category (health staff, police, other government staff, members of the public).
> 5. The schedule and records of inspection and maintenance of the AEDs (battery and pad replacement), and the designation of the officer responsible.
> 6. The number of occasions, if recorded, on which a programme AED was used on a person, by year.
> 7. A copy of any progress, review or evaluation report on the programme.
> If any part of this is held by another public authority, please transfer that part under section 6(3) and inform me. I request the information as photocopies or by email at ___ and will pay the prescribed cost on intimation. Application fee of ₹10 is enclosed by Indian Postal Order No. ___ dated ___.
> Name, postal address, date, signature.

Notes. Do not ask "why" or "what action will be taken"; those invite a refusal. Ask for no personal data (no names of trainees), which keeps the request clear of the personal-information exemption in s.8(1)(j). Parallel one-page requests can go to the PIOs of GHMC, Hyderabad Metro Rail Limited, TGSRTC and the police on "AEDs installed at your premises and staff trained"; central bodies such as South Central Railway and the Airports Authority take requests through the central portal rtionline.gov.in [P].

**6.4 Who files.** Any citizen can. The friend or a civic partner filing in their own name keeps the doctor's name off a document addressed to the government while he is still an Apollo consultant; he can equally file himself — it is a citizen's right, not a promotional act, and nothing in the NMC guidelines touches it. Decide on temperament, not on law.

**6.5 Reporting the request and the answer publicly**

1. Publish a dated page on his own site: what was asked (the application, with the applicant's address redacted), when, and what came back (the reply scanned in full). If nothing comes back in 30 days, say exactly that and that a first appeal has been filed.
2. Summarise in three neutral sentences and one chart; no adjectives, no blame. The frame is "here is what exists, here is the gap, here is what can be done", followed by a concrete offer (free CPR sessions; a public map of AEDs if locations are disclosed).
3. Send the documents to the reporters who cover this ground (M. Sai Gopal, Sumit Jha, Meghna Nath, Siddharth Kumar Singh, Kaniza Garari) with one paragraph and no request for coverage of himself.
4. Keep clinic, hospital and procedures out of it entirely. This is advocacy for a public-health measure, which is the safest category in the guidelines.
5. Update the page when the appeal is decided. A two-year paper trail that ends in a published AED list is a stronger contribution than any number of posts.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Trusting machine Telugu.** A quarter of words wrong on benchmark audio, no medical benchmark, and uneditable YouTube dubs. An uncorrected caption that changes a drug or a dose is a patient-safety error with his name on it.
- **Sharing passwords.** It breaches LinkedIn's user agreement, defeats two-factor authentication and makes the friend a single point of failure. Use the delegation routes in 2.9.
- **Accounts tied to an employer.** Any channel, domain or profile created with a hospital email or by a hospital's agency does not travel with him.
- **The clinic as a film set.** Patient paperwork, screens, voices in the corridor, a logo wall or his own signboard in frame each create a different violation. Do the frame check every time.
- **Paid or sponsored appearances presented as editorial.** Common in Telugu television and print; now clearly advertising. Ask in writing and log the answer. Avoid outlets' pages where his explainer would sit beside clinic rankings and supplement plugs.
- **The friend as publicist.** Logistics for education is defensible; pitching him, chasing coverage of him, or drafting his quotes is "engaging a third party" in substance.
- **Paying for reach by another name.** X Premium and Meta Verified buy ranking as well as a badge; treat as unsettled.
- **Non-commercial image licences on a monetised channel.** LITFL's licence and BioRender's free plan both forbid it. Draw it himself or use CC BY sources with a credit line.
- **WhatsApp API campaigns to patients.** Promotional in character, costs money, and mixes the clinical list with the content pipeline.
- **CapCut and unverified apps.** Availability in India is uncertain; do not make a banned or grey app the centre of the workflow.
- **RTI tone.** A request written as an accusation, or publicised as a personal campaign, turns a public-health contribution into a quarrel with the department he may need as a partner.
- **Stale names.** Three of the reporter names recalled in earlier rounds turned out to be unverifiable or to have moved. Check the byline in the week of first contact.
