# Verified exemplars closest to him: Telugu/Hyderabad doctors with personal public voices, and the Apollo precedent

**Research date:** 12 November 2026. **Method note:** 16 web searches (over the 12 budgeted; the search tool returned little for Telugu channel names) and about 110 direct page fetches. Channel figures are vidIQ readings dated 10–12 Nov 2026 unless stated. Channel handles were resolved through YouTube's own oEmbed endpoint and then read on vidIQ. Not reachable in this session: x.com (402), Nitter/xcancel, Social Blade (403), apollohospitals.com and apollo247 doctor pages (403), sakshi.com (403), YouTube search pages (no content returned), socialveins.com (503). Anything I could not open is marked "could not verify".

## Executive summary

1. **The "open lane" thesis is wrong.** A Telugu cardiologist-owned channel exists and is large: **Dr M S S Mukharjee | Pulse Heart** (Dr Mukharjee Madivada, Miyapur, Hyderabad) has **493K subscribers, 170M views, 733 videos**, and gained 46K subscribers in the last 28 days. It will pass 500K within days at the current 1–2K a day.
2. **His curve is the best planning data we have:** 6 years to reach 58.6K (Nov 2023), then 211K by May 2026, then 270K more in five months after moving to a near-daily 2-minute Telugu Short built on "new study", "which test", and news triggers.
3. **Dr Sudhir Kumar's method is text, not video:** about one evidence thread a week on X, case vignettes with a take-home, and reporters lifting his posts into stories (Telangana Today, Economic Times, Medical Dialogues). His YouTube, after 8 years and 434 videos, has only 11.4K subscribers.
4. **Apollo tolerates personal voices in practice** (Sudhir Kumar since 2004 with Apollo in his bio; Dr Gokhale's personal site and channels), but **no written Apollo policy could be found**. The binding constraint is now the NMC's October 2026 guidelines, not Apollo. Neither exemplar shows a visible change since 6 October.
5. **Realistic targets for a new Telugu+English cardiologist voice:** 10K–25K YouTube subscribers at 12 months and 50K–120K at 24 months, if he publishes 3–4 short Telugu explainers a week. At one English long-form video a week the observed rate is 60–220 subscribers a month (Dr C Raghu, Dr Sudhir Kumar).

**1i. The method, step by step (what the evidence supports)**
1. State a one-line public mission and keep it for years.
2. Anchor in the specialty but write about everyday prevention that every reader can act on.
3. Publish one numbered evidence thread a week; add short posts and replies between.
4. Start from a trigger: a patient's question in clinic, a new study, or a death in the news.
5. Tell the case in five moves: hook, who (age, sex, work), what was missed, the evidence, a bulleted take-home.
6. Cite the study with a link; use Indian data and guidelines.
7. Put the affiliation in the bio only; no hospital name, booking link or self-praise inside content.
8. Let reporters come. Local health reporters monitor the feed; a quotable number or prescription becomes the headline.
9. In disagreement, make one technical point and stop.
10. Accept guest podcasts and doctor-facing webinars; treat video as secondary.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Explaining a named person's death.** Two of Dr Mukharjee's top five videos do this. Without the records it is speculation about an identifiable person, and families object. Use the news as the trigger, then talk about the condition, not the person.
- **Hype titles.** "Reversal", "100 years", "in 8 minutes", "secret" all win views in the data above and all conflict with the NMC bar on guaranteed or exaggerated claims.
- **Copying Sudhir Kumar's case style as it was in 2023.** First names, family details and a prescription photo would not pass the 2026 guidelines, and AIIMS has now banned case details online even when de-identified.
- **Hospital name in channel names, thumbnails or sign-offs.** Dr Mukharjee can do this as an owner at his own regulatory risk; an Apollo consultant falls under "without promoting their employer organization".
- **A consult or booking link in the bio.** Dr Sudhir Kumar's "Online consultation: Apollo 24/7 app" line is the element of his profile most exposed to the solicitation rule.
- **Off-topic candour.** His stock-picks and pay posts became national stories about him rather than about health.
- **Assuming Apollo's tolerance is a rule.** Both precedents are 20-year seniors; nothing found says a junior consultant enjoys the same latitude.
- **Aggregator dependence.** Telugu Health Focus, iDream and PMC own the audience their guests bring; several carry fertility-guarantee and "plant that improves eyesight 100%" videos beside the doctor interviews.
- **Daily output as a starting point.** Dr Mukharjee reached six a week after eight years. Starting there invites burnout in a reluctant communicator.
- **Reading the targets as promises.** They come from survivors.
