# 29 — The trainee ecosystem, and a teaching and research home for a cardiologist who will not be at a teaching hospital

**Research date:** 12 November 2026. **Answers:** task briefs R4-6 and part of R4-3 (file 25, Part 6). **Builds on:** file 19 section C (training landscape, mentoring design), file 16 section 5 (NBEMS faculty rules, TGMC CPD, adjunct routes), file 11 (consent and de-identification protocol). It does not redesign the weekly case conference (file 24) or cover ethics committees (file 21).

## Executive summary

1. **The open Indian teaching series that trainees used have gone quiet.** The two largest found, "Cardiology Lectures DM DNB" (26,100 subscribers) and "pgtrack csidb" (7,430; 100+ numbered sessions), last uploaded three to four years ago. What is active is commercial entrance-exam preparation (DocTutorials SS 76,400, PrepLadder SS 72,900, Marrow SS 41,800 subscribers) and a paid mentorship in Hyderabad at Rs 2,50,000. A free, regular, sponsor-free series has little live competition.
2. **The Telugu states train about 440 cardiology residents at any time, in a list short enough to reach by letter.** DM seats sit in 8 Telangana and 9 Andhra Pradesh colleges (NIMS 8, Osmania 7, Andhra Medical College 7 are the largest); DrNB seats are in about 39 hospital units (file 19).
3. **Open-licence material removes most of the consent burden.** PTB-XL (21,799 ECGs, CC BY 4.0), Chapman-Shaoxing-Ningbo (45,152 ECGs, CC BY 4.0) and ARCADE (3,000 angiogram stills, CC0) can be used in a public atlas with attribution. EchoNet-Dynamic and ECG Wave-Maven cannot. A Creative Commons licence never covers a patient's privacy rights.
4. **Medical-college and NBEMS rules give a visiting clinician no title; other institutions do.** The NMC faculty regulations of 2025 contain no part-time or visiting category; NBEMS excludes part-time consultants. IIT Hyderabad's biomedical engineering department lists two Hyderabad private-hospital clinicians as adjunct faculty, and PHFI (parent of IIPH Hyderabad) lists a private-hospital endocrinologist. UGC "Professor of Practice" needs 15 years' experience.
5. **Every Indian research grant needs an institution; a few international awards do not.** ICMR's 2027 call (closes 20 January 2027; Rs 10 lakh to under Rs 2 crore) requires regular employment at an institution. The SCAI Early Career Research Grant (USD 42,500, closes 26 January 2027) is open to international members. The EAPCI training grant excludes India; its coaching programme and congress grants do not.

### 3. Mentoring: evidence, formats, conduct, recognition

#### 3.1 What structured programmes achieve

| Review | Size | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sambunjak, JAMA 2006 | 39 studies | Mentoring is reported to influence career choice, personal development and research productivity, but 87% of studies were cross-sectional self-reports: "the evidence to support this perception is not strong" | PMID 16954490 [V] |
| Kashiwagi, Acad Med 2013 | 18 programmes | Mentees prefer choosing their mentor; both sides value protected time; **written agreements** set limits and accountability; "no programs reported long-term results" | PMID 23702518 [V] |
| Pethrick, Can Med Educ J 2020 | 9 studies | Peer mentoring in residency gives psychosocial support and higher satisfaction with training | PMID 33349761 [V] |
| Farid, Clin Teach 2022 | 19 studies | Peer groups of 3–104, mostly small groups meeting **monthly**; several report more publications, grants, retention and promotion | PMID 35751456 [V] |
| Winderbaum, Med Educ 2024 | 13 studies | Formal near-peer programmes help first-year doctors; "mentorship alone cannot overcome endemic cultural challenges" | PMID 38221676 [V] |
| Kaba, JBI Evid Synth 2023 | 77 reports | Research mentorship in low- and middle-income countries is limited; 55 projects were funded from high-income countries; only 4 reports came from South-East Asia | PMID 37461876 [V] |
| Cheong, Adv Health Sci Educ 2020 | 98 articles | Ethical breaches arise from "inadequate mentor training, cursory codes of conduct, sketchy standards of practice", poor matching and weak oversight; the host organisation's oversight is central | PMID 31705429 [V] |

Honest summary: mentoring improves satisfaction and, in small studies, output. No trial shows that it improves patient outcomes or long-term careers. It should be offered as a service and measured by what mentees produce.

#### 3.2 Formats that scale for one mentor

| Format | Dose | Evidence or precedent | Output to count |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mentoring circle** (6–8 trainees, mixed years, near-peer lead) | 90 minutes a month | Small monthly groups are the commonest design in peer-mentoring reviews (Farid 2022); near-peer layers work (Winderbaum 2024; file 19) | Attendance; each member's stated goal and whether it was met |
| **Office hours** (open online slot, first come) | 60 minutes a fortnight | SCAI Mentor Connect and ACC's programme show the demand for access to a named senior | Number of conversations; topics log |
| **Case-report clinic** | 60 minutes a month; trainees bring a draft | CardioNerds' fellow-written cases [19]; journals and fees in file 16 | Submissions and acceptances with the trainee as first author |
| **Project pairing** on the EAPCI model | Two hours a month for 12 months, one project per pair | EAPCI Online Coaching Programme (15 pairs a year) | One finished audit, review or abstract per pair |
| **Career letters** (a written letter each quarter on one decision: choosing a fellowship, the first consultant contract, setting up a clinic, a complication) | 2–3 hours a quarter | Fits an introvert; addresses the "careers by chance" and job-search gaps (EAPCI Young; MENTOR) | Reads; replies; reuse by other teachers |
| **Speed mentoring** at a chapter meeting | One hour a year | Pre-post studies in several specialties (PMIDs 20662583, 24816424, 30733161) | Pairs formed |

A realistic ceiling inside the 4-hour weekly cap used in file 25: one circle, one clinic and two office hours a month (about 4.5 hours a month), with letters written in the same time as other writing.

#### 3.3 Conduct and safeguarding

Sources: Cheong 2020; Kashiwagi 2013; the British bullying data; Chopra, Edelson and Saint, "Mentorship Malpractice", JAMA 2016 (PMID 27115263; citation verified, content [BK]: mentors who take credit, hoard mentees or are never available). A one-page written code should state:

1. **Role.** Adviser only. Not an examiner, employer, referee for a fee, or source of referrals.
2. **No money, no gifts, no sponsors.** Mentoring is free; no industry support.
3. **Authorship.** ICMJE criteria both ways [16]; the trainee who did the work is first author.
4. **Open selection.** Published criteria; places for women and for trainees outside Hyderabad; no selection by college or caste network (file 19 §7.1).
5. **Boundaries.** One-to-one meetings online or in open settings; messages on the group channel or by email, not late-night personal chat; no social-media contact initiated by the mentor.
6. **Confidentiality** of what trainees disclose, with a stated exception for patient safety.
7. **No patient identifiers** in any mentoring channel.
8. **A complaints route** to a named senior outside the programme (for example a CSI Telangana office-bearer), stated in writing.
9. **Trainees' own data.** Collect names, colleges and email only; DPDP obligations apply to this list from 13 May 2027 [16].
10. **Exit.** Either side can end the pairing without reasons; reviewed every 12 months.

#### 3.4 How mentors are recognised

- **By mentees' output:** case reports, abstracts and theses that acknowledge him; an alumni list after three years.
- **By formal status:** NBEMS PG-teacher and thesis-guide status (only as full-time faculty of an accredited department [16]); speaker entries on the TGMC CPD portal [16]; reviewer records [16].
- **By awards:** ACC distinguished awards for mentors and educators [BK; page not reachable]; CSI orations and state "best teacher" awards are senior-career and not applied for [07]. Teaching portfolios count in FACC and FESC files [16].
- Recognition follows about a decade of output (file 19 §6).

For his atlas: release **his own text and drawings under CC BY 4.0** (widest reuse, simplest for other teachers and for Wikimedia); mark every third-party image with its own licence; state that the licence does not extend to any patient-derived image unless the consent says so.

#### 4.4 Journal consent norms and de-identification

- **Elsevier** (publisher of Indian Heart Journal, IHJ Cardiovascular Reports and the JACC family): "Formal consents are not required for the use of entirely anonymized images", but for **case reports "informed consent is always required whether or not the images are anonymized"**; eye bars or blurring are not sufficient; "Written consents must be retained by the author"; India's Information Technology Act and privacy rules are named among applicable laws [V]. High.
- **ICMJE:** consent whenever anonymity is in doubt [11].
- **European Heart Journal family:** publisher page blocked [16]; not re-read.
- **De-identifying loops**, beyond the checklist in file 16: check **every frame** (banners can appear on only some); export cropped video instead of DICOM; remove overlays showing name, hospital number, date, operator and institution; remove audio; give age in bands and omit dates and city when the anatomy is rare, because rare anatomy with a date and a hospital can identify a person.

#### 4.5 Indian rules on patient images for teaching

- **Regulation 7.14 (2002):** a practitioner "shall not disclose the secrets of a patient that have been learnt in the exercise of his / her profession" except under court order, serious identified risk to a person or community, or notifiable disease [V].
- **Regulation 7.17:** "shall not publish photographs or case reports of his / her patients without their permission, in any medical or other journal in a manner by which their identity could be made out. If the identity is not to be disclosed, the consent is not needed" [V].
- **NMC Guidelines of 6 October 2026:** stricter for public communication; de-identified angiograms shown to the public are a "no" in file 11's reading; teaching doctors with consented, de-identified material is allowed [11].
- **DPDP Act:** research processing to prescribed standards is exempt; full obligations from 13 May 2027 [16]. Teaching is not a listed exemption, so identifiable images need notice and consent; truly anonymised images are outside the Act [BK, Medium; a lawyer should confirm].
- **Ownership:** records and images belong to the hospital where they were made (file 18). After he leaves Apollo he cannot take or use its images without written permission.

Consequence: the public atlas should be built from open datasets and his own drawings; teaching sets with real images should come from his own clinic with written consent, or be shown only in doctor-only sessions.

#### 5.3 UGC Professor of Practice

Guidelines (PDF dated 29 September 2022) [V]:

- Eligibility: "proven expertise in their specific profession or role with **at least 15 years of service/experience, preferably at a senior level**"; no formal qualification or publications required.
- Cap: "should not exceed 10% of the sanctioned posts"; the positions are outside sanctioned posts.
- "Not open for those in teaching profession- serving or retired."
- Three categories: industry-funded, institution-funded, **honorary**.
- Selection: nomination to the Vice-Chancellor or Director with a biodata and "a brief write-up about their potential contribution"; a committee of two senior professors and one external member.
- Tenure: one year at first; at most three years, four in exceptional cases.
- The UGC portal shows about 21,017 registered experts and 571 institutions [V].

Applied to him: registered in 2012, MD 2016, DM 2020. On the strictest count he reaches 15 years of professional experience in 2027, but not "at a senior level". ## Risks & things to avoid

1. **Showing real angiograms or echo loops on a public channel.** File 11 reads this as barred; a CC licence does not cure it. Public uploads use open-licence or drawn material only.
2. **Assuming "open" means "consented".** ARCADE has no ethics statement on its record; CC BY figures were consented for one publication. Use non-identifiable frames and cite.
3. **Reusing restricted sets.** EchoNet-Dynamic and ECG Wave-Maven forbid redistribution; LITFL and ECGpedia forbid commercial reuse. A monetised channel may count as commercial.
4. **Taking Apollo images when he leaves.** The hospital owns them.
5. **Pharma-run trainee platforms.** India Coronary Conquest is run by a drug company. Faculty roles there fall under UCPMP and regulation 6.8 (file 16) and would weaken the "no sponsor" position.
6. **Selling mentoring.** A local paid mentorship exists at Rs 2,50,000. His value is that his is free, open and governed by a published code.
7. **A title without substance.** "Adjunct" or "Professor of Practice" on a profile before any joint work reads as branding and may be treated as a qualification claim under the NMC guidelines. Do the project first; state the title exactly as conferred.
8. **Power and boundaries.** One in nine UK cardiology trainees reported bullying, mostly by consultants. Private one-to-one channels with juniors, gifts and unearned authorship are the common failures.
9. **Naming himself PI on a grant without an employer institution.** ICMR will reject it; an NGO route needs recognition documents first.
10. **Counting subscribers as reach.** The exam platforms' numbers cover all specialties; the cardiology trainee population is about 3,200 nationally. A few hundred regular viewers is most of the Telugu-state cohort.
