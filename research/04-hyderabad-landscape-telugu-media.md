# The local playing field: Hyderabad / Telangana cardiology visibility and the Telugu + English health-media ecosystem

**Research date:** 12 November 2026. **Method note:** the shared web-search budget ran out after about 25 searches, so most of this report rests on direct page fetches (news sites, hospital sites, Wikipedia, vidIQ channel-statistics pages read on 10–12 Nov 2026). Blocked in this session: apollohospitals.com (403), thehindu.com, newindianexpress.com, sakshi.com. Anything I could not open is marked "could not verify".

## Executive summary

1. **Hospital brands own the megaphone, individual cardiologists do not.** CARE (1.2M), Apollo (1.12M) and Yashoda (1.11M) each have over a million YouTube subscribers; the one Hyderabad cardiologist-owned channel I could verify (Dr C Raghu, Yashoda) has 16,900 after 11 years and 326 videos.
2. **Telugu health video is dominated by aggregator channels** (PlayEven/"Diabetes Telugu" 2.07M, SumanTV Health Care 1.43M, QubeTV Health 918K, ETV Life 980K) whose top heart content is "foods that clean arteries"-style, mostly not from cardiologists. A named, evidence-based Telugu cardiologist voice is an open slot.
3. **Hospital PR in Hyderabad runs on a few repeatable story types**: "first/youngest" procedures, "young techie saved", World Heart Day walks and mass CPR, clinic launches. In 2026 the Financial District itself was contested on World Heart Day by Star (Nanakramguda "Heart Dharna") and Medicover (new Valve Clinic).
4. **The audience case is strong and local**: 37% of Telangana deaths fall in the 15–59 age band (highest among big states); 84% of 345 Hyderabad IT employees had fatty liver, 76.5% high LDL, 72% sit over 8 hours a day.
5. **Winnable white space for him**: preventive cardiology for IT-corridor professionals, plain Telugu explainers of tests and procedures, women's heart health, heart failure and life after a stent, delivered through reporters' quotes, employer talks and short video rather than paid TV slots.

### 3. How hospital PR works in Hyderabad, and how a consultant can ride it

**Recurring story types (all with 2025–26 examples).**
- *"First / youngest / rare"*: Apollo Jubilee Hills "youngest valve-in-valve TAVI in India" (Aug 2025); CARE "Andhra Pradesh's first bi-plane cath lab" (Oct 2026); older: AIG "first in South India" cryoballoon ablation (2021).
- *"Young professional saved"*: KIMS Kondapur's 29-year-old techie (Jun 2025). Single-source, patient unnamed, dramatic headline.
- *World Heart Day (29 Sep)*: in 2026 Yashoda ran a "1 Minute Heart Challenge" with the TPCC president and the city police commissioner; CARE held three walks; Star ran "Heart Dharna" with HMDA at Nanakramguda Circle and trained 5,000 in CPR; Medicover Financial District launched a Valve Clinic; Renova held a 3K walk; Kamineni ran a session on young-adult risk.
- *Academic showcase*: Yashoda's international live interventional workshop (Jun 2025).
- *District outreach press meet*: a Yashoda cardiologist addressing reporters in Kothagudem about a fortnightly clinic (2023, stale but typical).
- *Awards and listings*: Hybiz TV Healthcare Awards (45 specialty categories), Outlook and siliconindia "doctors from Hyderabad" features. Selection criteria are not transparent.

**Distribution mechanics.** Releases go to city reporters and are also pushed through paid wires: Apollo's TAVI release ran under "ANI Press Releases" sourced from PNN and carried an advertorial disclaimer. Earned pickup comes from PTI, UNI and IANS. Hospitals log clippings by paper.

**Agencies.** I could not verify which agencies serve Apollo, Yashoda, KIMS or CARE; these chains have in-house marketing and digital heads (CARE names a Chief of Sales & Marketing and a Head of Digital Strategy). Hyderabad-linked healthcare PR firms that surfaced: Flags Communications (FMS Dental mandate, 2024), Public Media Solution (Germanten Hospitals), Ajantha Public Relations. Confidence: Low-Medium.

**How an individual Apollo consultant plugs in (practitioner inference, not sourced policy).**
1. The unit marketing/PR manager needs a steady supply of cases, quotes and faces. A consultant who hands over a clean, consented, de-identified case note with one clear "why it matters" line gets used.
2. Reporters want a doctor who answers within two hours, in plain language, in Telugu and English. Being on the PR desk's "available for comment" list for cardiology is the single most useful step.
3. Hospital channels (Apollo's 1.12M-subscriber YouTube, unit Instagram) need doctor-led videos. Volunteering for Telugu explainers gets him reach he cannot build alone in year one.
4. Personal channels are evidently tolerated at Apollo: Dr Gokhale runs his own site, X, Instagram and YouTube. What Apollo's written policy requires (approvals, branding, patient content) was not obtainable and must be asked directly.

### 4. Audience data

**Language and platforms.**
- Telangana: about 38.5M people (2025 estimate), 49% urban; Telugu 75.5%, Urdu 12.3% (2011 census). Hyderabad city: Telugu 58.2%, Urdu 29.5%, Hindi 4.9% (2011). High, but dated.
- Hyderabad IT/ITES employment: about 778,000 across 1,500+ companies (2022). The Financial District, Gachibowli, HITEC City, Kondapur belt is where they work. Medium (Wikipedia citing Mint).
- Platform use for news among English-speaking online Indians (Reuters Institute DNR 2025): YouTube 55%, WhatsApp 46%, Instagram 37%, Facebook 36%. Not Telangana-specific. State-level platform data (IAMAI/Kantar): could not retrieve.

**Disease burden and the "young heart attack" concern.**
- Ischaemic heart disease is the top cause of disease burden in Telangana (10.3% of DALYs); cardiovascular disease caused 38.1% of deaths at ages 40–69 and 13.5% at ages 15–39 (ICMR/PHFI/IHME, 2016 data, stale but the only state profile).
- 37% of all Telangana deaths occur at ages 15–59, against 29.8% nationally, the highest among big states (SRS 2024, reported May 2026).
- Medically certified deaths in 2024: 736 acute MI and 1,645 ischaemic heart disease deaths in Telangana, with only 38.3% of deaths certified (MCCD 2024).
- NFHS-6 (2023–24) as reported by Telangana Today: hypertension 28.2% of men and 24.2% of women; high blood sugar 24.5% of men and 19.6% of women; 50.4% of Hyderabad women aged 15–49 overweight or obese.
- Apollo "Health of the Nation 2026": 81.3% of people screened in Hyderabad overweight or obese; 25.3% diabetic (screened population, not a random sample).
- Hyderabad IT employees (University of Hyderabad + AIG, *Scientific Reports*, Mar 2025, n=345): 84% fatty liver, 70.7% obese, 76.5% high LDL, 37.8% elevated BP, 34% metabolic syndrome, 72% sit more than 8 hours, 70% sleep under 7 hours.
- Heart failure costs in Hyderabad (ICMR-funded, NIMS site, *Global Heart* Apr 2026): 68% uninsured; mean hospitalisation ₹1.19 lakh; 17.7% sold assets or took informal loans.
- Indian risk scores: a GB Pant study of about 5,000 MI patients found 80% had been classed low or moderate risk by Western calculators (reported Apr 2026).
- National cardiologist survey (Tata AIG, Oct 2025, about 300 specialists): 78% say patients dismiss chest pain as acidity; 16% say women's symptoms are overlooked.

**Post-COVID sudden-death anxiety.** An AIIMS autopsy series (2,214 autopsies, May 2023–Apr 2024, *IJMR*) found coronary disease behind most young sudden cardiac deaths, 57% of young victims smoked, and no link to COVID vaccination. Local triggers keep the fear alive: on-court collapses in Hyderabad in July 2024 and August 2025 were widely covered.

**What people look for (proxy, since Google Trends data could not be pulled).** The best-performing heart videos on Telugu channels are: heart attack early symptoms in Telugu (215K), "foods that clean arteries" (1.06M), a 3-step prevention protocol (99K), cardiac pain versus neck/cervical pain (750K), CT angiogram explained (78K), angioplasty versus bypass (25K), young-age heart attack. Demand clusters around symptoms, "is this pain cardiac", tests, stent versus bypass, and food.

**Misinformation.** Telangana Medical Council identified 35 fake "doctors" with 1–3 lakh followers each selling diabetes reversal and detox advice (Dec 2024), found over 117 quacks via decoys in 2025, with about 500 police cases by Aug 2025. Naturopathy and millet-cure content draws seven-figure views. NewsMeter's cardiologist explainer lists the live myths: young people don't get heart disease, it's a men's problem, a normal ECG clears you, gym workouts cause heart attacks, pink salt is heart-healthy.

This alone puts him in the PTI/Telangana Today/Hans India flow that currently goes to Jubilee Hills and Secunderabad colleagues.
2. **Fix the name and listing mess (one-off, 2–3 hours, friend can do it).** Pick one spelling for public use, and correct third-party listings that show Jubilee Hills or Star Hospital and the Citizens page that shows "4 years". Confirm whether the Citizens affiliation is current.
3. **Own one theme: "heart health for people who sit for a living".** Build a 20-minute employer talk plus a one-page risk checklist using the Hyderabad IT data, and deliver it at 1–2 Financial District companies a month through Apollo's corporate team (2–3 hours a month). Talks suit an introvert better than reels and each one yields photos, a LinkedIn post and a possible reporter note.
4. **Record Telugu explainers for Apollo's channel first (1 hour a fortnight, batch 3–4 clips).** Topics in order of proven demand: chest pain versus acidity or neck pain; heart attack warning signs; CT angiogram and calcium score; stent versus bypass; Lp(a) and family history; women's symptoms. Repost on a personal channel only if policy allows.
5. **Introduce himself to four reporters with data, not a pitch (one-off, 2 hours; then 15 min a month).** M. Sai Gopal (Telangana Today), Sumit Jha (South First), the Deccan Chronicle health desk, and NewsMeter's health fact-check editor. Offer a bylined myth-buster to NewsMeter before World Heart Day.
6. **Plan World Heart Day 2027 in June, not September (one-off, 4–6 hours across three months).** The corridor was taken in 2026 by Star and Medicover. A distinct angle: free risk-score-plus-Lp(a) screening day for IT employees or a CPR session with TGCSI, with Apollo PR handling the release.
7. **Join or become active in the CSI Telangana chapter (TGCSI) (1–2 hours a month).** It co-brands public CPR events and is the peer-reputation route that feeds invitations to speak.
8. **Send Apollo PR one de-identified, consented case a quarter (30 min each).** Prefer "what the patient nearly missed" stories over "first" claims.
9. **Later (month 6+): one guest appearance on a doctor-interview podcast** (TGV Telugu, Yashoda-style formats) and an Eenadu Sukhibhava or Andhra Jyothy quote via Apollo's Telugu PR contact. Do not pay for slots.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Paid programme slots and purchased awards/listings.** Low trust, and advertising by doctors is restricted under medical-council ethics rules (a separate research strand should confirm the current NMC position). A panel at the Dakshin Health Summit (Nov 2025) said councils have acted against doctor-influencers for endorsements and paid collaborations.
- **"First in Telangana / youngest in India" claims.** These are hospital claims that reporters print unverified; a rival can contradict them and the named doctor carries the embarrassment. Let the hospital make such claims, not him.
- **Patient stories without written consent and hospital clearance**, including "techie saved" narratives and before/after imagery.
- **Commenting beyond evidence on vaccine-related sudden death, supplements, or "reversal" diets.** State what studies show (the AIIMS autopsy series) and stop there.
- **Aggregator channels with a "business promotions" model.** Appearing next to artery-cleaning-food videos dilutes credibility.
- **Bypassing Apollo's communications team.** Personal press contact without telling the hospital PR lead is the fastest way to lose their support.
- **Trolling.** Hyderabad doctors report abuse after posting and little police follow-up. Keep comments moderated and let the friend handle replies.
- **Using screened-population numbers as population prevalence** (Apollo's 81.3%, the 84% IT figure from 345 people). Quote them with the sample stated.
