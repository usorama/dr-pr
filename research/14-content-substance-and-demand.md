# The substance: what Dr. Avinash should teach — public demand, Indian evidence base, myths, craft, signature point of view and a 52-week calendar

**Research date:** 12 November 2026. **Round 2, topic 14.**

## Executive summary

1. **Demand is measurable and it is basic.** Google/YouTube autosuggest (India; English, Hindi, Telugu), Google Trends for Telangana and Telugu YouTube view counts all point to the same questions: heart-attack symptoms, "gas or heart", what to do at home, cholesterol and BP "how much should it be", what an ECG/echo/TMT/angiogram is and costs, stent vs bypass, and life after a stent. Telugu users literally type "స్టెంట్ తర్వాత ఎన్ని రోజులు విశ్రాంతి" and "గ్యాస్ నొప్పి మరియు గుండెపోటు మధ్య తేడా".
2. **The Telugu supply is dominated by non-cardiologists.** Naturopathy channels (Dr. Manthena Official 37.9 lakh subscribers; Good Health 31.4 lakh; Health Mantra 24.8 lakh) own "cholesterol", "BP" and "heart cleaning" with multi-million-view videos. Two Telugu cardiologist channels have real scale (Dr M S S Mukharjee/Pulse Heart 4.95 lakh; Dr. Movva Srinivas 3.18 lakh). The most-viewed titles on one lean on food claims and alarm; the other's top video is a plain angiogram explainer with 22 lakh views, which shows calm procedure content can travel. A numbers-and-sources Telugu voice is still open.
3. **There is a strong, citable Indian evidence base** for almost every topic: first heart attack about 6 years earlier in South Asians (INTERHEART), 84% fatty liver in 345 Hyderabad IT employees, only 21% BP control in IHCI districts (incl. Telangana), only 27% of Indian CAD patients at LDL ≤50 mg/dL, median 5–6 hours from symptoms to hospital, women treated less, 0.26% survival after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in a Telangana district-hospital series, and ICMR/AIIMS studies finding no vaccine link to sudden deaths. Two results from **last week** (AHA, 7 Nov 2026) change the script: the Lp(a) drug pelacarsen failed (Lp(a)HORIZON), and the Indian Yoga-CaRe HF trial improved quality of life.
4. **Recommended signature point of view:** lead with **"After the stent — the second half of treatment nobody explains"** (least crowded, closest to his daily work, highest unmet Telugu demand), run **"The first 60 minutes"** as the community flagship, and use **"Know your numbers"** as the standing prevention format for the IT corridor. Treat "heart attacks at 35" as a news-peg topic, not an identity; hold "when you do not need a stent" as a tone ("right test, right time") until credibility is banked.
5. **Deliverables in this file:** 60 ranked questions with Telugu phrasings for the top 20; an evidence table with primary sources; a 15-item misinformation map with fact-first lines; craft rules (plain language, numbers, analogies, Telugu register); five point-of-view candidates scored; six pillars, a 52-week calendar (English + Telugu, public / peer / community columns) and 20 evergreen explainer assets; a peer-facing plan with the 2026–27 trial and guideline pipeline.

**Method and limits.** The shared search budget allowed 12 web searches. Everything else came from direct fetches: Google and YouTube autosuggest endpoints (about 190 seed phrases, India locale, English/Hindi/Telugu), Google Trends related-queries for Telangana (last 12 months), YouTube search results sorted by view count, Europe PMC records and abstracts for every paper cited, the NFHS-5 Telangana state report PDF, the Debunking Handbook PDF, NHS/WHO/CDC pages and conference coverage. **Autosuggest and Trends give order and relative interest, not search volumes.** Blocked or not retrievable: Reddit r/india (403), Practo and Lybrate question pages (404), Quora (not attempted), heart.org cough-CPR page (403), nature.com (paywall redirect), ICMR-NIN dietary guidelines PDF (404). Confidence tags: **High** = primary source read this session; **Medium** = abstract/secondary source or search-result summary; **Low** = from memory, not re-checked.

#### 1e. Published studies on health-information seeking (India)

- Online cardiovascular content is unreliable at scale. A 2026 scoping review of 22 studies found misinformation on smoking, nutrition, BP, alcohol and diabetes; about one-third of YouTube myocardial-infarction videos contained inaccuracies and posts discouraging salt reduction reached 1.5 million followers (Bhandari et al., BMJ Public Health 2026). (High, abstract)
- An Indian-authored audit of YouTube heart-attack videos found only 33% uploaded by doctors and 7.8% by healthcare organisations; 15.7% carried promotional material (Holge et al., Cureus 2023). (High, abstract)
- No representative study of where Telugu speakers get heart-health information was found. (Gap)

#### 3b. How to debunk without amplifying: what the evidence says

| Principle and evidence | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Use fact, warning, fallacy, fact. Lead with a clear, sticky fact; warn that a myth is coming and state it once; explain why it misleads; end on the fact. This is the evidence-based form of the "truth sandwich" | Debunking Handbook 2020 (22 authors), DOI 10.17910/b7.1182; PDF read for these sections | High |
| Do not fear backfire. "Backfire effects are not as common as we used to think"; familiarity backfire tests "have come up empty" | Debunking Handbook 2020; Swire-Thompson et al., J Appl Res Mem Cogn 2020 | High |
| Pick battles. "If a myth is not spreading widely… the less said about an unknown myth the better." Address only myths with traction (the table above gives the evidence of traction) | Debunking Handbook 2020 | High |
| Prebunk. Short videos that explain manipulation techniques (emotional language, false dichotomies, and so on) improved people's ability to spot them in six trials (n = 6,464) and a YouTube field study (n = 22,632) | Roozenbeek et al., Science Advances 2022 | High |
| Give the alternative explanation, not just "false". A correction that leaves a gap is filled by the myth again (continued influence effect) | Debunking Handbook 2020 | High |
| Do not attack people. Ridicule polarises; "your talking points, not theirs" | Debunking Handbook 2020 | High |
| Misinformation has clinical cost: negative statin news was followed by stopping and more heart attacks; follow-up under alternative medicine doubled the odds of a second heart attack (AOR 2.04) | See 2e | High |

**Working rules for him**

1. Title with the fact or the question, never the myth as a claim. "కొలెస్ట్రాల్ మందులు: సైడ్ ఎఫెక్ట్స్ నిజమెంత?" not "Statins are poison?".
2. State the myth once, in neutral words, without naming or showing the person or channel that spreads it.
3. Explain the trick: "one person's story", "a lab finding is not a heart attack prevented", "natural is not the same as tested".
4. Offer something to do instead (the number to check, the question to ask).
5. One prebunk segment a quarter: "five signs a heart video is selling you something" (promises a cure, a time limit such as "7 days", "doctors hide this", a product link, no numbers).
6. Respect for traditional food and practice; firmness about stopping medicines. Yoga has trial evidence for quality of life; herbs do not replace a statin.

**Telugu register (practitioner judgement, grounded in the search data in 1d; Medium)**

- Speak conversational Telugu with the English medical nouns people already use (BP, sugar, ECG, stent, block). Avoid Sanskritised textbook terms as the main word: say "బీపీ" first, "రక్తపోటు" once.
- Use the neutral "మీరు". Avoid both the lecturing tone and slang.
- Telangana and Andhra usage differ in everyday words. Prefer forms common to both, and test scripts with one listener from each region and one person over 60.
- There is no validated readability formula for Telugu that this research could find. Use listener testing instead: can a Class 6 student repeat the main message?

**Visuals.** Icon arrays for risk; one labelled diagram per video; real equipment (a stent on a fingertip, a BP cuff) rather than stock animation; angiogram images only from published, de-identified, consented material and only for peer channels (file 02).

#### A. "After the stent — the second half of treatment nobody explains"

- **Evidence base.** Only 27% of treated Indian CAD patients reach LDL ≤50 and therapy is stepped up in only 37% of the rest; adherence 56%; follow-up under alternative medicine doubles the odds of a second heart attack; one rehab place per 360 patients; Yoga-CaRe and Yoga-CaRe HF give an Indian, low-cost rehab option; negative statin news leads to stopping and more events.
- **Audience.** Patients with stents or bypass and their families (the adult children in the IT corridor who manage a parent's care); referring physicians and GPs who see these patients between cardiology visits.
- **Emotional hook.** Relief turning into drift: "ఆపరేషన్ అయిపోయింది" followed by stopped tablets at month six. The line: "The stent fixes two centimetres. The next ten years depend on five tablets and four numbers."
- **Differentiation.** Telugu supply is thin (best video 5.7 lakh views against clear search demand: "స్టెంట్ తర్వాత ఎన్ని రోజులు విశ్రాంతి", "గుండె స్టెంట్ జీవితకాలం", "after stent surgery precautions in telugu"). Hospitals market the procedure; almost no one owns the year after it. It also gives GPs a reason to follow and refer.
- **Risks.** Could look like procedure marketing if he shows cases or outcomes; any personal medication advice in comments; drug brand names (use generic names; people search "Ecosprin", so the brand can appear only as a search term if the compliance reviewer allows). Mitigation: no patient stories, no "my results", every video ends "your own doctor decides your dose".

#### B. "The first 60 minutes — what to do before you reach a hospital"

- **Evidence base.** Median 5–6 hours from symptoms to hospital while door-to-needle is 15 minutes; women and people who think "gas" come later; cardiac-arrest survival 0.26% in a Telangana district-hospital series; bystander CPR under 10%; a French registry saw survival triple as bystander CPR became routine.
- **Audience.** Everyone; especially spouses and adult children, apartment associations, security staff, cab drivers, office floor wardens.
- **Emotional hook.** "ఆ రాత్రి గ్యాస్ అనుకున్నాం." The gap between a treatable event and a death is a decision made at home.
- **Differentiation.** Hospitals run one-day CPR events; a named cardiologist who teaches a fixed five-step drill all year, in Telugu, with a wallet card and a count of people trained, is different. He does STEMI work, so it is authentic. Connects to the CPR initiative in file 09.
- **Risks.** Aspirin advice (allergy, bleeding, stroke confusion) needs careful wording and legal review; must say "nearest hospital with a cath lab", never his own; emergency numbers must be current. Crowded on World Heart Day, so run it the other 51 weeks.

#### C. "Know your numbers — six numbers every Telugu family should know"

- **Proposed six.** BP; LDL cholesterol; HbA1c (or fasting sugar); waist; Lp(a), once in a lifetime; and 108. Five body numbers and one phone number. Family history is the standing seventh question ("father or brother before 55, mother or sister before 60", the CSI definition).
- **Evidence base.** CSI 2024 targets (LDL <100 / <70 / <55; Lp(a) <50); IHCI 21% BP control; INDIAB prevalence; NFHS-5 Telangana; the Hyderabad IT study (76.5% high LDL, 84% fatty liver); 1 in 4 South Asians with high Lp(a).
- **Audience.** IT-corridor professionals aged 28–45 and, through them, parents. Corporate HR and wellness leads on LinkedIn.
- **Emotional hook.** "You know your CTC, your credit score and your steps. Do you know your LDL?"
- **Differentiation.** Many doctors say "get tested"; few give Indian targets, a one-page card and a rule for what to do with each result. The search data show people ask exactly "ఎంత ఉండాలి?".
- **Risks.** Over-testing and anxiety in the young; lab-package marketing by association; Lp(a) messaging after the failed pelacarsen trial (say: test once, there is no Lp(a) drug yet, so treat everything else harder). Guidelines differ on BP targets; present ranges and "ask your doctor for your target".

#### D. "Heart attacks at 35 — the IT-corridor problem and what actually prevents it"

- **Evidence base.** INTERHEART age gap; NORIN-STEMI (a third of STEMI at ≤50; tobacco and inactivity dominate); AIIMS autopsies; ICMR risk factors; Hyderabad IT study; night-shift meta-analysis.
- **Audience.** Techies, HR, journalists after each sudden-death story.
- **Emotional hook.** Fear and "people like me".
- **Differentiation.** Low: this is the single most-quoted topic among Hyderabad cardiologists (file 04). His angle would have to be numbers and calm: "fear is not a plan".
- **Risks.** Sensationalism; implying a rising trend without incidence data (registries show proportions, not rates); commenting on named deaths (do not); gym and supplement claims without Indian data.
- **Verdict.** Use as the reactive, news-peg topic and as the entry point to C, not as his identity.

#### E. "Honest cardiology — when you do not need an angiogram or stent"

- **Evidence base.** ISCHEMIA (5,179 patients with stable disease: invasive strategy did not reduce events or death over 3.2 years); ORBITA-2 (PCI does relieve angina better than placebo); CorCal Outcomes at ESC 2026 (calcium-guided statin decisions did not meet non-inferiority, per ACC coverage); search demand "is angioplasty necessary after angiography".
- **Audience.** Anxious patients told they need a stent; second-opinion seekers; GPs.
- **Emotional hook.** Distrust: "నిజంగా అవసరమా?"
- **Differentiation.** Very high. Almost no interventional cardiologist says it publicly.
- **Risks.** Highest of the five. Peer and employer backlash; quotes clipped by "no stent" channels ("cardiologist admits…"); a patient with a real heart attack delaying because of it. NMC rules also bar disparaging other practitioners.
- **Verdict.** Do not headline it. Carry it as a tone inside A, B and C: "right test, right time; in a heart attack a stent saves life and minutes matter; in stable disease, medicines come first and the stent is for symptoms." Revisit as a named series after 9–12 months, paired with peer-reviewed writing.

**Recommended positioning (one sentence):** "The cardiologist who explains the whole journey in plain Telugu and plain numbers: before the heart attack, the first hour, and the years after the stent." A leads, B is the community flagship, C is the standing prevention format.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Personal medical advice in public.** Never give doses or "you should stop/start" to an individual in comments. Standard line: "మీ డాక్టర్‌తో మాట్లాడండి; ఇది సాధారణ సమాచారం."
- **Aspirin and emergency advice.** "Chew aspirin" has exceptions (allergy, active bleeding, possible stroke). Word it once, have it reviewed, and reuse the same wording everywhere.
- **Brand names.** People search for brands ("Ecosprin 75", "sorbitrate"). Speak in generic names; do not show packs. Ask the compliance reviewer whether brand names may appear as search keywords.
- **Fear as a growth tactic.** The best-performing Telugu titles use alarm and miracle food claims. Copying that style would win views and lose the differentiation and possibly breach the NMC guideline on misleading claims.
- **Naming or showing misinformation sources.** Do not name channels or individuals; do not stitch or duet their clips. It amplifies them and risks a professional-conduct complaint.
- **Overstating the evidence.** The IT study shows risk factors, not heart attacks. Registries show proportions of young patients, not rising incidence. Yoga trials improved quality of life, not events. Say exactly that.
- **Lp(a) and calcium-score enthusiasm.** After Lp(a)HORIZON and CorCal Outcomes, avoid implying that a test leads to a specific treatment. Avoid any link with lab packages.
- **"You do not need a stent" clips.** Any sentence on overuse must carry its pair in the same breath: "in a heart attack, a stent saves life".
- **Sudden deaths in the news.** Never speculate on a named person. Release the pre-approved general explainer.
- **Analogy drift.** The plumbing picture feeds the "cleaning" myths. Always follow an analogy with the literal fact.
- **Employer neutrality.** "Nearest hospital with a cath lab", never the name of his own; no hospital logo on cards (file 02).
- **Telugu errors.** A wrong word in a medical instruction is a safety problem. Every Telugu script and card gets a second native reader, preferably a doctor.
- **Stale numbers.** CREATE (2001–05) and the rehab audit (2016–17) are old. Say the year when quoting them.
