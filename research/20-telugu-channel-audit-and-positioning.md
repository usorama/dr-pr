# The Telugu heart-health field, audited: who holds which position, and the positions nobody holds

**Research date:** 12 November 2026. **Round 3, topic 20.**

## Executive summary

1. **The Movva Srinivas conflict is settled: both earlier reports were partly right.** A channel named "Dr. Movva Srinivas" (youtube.com/@Dr.MovvaSrinivas) exists with **318K subscribers, 1,420 videos, 72.4M views, created 6 Feb 2023** (read on YouTube 12 Nov 2026; vidIQ agrees). He is a real cardiologist listed at Pulse Heart Hospital, Miyapur. But the channel behaves like a media-network channel under his name (SumanTV and PlayEven tags, re-uploads, a general-health topic mix), and its growth has stalled at 1–3K subscribers a month.
2. **Only one Telugu cardiologist is a true incumbent: Dr M S S Mukharjee (495K, +46K a month).** He has touched every topic we planned (stents, CPR, AI, ApoB, guidelines). No topic is unoccupied. What is unoccupied is a **form**: a sequenced course, written sources, a registration number, independence from a hospital brand, text, and peer teaching.
3. **The naturopathy cluster is far larger and sets the frame.** A sample of about 420 heart, BP and cholesterol videos on three Manthena channels holds at least **103M views**; 30 of them exceed 1M each. The same "clean the vessels" and "BP 200, goodbye" title frames now appear on the cardiologist-named Movva channel.
4. **Four positions are open and fit his goals:** (a) "After the stent" as a numbered Telugu course; (b) "First 60 minutes" as a trainer, not a commentator; (c) a sourced, numbered, signed reference in Telugu cards and English text; (d) an open peer conference with a published AI method. The nearest rivals are structurally unlikely to move into any of them.
5. **Format: one Telugu anchor explainer a week (4–7 minutes, in a numbered series), one English written post a week, one Telugu card per anchor, one live session a month.** About 3 hours a week of his time. He should not compete on daily Shorts, news-trigger commentary, food tips, the aggregator interview circuit or subscriber counts.

**Method and limits.** I ran 12 web searches (the budget) and about 170 direct fetches. YouTube was read directly: channel About pages, Videos and Shorts tabs sorted by latest and by popular, playlists, in-channel search and site search, all on 12 Nov 2026. Titles were served with YouTube's English auto-translation in places, so some English titles below are YouTube's wording, not the uploader's. I read titles, descriptions and counts; **I did not watch videos**, so tone and evidence style are judged from titles and descriptions only. Instagram returned a login wall; Sakshi and Andhra Jyothy health pages were unreachable; socialveins returned 503; x.com and LinkedIn were not readable. Topic counts are hand classifications of titles and are approximate (a title can fit two classes). Confidence: **High** = read on the primary page this session; **Medium** = one secondary source or an inference from several primary observations; **Low** = thin evidence or recalled.

The pattern has five claims: vessels can be cleaned; blocks can be removed by food; BP can be normalised quickly without tablets; natural blood thinners exist; and "zero cholesterol" foods are safe in any amount. The fact-first replies and their sources are already drafted in file 14 (the 15-item misinformation map), so they are not repeated here. The frame has also crossed into cardiologist-branded content (2c), and aggregators repeat it with other doctors: iDream Super Doctor's "Top 5 Foods to Clean Your Arteries that Can Prevent a Heart Attack" has two uploads at 630K and 660K.

#### 3c. How large is the correction opportunity?

- **Large in audience, small in direct conversion.** More than 100M views of heart claims exist. A calm, sourced Telugu answer to each of the five claims has almost no competition from a cardiologist.
- **Do it as "what works, in numbers", not as rebuttal.** Three reasons. First, the Andhra Pradesh government appointed Manthena Satyanarayana Raju its adviser for yoga and naturopathy for two years on 29 Dec 2025, so he is an official figure with a devoted following. Second, Dr Mukharjee has already made the named rebuttal ("The Truth Behind Veeramachaneni Diet", 408K; a second at 296K). Third, a doctors' association is already in court (3d). A named fight would define him by an opponent.
- **Search-led titles carry the correction to the right viewer.** Someone typing "BP తగ్గాలంటే" (to lower BP) or "blocks కరగాలంటే" (to melt blocks) should find his card and explainer next to the claim.

**The four unoccupied positions**

No topic is untouched by Dr Mukharjee. Each position below is defined by form and proof. The "why unlikely" entries are my judgement from the incumbents' observed incentives (Medium).

**Position 1. "After the stent": the numbered Telugu course for the second half of treatment**

- **Nearest competitor:** Dr Mukharjee (about 12 scattered Shorts); the Movva channel (one 14-minute video); Dr Raghu (a 35-video heart-failure playlist, mostly English).
- **Why they are unlikely to move:** Mukharjee's growth comes from six novelty Shorts a week; a 10-part course has little novelty per item, and a hospital owner's channel gains more from new patients than from discharged ones. The Movva channels are run for general-health volume. Dr Raghu's reach is English-first.
- **Proof he needs:** guideline citations on every part; a named second reviewer; Telugu handouts tested with real listeners (file 18's verification method); within 12 months, his own audited numbers (for example, the share of his stent patients at LDL target at six months) presented at a conference, not in public posts.
- **First 10 pieces (Telugu anchors of 4–7 minutes, each with a one-page card):**
  1. The first 48 hours at home: the wrist or groin site, what is normal, when to call.
  2. The two blood thinners: what each does, how long, and why nobody stops them alone.
  3. The statin and your LDL number: the target, and the test at six weeks.
  4. Weeks 1 to 6: walking, stairs, driving, work, sex, travel.
  5. Food after a stent: what matters (salt, oil quantity, sugar) and what "pathyam" gets wrong.
  6. Chest pain again: what to do in the first 60 minutes (the bridge to Position 2).
  7. Can a stent block again? The numbers, and what lowers the risk.
  8. BP, sugar and tobacco: the three targets on one card.
  9. The follow-up calendar: which tests at 6 weeks, 6 months and 12 months, and which are not needed.
  10. The family episode: what the spouse and children should know; low mood after a heart attack; rehabilitation at home.

**Position 2. "First 60 minutes": the trainer, not the commentator**

- **Nearest competitor:** Dr Mukharjee (six CPR items reacting to events; a four-video "CPR Demo" playlist); CARE Hospitals (one Short, 2.3M); Dr Ramaka Srinivas in Warangal (mass CPR training and a registry, little online presence); Apollo's Billion Hearts Beating (file 13).
- **Why they are unlikely to move:** it needs repeated small-group work off screen, which earns no views; hospital programmes cluster around World Heart Day. Dr Ramaka Srinivas is a possible ally, not a rival.
- **Proof he needs:** a recognised BLS instructor credential; a count of people trained with a before-and-after skills check; a link to an existing registry or a short published evaluation.
- **First 10 pieces:**
  1. A four-minute Telugu film: chest pain, the five steps in the first hour.
  2. A fridge card (Telugu and English) with the steps, 108, and a blank for the nearest cath-lab hospital.
  3. Hands-only CPR on a manikin, two minutes, Telugu.
  4. The first native Telugu AED explainer.
  5. "Gas or heart?" as a decision card.
  6. Aspirin: who should chew one, who should not.
  7. A 60-minute session kit: slides, manikin plan, a five-question quiz before and after.
  8. The first session in one apartment community or IT campus; publish the counts and quiz change.
  9. An English note for HR and facility heads: "what an office needs for the first 60 minutes".
  10. A one-page evaluation of sessions 1–5, submitted to the CSI Telangana chapter or a journal letter.

**Position 3. The sourced, numbered, signed reference ("Know your numbers" in Telugu cards and English text)**

- **Nearest competitor:** Dr Sudhir Kumar (English X threads; a neurologist who already comments on heart risk); Dr Mukharjee (names studies aloud).
- **Why they are unlikely to move:** Dr Sudhir Kumar writes in English and outside cardiology. Mukharjee's unit is a two-minute video with a fixed description. Aggregators live on titles.
- **Proof he needs:** every item carries sources, a review date, his registration number and a visible corrections note; reporters start quoting it (file 13's yardstick: four press pick-ups in 12 months).
- **First 10 pieces:**
  1. "How I source what I say": one page on sources, review dates, corrections and his registration number.
  2. The numbers card: BP, LDL, HbA1c, waist, with Indian targets and sources.
  3. "LDL: one number, three targets."
  4. "Home BP: how to measure, and which number counts" (demand is proven: a BP-machine video drew 827K in three weeks on the Movva channel).
  5. "What food, salt, weight and walking can do for BP, in mmHg" (the calm answer to "190 to 110 with a pinch", naming no one).
  6. "Can blocks be cleaned? What actually changes plaque, in numbers."
  7. "Foods called blood thinners, and the tablets after a stent: what trials show."
  8. English post for the IT corridor: "Your annual health check: the numbers that matter and the ones that do not."
  9. "Which heart-risk score for Indians?" (news peg: file 13 notes a neurologist was quoted on this in April 2026).
  10. A quarterly "what changed" note (for example, the negative Lp(a) trial recorded in file 14).

**Position 4. The open peer conference with a published AI method**

- **Nearest competitor:** Dr Raghu (an annual CME with AI talks, uploaded under a hospital banner, 9–118 views); the dormant 2020–22 national series; Dr Theo (careers); Dr P R K Varma (general "AI for Beginners"); Dr Mukharjee ("AI at Pulse Heart", 16K, and ChatGPT commentary for the public).
- **Why they are unlikely to move:** event uploads are not a weekly habit; the national series depended on pandemic-era volunteers; Mukharjee's AI content is consumer commentary plus hospital promotion; none has published how they check AI output.
- **Proof he needs:** a published AI charter; a weekly rhythm held for six months; one abstract on the Telugu-explanation method with error rates; cases handled under journal-grade de-identification and consent (files 11 and 19; confirm with a lawyer and Apollo).
- **First 10 pieces:**
  1. The AI charter, one page: what AI is used for, what it is never used for, how output is checked.
  2. "How I verify a Telugu patient explanation drafted with AI": the checklist, with two failures shown.
  3. Open case conference no. 1 (30 minutes; a published or fully de-identified, consented case; doctors register).
  4. "One image, one lesson": a weekly ECG or angiogram teaching post in English.
  5. "My discharge checklist after PCI" for young consultants (the peer face of Position 1).
  6. A monthly trial note: "what changes on Monday morning".
  7. A prompt-and-verify sheet for trainees: summarising a guideline section and checking it against the source.
  8. A talk proposal to the CSI Telangana chapter on the verification method.
  9. A small ethics-approved study: comprehension of AI-drafted versus standard Telugu discharge advice.
  10. A monthly trainee hour on clinical reasoning.

**Positions that look open and are not:** "evidence-based Telugu cardiologist" (the default claim); "new study explained" (Mukharjee's core); naturopathy myth-busting as an identity (already done by Mukharjee, now in court via a doctors' association, and politically costly).

#### 6b. The recommended mix (about 3 hours a week of his time)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Telugu anchor explainer**, 4–7 minutes, numbered within a series, calm, sources on screen and linked | 1 a week, recorded two at a time | 75 minutes a fortnight | Edit, subtitle, title, publish; cut **two Shorts from each anchor** (no extra filming) | Search-led, evergreen, suits a prepared introvert; the Shorts are pointers, not the product |

| **One live session a month**, alternating community ("First 60 minutes") and peers (case conference) | 1 a month | 90 minutes plus preparation | Logistics, registration, the quiz | The proof that turns content into a lighthouse |

One topic a week feeds all four. That is roughly 40 + 20 + 45 minutes weekly, plus about 40 minutes a week averaged for the monthly session: 2.5 to 3 hours.

**Honest expectation.** Reach will be modest at first. Report 13's ranges put one video a week at 1K–3K subscribers in 12 months and three to four a week at 10K–25K. One anchor plus two derived Shorts sits between them; my estimate is 3K–10K at 12 months (Low confidence). The measures that matter are different: the course completed, search position for "స్టెంట్ తర్వాత" queries, card downloads and forwards, press pick-ups, people trained, trainees attending.

#### 6c. What he should not compete on

1. **Daily Shorts.** Mukharjee's cadence took eight years to build and is tied to novelty.
2. **Explaining named people's illnesses and deaths.** Seven of Mukharjee's 40 most recent long-form videos and two of his top five do this. It is speculation about identifiable people.
3. **Food and diet tips.** Ten of the Movva channel's top 25 and the whole naturopathy cluster live here. Food appears in his work only as numbers (salt, oil quantity, LDL effect).
4. **The "new drug, new study" feed.** About 12 of Mukharjee's last 40 long-form videos. A quarterly "what changed" note is enough.
5. **General medicine breadth.** Eleven of 36 recent Movva titles are outside cardiology.
6. **The aggregator interview circuit** (SumanTV, iDream, Telugu Health Focus) as a routine. One selective guest slot a year on a news-owned channel is a different matter.
7. **Hype titles and guarantees**, which the NMC guidelines bar and which define the other quadrant of the map.
8. **Instagram reels and subscriber counts** as goals.
9. **A hospital-branded channel or sign-off.** His handle and channel carry his own name only.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Titles written by someone else still carry his name.** The Movva channel shows how a cardiologist ends up under "cholesterol vanishes" headlines. He approves every title and thumbnail; the friend does not publish without it.
- **Reading "unoccupied" as "uncontested for long".** Dr Mukharjee can post a stent series in a week. The defence is depth, sequence, sources and the cards, plus the off-screen sessions, not being first.
- **Defining himself against naturopathy.** Its leader is a state adviser with a 9M-subscriber footprint; the fight is already in court. Stay with "what works, in numbers".
- **Case material in the peer conference.** AIIMS has barred online case details even when de-identified (report 13). Use published cases or fully consented, de-identified ones; get the lawyer's and Apollo's view first.
- **Long-form drift.** Four to seven minutes per anchor. Beyond ten minutes the recording burden rises and the card stops fitting on a page.
- **Promotional residue.** No booking link, no "best", no testimonials, no hospital in titles. Dr Raghu's site and Dr Sanjeev Kumar's channel show what now breaches the October 2026 guidelines.
- **Judging the plan by subscribers in the first six months.** The format is chosen for search life and trust, not for early numbers.
- **Treating title-level judgements as proven.** This audit did not watch videos. Before any public statement about another channel's content, someone must watch it; better still, make no such statement.

## Open questions / gaps

1. **On-screen practice.** Do Dr Mukharjee's videos show sources or a registration number on screen, and how does he sign off? Needs 20 videos watched by a Telugu speaker. The same check should confirm what the top naturopathy videos actually advise.
2. **Who operates @Dr.MovvaSrinivas and the "Official" channel**, and on what terms doctors appear on SumanTV and iDream (paid or not). Needs a direct enquiry or channel-ownership records.
3. **Instagram, Facebook, ShareChat and WhatsApp Channel reach** of Telugu cardiologists. None could be read here; needs a logged-in manual check of about 15 handles.
4. **Telugu print.** Whether Sakshi "Family", Andhra Jyothy "Navya" or Eenadu run a named cardiologist's column or Q&A now; and whether any recent Telugu heart book by a cardiologist is in print (check Visalandhra and Navodaya catalogues).
5. **Outcome of the HRDA v. VRK suit** after the 7 Oct 2026 hearing, and whether the aggregator respondents removed content. This decides how risky the interview circuit is.
6. **Format test.** No Telugu data yet shows that a numbered course outperforms standalone items. The first ten parts should be treated as the test, with retention and search data reviewed at week 12.
