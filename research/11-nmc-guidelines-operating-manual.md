# The NMC Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication (6 Oct 2026) as an operating manual for one doctor, one helper and AI tools

**Executive summary**

1. I re-read the full 13-page NMC notice (No. R-13014/01/2024-Ethics, 6 Oct 2026) and the primary text of the NMC Act, 2019 and the 2002 Ethics Regulations. The Guidelines define "advertisement" and "social media" but **do not define "public communication", "promotion", "third party", "educational", "designation" or "promotional marketing"**. There is no single education-versus-promotion test in the text; a workable five-part test can be assembled from clauses 3.2, Explanation I, 5.1 and 8.2(iii).
2. **No clarification, FAQ, corrigendum, court challenge, stay, IMA/AHPI statement or enforcement action was found** in the five weeks since issue (12 searches, NMC "What's New" through 6 Nov 2026, TGMC downloads, Medical Dialogues follow-ups). The Medical Dialogues piece "NMC lists exceptions..." is a report on clause 8.2, not a new NMC document. The only law-firm commentary found is LexCounsel (23 Oct 2026). Confidence in "nothing has happened": Medium.
3. **For an individual doctor the Guidelines are binding in practice.** They are not gazetted regulations under s.57, but s.30(2) of the NMC Act tells State Medical Councils to act on "the regulations made, and the guidelines framed, under this Act", and almost every prohibition on doctors restates clause 6.1.1 of the 2002 Regulations, which is binding and non-exhaustive (cl. 8.1). The legally weaker parts are the new ones: hospital obligations, the monetary penalty, per-post registration disclosure and AI labelling.
4. **A friend who edits and schedules educational posts, and an AI drafting tool, are not "engaging a third party for marketing medical services" on a fair reading**, provided the content is educational, the doctor approves every item, the helper never markets him and no payment is tied to patients. This is interpretation, not text; it should be confirmed in writing by a lawyer.
5. The six deliverables are in section 9: a one-page pre-publication checklist, 20 safe-harbour formats, a red-flag word list (English and Telugu), a tiered consent and de-identification protocol, five worked rewrites, and the written questions for a health lawyer, the Telangana Medical Council and the hospitals. **Most urgent finding: every public listing shows an Andhra Pradesh Medical Council number (74749) and no Telangana number, while Explanation III requires his registration number on electronic posts.**

> **Method note.** Used the full budget of 12 web searches; the rest is direct retrieval of primary documents (NMC notice, NMC Act 2019 from nmc.org.in, 2002 Regulations from nmc.org.in, NMC "What's New", TGMC downloads, ICMJE) and of the news and commentary pages the searches surfaced. Quotations from the Guidelines are from the PDF text; the PDF has OCR/typing quirks ("programmers", "Al" for AI) which I have left as printed or marked [sic]. This is research, not legal advice.

Convention used throughout: **(a) Text** = what the document says, quoted. **(b) Interpretation** = my reasonable reading, with confidence. **(c) Unsettled** = the text does not answer it and nobody authoritative has yet.

### 1. Definitions, the friend, the AI tool, and the education-versus-promotion line

**(a) Text**

- **"Advertisement" (3.2):** "any form of communication, promotion, publicity, announcement, representation or dissemination of information in any medium, which directly or indirectly solicits, promotes or is intended to promote the professional services, reputation, skills, qualifications, achievements, facilities or commercial healthcare services of an RMP or hospital/medical institution."
- **Digital limb (3.2, second sentence):** "digital, social-media, sponsored, targeted, influencer-led, search-engine, platform-based, audio-visual, Al-generated or Al-assisted promotional communications shall be treated as advertising where their content or manner of dissemination has a promotional character."
- **Explanation I (the exemption):** "The discourse of information in the normal course of discharge of information to the public at large, without same being promotional and without any intent for commercial benefit shall be exempted from the above definition of 'Advertisement'."
- **"Medium of advertisement" (3.5)** "means and includes but is not limited to print media, Television, Radio, Social media platforms, websites and blogs, messaging platforms (WhatsApp, Telegrams, Facebook etc.), Podcasts, Influencer marketing, Digital marketing campaigns, sponsored posts, artificial intelligence generated promotion, public interviews, news articles intended for promotion, online healthcare platforms, any audio-visual or digital communication by whatsoever name called."
- **"Social Media" (3.6):** "all forms of publications and sharing through internet-based websites, apps, etc. by whatsoever name called, which allows users to create, share and join online communities, participate in social networking, etc."
- **Not defined anywhere:** "public communication" (appears only in the title), "promotion" / "promotional", "third party", "educational", "self-promotion", "designation", "employer organization", "monetized", "promotional marketing", "Electronic media posts". Confidence: High (full-text check).
- **Third parties.** 8.1(ii): an RMP "Shall not solicit patients directly or indirectly, promote his/her professional services through self-promotion, engage any third party for marketing medical services, permit the use of their name, image, voice, testimonial or endorsement for promotion of services or any products, participate in promotional campaigns giving an impression of commercialization of medical practice."
- 8.1(viii): "The engagement of advertising agencies, digital marketing agencies, influencers or online platforms shall not be structured in a manner that results in payment or consideration being linked to the procurement or referral of individual patients."
- 8.1(xi): "No RMP shall use or permit any celebrity, influencer, patient, employee or third party to promote professional services through prohibited testimonials, endorsements or recommendations. Any material commercial relationship in lawful public-health or informational communication shall be transparently disclosed in accordance with applicable law. No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall use a third party as an intermediary to do indirectly what the RMP or institution is prohibited from doing directly."
- 4.4: publication through "a digital platform, third party, advertising agency, influencer or other intermediary shall not, by itself, absolve an RMP ... from responsibility for content which it has authorized, commissioned, sponsored, adopted or knowingly permitted."
- **Education.** 5.1: "Medical Practitioners may participate in health awareness programmers [sic], public health campaigns, academic discussions, educational content, provided that such activities do not promote personal practice, do not solicit patients and are not monetized through promotional marketing." 8.2(iii): "Medical Practitioners may deliver lectures or talks on public health and diseases for public awareness through television, radio and electronic media in their own name and designation, without promoting their employer organization."
- **AI.** 7.2: "Al Generated promotional campaign for furtherance of commercial interest is prohibited. Any Al generated promotional content conforming to the provisions of these guidelines ... shall compulsorily carry the source mark, explicitly stating that the origin of the content is Al." 7.2(b): AI "shall not be used to create or manipulate a patient's image, testimonial, voice or clinical outcome, or to create a synthetic endorsement or representation purporting to be that of an actual patient, RMP or other person." 7.2(c): "Where Al-generated or materially Al-altered content is used in a manner in which its artificial nature may reasonably be material to the audience's understanding of the communication, appropriate disclosure shall be made." 7.2(d): "Any use of patient information as input for an Al system shall comply with applicable privacy, confidentiality, data-protection and professional-ethics requirements."
- **Older, still binding text.** 2002 Reg. 7.11: a physician "should not contribute to the lay press articles and give interviews regarding diseases and treatments which may have the effect of advertising himself or soliciting practices; but is open to write to the lay press under his own name on matters of public health, hygienic living or to deliver public lectures, give talks on the radio/TV/internet chat for the same purpose and send announcement of the same to lay press."

**(b) Interpretation**

*Is there a test for "educational" versus "promotional"?* Not a stated one. Reading 3.2, Explanation I, 5.1, 8.2(iii) and Reg. 7.11 together gives five questions, and content is safe only if it passes all five. Confidence: Medium-High that this is how a council would reason; it is my synthesis, not the regulator's.

| # | Question | Source | Fail example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Subject:** is the subject a disease, risk, test, treatment class or public-health behaviour, and not him, his skill, his results or his services? | 3.2 ("professional services, reputation, skills, qualifications, achievements"); 2002 Reg. 6.1.1 ("invite attention to him or to his professional position, skill...") | "How I opened a 100% blocked artery" |
| 2 | **Ask:** does it ask the audience to do something for their health, and not to come to him or his hospital? | 5.1 ("do not solicit patients", "do not promote personal practice"); 8.1(ii) | "Book a consultation", clinic number in caption |
| 3 | **Money:** is it free of payment in either direction (no sponsorship, affiliate link, brand deal, paid slot, paid boost)? | 5.1 ("not monetized through promotional marketing"); Expl. I ("without any intent for commercial benefit"); 3.2 ("sponsored, targeted") | Sponsored wearable review; boosted reel |
| 4 | **Manner:** is the *way it is distributed* ordinary public information, and not a campaign? | 3.2 ("content or manner of dissemination has a promotional character"); 8.3(ii) for hospitals: "The form, medium or frequency of communication shall not convert otherwise factual information into solicitation or promotional self-aggrandizement" | Same educational video run as a targeted ad to "people near Financial District with heart disease interest" |
| 5 | **Employer:** does it avoid promoting the hospital? | 8.2(iii) | Hospital logo backdrop, "at Apollo we have the latest cath lab" |

A sixth, stricter test survives from 2002 and prevails in any conflict (cl. 4.2): Reg. 7.11 looks at **effect**, not only intent ("which may have the effect of advertising himself"). Interviews and lay articles about "diseases and treatments" are the risk category; "public health, hygienic living" is the safe category.

*The reputation problem.* Literally, any communication "intended to promote the ... reputation ... of an RMP" is an advertisement. A programme whose internal documents say its purpose is to make him "widely known" is, on its face, inside the definition. The exit is Explanation I plus 5.1: information to the public "without same being promotional and without any intent for commercial benefit". **Consequence: purpose is evidence.** The programme's written charter, prompts, briefs and logs should describe public health education and academic contribution. Confidence: Medium-High.

*The friend.* 8.1(ii) bars engaging a third party "for marketing medical services". Editing a script on hypertension and pressing "schedule" is not marketing medical services. 8.1(viii) also shows that engagement of agencies and platforms is contemplated as lawful so long as payment is not tied to patients, which means "engage any third party" in 8.1(ii) cannot be a ban on all outside help. So:

- Friend edits, formats, translates, schedules and logs **educational** content that the doctor has approved: not caught. Confidence: Medium-High.
- Friend posts on his own LinkedIn/X praising the doctor's skill, recommending him, or telling how the doctor treated a relative: this is a third party promoting professional services through an endorsement or recommendation; if the doctor knows and lets it continue he "permits" it (8.1(xi), 4.4). Caught. Confidence: High.
- Friend reshares an educational post with a neutral line ("Useful explainer on BP targets"): not caught. Confidence: Medium-High.
- Friend is paid: allowed only as a flat fee for editorial work, never linked to patients, leads, followers-to-appointments or reviews (8.1(vii), (viii)). Unpaid is cleaner.
- The doctor stays fully responsible for everything published in his name (4.4). The F1000Research opinion piece from AIIMS Bibinagar (4 Nov 2026) makes the same point about delegated accounts: "it is the doctor who will be answerable".

*The AI tool.* A drafting tool is not a "third party"; it is covered by 3.2 (AI-assisted *promotional* communications count as advertising) and 7.2. On the text:

- AI-assisted drafting of educational text that he reads and approves: permitted. The compulsory "source mark" in 7.2 attaches, in terms, to "Al generated promotional content". For educational content the governing rule is 7.2(c): disclose where the artificial nature "may reasonably be material to the audience's understanding". A text draft he has rewritten and approved is unlikely to meet that threshold; an AI image, AI voice, AI avatar or AI-dubbed video does. Confidence: Medium.
- No patient information of any kind goes into an AI tool (7.2(d)). Confidence: High.
- AI must never produce a patient quote, patient image, outcome, review or any statement attributed to a real person (7.2(b)). Confidence: High.

**(c) Unsettled**

1. Whether a council would treat an organised, friend-run, AI-assisted publishing system for one doctor as a "promotional campaign giving an impression of commercialization" (8.1(ii)) because of its scale and regularity, even if each post is educational. The hospital clause on "form, medium or frequency" (8.3(ii)) shows the regulator thinks frequency can change character.
2. Whether "engage" requires payment or a contract, or covers an unpaid friend.
3. Whether AI-drafted educational text needs a source mark. Commentators differ; HMS Consultants suggest a label only for promotional content and "review every line".
4. Whether an AI clone of his own voice (for Telugu dubbing) with his consent and a label is a prohibited "synthetic ... representation purporting to be that of an actual ... RMP" (7.2(b)).
5. Where "public health and diseases for public awareness" ends and "diseases and treatments which may have the effect of advertising himself" (Reg. 7.11) begins, for content on procedures he personally performs (angioplasty, pacemakers, TAVI).

### 2. Patient stories, teaching cases, images

**(a) Text**

- 6.1: "Patient information, photographs, videos, medical records, clinical images, treatment details, testimonials or other identifiable information shall not be used for advertising purposes except where such use is permitted by applicable law and the necessary consent and other safeguards have been duly complied with."
- 6.2: "Patient consent shall not, by itself, make permissible any testimonial, endorsement, promotional patient story, before-and-after depiction, success claim or other advertising practice that is otherwise prohibited under these Guidelines or applicable law."
- 6.3: where consent is legally required it "shall be specific, informed, voluntary, documented and capable of verification".
- 6.4: "Even where disclosure is lawfully permitted ... RMP shall be duty bound to prevent unnecessary disclosure of names or to crop, blur, black out, birth marks/ identification marks, or other information through which the patient may be identified."
- 7.4: "RMP/ Clinical establishment shall not share any patient data, such as names, faces, anomalies, etc., which can be identified publicly."
- 8.1(v): "Shall not publish cases for promotional purposes, advertise surgical results, display 'before and after' photographs, publicize celebrity patients, promote personal success stories unless published strictly for scientific or educational purposes with anonymized patient consent."
- 8.2(i): "Only research-based communication, wherein they have published interesting case studies in reputed medical journals shall be allowed."
- Explanation V: "An RMP shall not request or share patient testimonials, recommendations, endorsements or reviews for professional promotion on social media."
- 7.5: no live broadcast or commercial dissemination of procedures inconsistent with the NMC live-surgery framework (NMC live-surgery guidelines of July 2025, secondary source: live surgeries "must not promote individual surgeons, hospitals, or commercial brands").
- 2002 Reg. 6.1.1: "nor shall he boast of cases, operations, cures or remedies or permit the publication of report thereof through any mode." Reg. 7.17: he "shall not publish photographs or case reports of his / her patients without their permission, in any medical or other journal in a manner by which their identity could be made out. If the identity is not to be disclosed, the consent is not needed."

**What is barred, exactly.** Seven things, none cured by consent: (1) testimonials; (2) endorsements by patients; (3) a "promotional patient story"; (4) before-and-after depictions; (5) "success claim[s]" and advertised surgical results; (6) publicised celebrity patients; (7) "personal success stories". Plus any identifiable patient data in public (7.4), with or without a promotional purpose.

**(b) Interpretation**

The phrase "unless published strictly for scientific or educational purposes with anonymized patient consent" is badly drafted. The sensible reading is: case material may be published if (i) the purpose is strictly scientific or educational, (ii) the patient is anonymised **and** (iii) the patient has consented. That is stricter than Reg. 7.17, which needs no consent when identity is not disclosed. Follow the stricter rule. Confidence: Medium-High.

| Use | Position | Basis and conditions | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Case report or series in a reputed, indexed journal | **Allowed** | 8.2(i); 8.1(v) exception; Reg. 7.17. Written consent (journals following ICMJE require it whenever anonymity is in doubt: "Informed consent should be obtained if there is any doubt that anonymity can be maintained"), anonymisation, hospital permission | High |
| Sharing the link to his own published case report with a neutral summary | **Allowed** | 8.2(i) "research-based communication". Caption states the teaching point, not his feat | High |
| Case presentation to peers at a conference, CME, grand round | **Allowed** | 5.1 "academic discussions"; 8.1(v) exception. Consent + anonymisation + hospital permission. Audience is professionals; no solicitation | Medium-High |
| Closed, verified doctor-only groups | **Allowed with the same safeguards** | As above. Assume screenshots leak; nothing identifiable | Medium |
| Posting conference case slides or angiogram loops on open X/LinkedIn "for peers" | **Amber, lean no** | The audience is the public once it is on an open platform; angiogram before/after is the named prohibited format. If done at all: after journal publication, linking to the paper | Medium |
| **Composite or hypothetical teaching scenario** for the public ("A 48-year-old software manager wakes at 3 a.m. with pressure in his chest...") | **Allowed** | It is not patient information. Must be labelled illustrative, must not be traceable to one real patient, and the doctor must not be the hero. Must not be AI-fabricated *as if real* (7.2(b)) | Medium-High |
| A **real** but fully de-identified case told to the lay public | **Amber, default no** | Text permits "educational purposes with anonymized patient consent", but the same story reads as a "promotional patient story" or "personal success story" the moment it shows his good result; re-identification risk within Hyderabad is real | Medium |
| De-identified **ECG** shown to the public to teach recognition | **Allowed if it is not his patient's**: use a textbook, open-licence or simulated trace, or redraw it | Avoids 6.1 and 7.4 entirely. A stripped ECG is practically non-identifying, but "my patient's ECG" adds nothing educational and adds risk | High for the substitute route |
| De-identified **angiogram stills or loops** to the public | **No**; use diagrams, animations or licensed illustrations | "advertise surgical results", "before and after" (8.1(v)); "anomalies" as identifiers (7.4); the 2023 Guideline-6 (no legal force) said no scan images at all | Medium-High |
| Same angiogram/ECG to peers | **Allowed** with consent, anonymisation, hospital permission, teaching framing (include a complication or a decision point, not only the win) | 8.1(v) exception | Medium-High |
| Any before/after pair, to anyone outside a journal or closed teaching session | **No** | 6.2, 8.1(v) | High |
| Live or recorded procedure video on public channels | **No** | 7.5; 2023 Guideline-6 point 7 as best practice | High |

*Patients posting their own stories on their own accounts.* The Guidelines regulate the doctor, not the patient. A patient's unprompted post is the patient's speech. What the doctor may not do is "request or share" it (Expl. V), "permit" a patient to promote him through a testimonial (8.1(xi)), or "adopt" it (4.4).

- **Reshare, quote, screenshot, repost to stories, embed on a site:** prohibited ("share"). Confidence: High.
- **Like or public "thank you" reply:** not named in the text, but a like is the lightest form of adoption and amplifies the post on most platforms; a reply confirms a doctor-patient relationship in public, which is a confidentiality problem under Regs 2.2 and 7.14 regardless of advertising. **Do neither.** Thank the patient privately, in person or through the hospital. Confidence: Medium-High that this is the safe line; Medium that a like alone would ever be actioned.
- **Being tagged:** he cannot stop it. Set profiles so tagged posts do not display on his profile without approval, and do not approve. Confidence: Medium.
- **Duty to ask the patient to delete:** none on a fair reading; "permit" implies power to prevent. Confidence: Medium.

**(c) Unsettled**

1. Whether 8.2(i) ("Only research-based communication ... published ... in reputed medical journals shall be allowed") narrows the 8.1(v) exception so that *only* journal-published cases may be communicated at all, which would put unpublished conference cases in doubt.
2. What "anonymized patient consent" means, and whether consent is required even where identity cannot be made out (Reg. 7.17 says no; 4.2 says the Regulations prevail in "irreconcilable conflict", but a stricter guideline is not an irreconcilable conflict).
3. Whether a real, consented, anonymised case told to the lay public for genuine education is permitted. Text says yes; the risk of it being read as a "promotional patient story" is high.
4. Whether liking a patient's post is "adopting" it.

### 3. Reviews

**(a) Text**

- Explanation V, first sentence: "No RMP or hospital/medical institution shall solicit, purchase, procure, manipulate or cause to be published fake, paid or misleading patient reviews, ratings, testimonials, endorsements or recommendations for the promotion of medical services."
- Explanation V, second sentence: "An RMP shall not request or share patient testimonials, recommendations, endorsements or reviews for professional promotion on social media."
- 8.1(xii): no RMP or hospital shall "procure, manipulate or cause any third party to procure or manipulate fake followers, likes, reviews, ratings, testimonials, comments, views or other digital engagement, or manipulate search rankings, visibility or algorithms to create a misleading impression of professional standing."
- 9.4: "All healthcare platforms hosting RMP shall ensure compliance with NMC ethical advertising norms, prohibition of paid ranking of doctors, maintain transparency in listing practices."
- Nothing in the text mentions replying to reviews, retrospective removal, or third-party sites.

**(b) Interpretation**

Two different rules sit in Explanation V. Sentence one covers doctors **and hospitals** but only "fake, paid or misleading" reviews. Sentence two covers genuine reviews but only **the RMP**, only "request or share", only "for professional promotion", only "on social media". Google Maps and Practo fall comfortably inside the 3.6 definition of social media.

| Question | Answer | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| May **he** send or hand out a link/QR to his Google or Practo review page, however neutrally worded? | **No.** It is a request for a review on social media, and the only plausible purpose of a public review is the one the clause names | Medium-High (HMS Consultants: "stopping until the NMC clarifies") |
| May he ask patients for **private** feedback (a form that goes only to him or the hospital quality team, never published)? | **Yes.** It is not a "review ... on social media" and not promotion. Keep it truly private; never convert responses into quotes | Medium-High |
| May the **hospital** send every patient a neutral feedback link? | Sentence two does not bind hospitals. A private satisfaction survey is fine. A link to a public review page about the hospital is the hospital's legal call. It must not be about him, route to his personal profile, or be selective. He should not ask for it or run it; "indirectly what [he] is prohibited from doing directly" (8.1(xi)) | Medium |
| May he **reply** to Google reviews? | Text is silent; replying is neither "request" nor "share". The risk is confidentiality (confirming a patient relationship) and looking like adoption of a testimonial. Recommended: **do not reply to positive reviews at all; reply to negative or factually wrong ones with one fixed, generic line** that confirms nothing and points to the hospital's patient-relations desk | Medium |
| Must he remove **existing** testimonials? | For anything he controls or took part in: **yes, now.** The notice is not retrospective in terms, but a page that is still live is still being published, and 4.4 reaches content he "authorized ... adopted or knowingly permitted". This means: testimonial videos he appears in or shared, reposted reviews, review widgets or "patient stories" on any profile he has claimed | Medium-High |
| Hospital pages and videos he does **not** control | He cannot delete them, but 11.5 reaches him where he "participated in" the communication. He must **ask in writing** for removal and keep the request. That letter is his defence | Medium-High |
| Unsolicited reviews patients wrote on Google or Practo | No duty; they are not his communication and he cannot remove them. Do not flag genuine ones for removal either (that is manipulation) | Medium-High |
| Hospital quoting reviews about him on its site, reels or ads | Prohibited for the hospital as "promotional portrayal of an individual RMP" (8.3(ii)); for him it is "permit[ting] ... patient ... to promote professional services" (8.1(xi)) once he knows. **Object in writing** | Medium-High |
| Star-rating badges ("4.9 on Google") on his profiles or the hospital's page about him | Same as sharing reviews. No | Medium-High |

**(c) Unsettled**

1. Whether a neutral, all-patient request by the doctor for honest reviews is "for professional promotion". Every commentator found treats this as open.
2. Whether keeping a claimed Practo profile on which the platform itself displays patient stories is "knowingly permitting". Clause 9.4 puts the duty on the platform, which helps.
3. Whether replying "thank you" to a review is "sharing" or "adopting".
4. Whether the notice applies to content first published before 6 Oct 2026 that remains online.

### 4. Mandatory disclosures

**(a) Text**

- Explanation III: "RMP while publishing any Electronic media posts should transparently disclose their name, qualifications, registration status and SMR/NMR Registration number."
- Explanation IV: clinical establishments publishing electronic media posts "should transparently disclose the names of the RMP with qualifications, registration status and SMR/NMR Registration number."
- 8.1(xi): "Any material commercial relationship in lawful public-health or informational communication shall be transparently disclosed in accordance with applicable law."
- 7.2: AI source mark and 7.2(c) disclosure (see section 1).
- 2002 Reg. 1.4.1: registration number displayed "in his clinic and in all his prescriptions, certificates, money receipts". Reg. 1.4.2: suffixes limited to "recognized medical degrees or such certificates/diplomas and memberships/honours which confer professional knowledge or recognizes any exemplary qualification/achievements". Reg. 7.13: signboard and prescription paper limited to "his name, qualifications obtained from a University or a statutory body, titles and name of his speciality, registration number including the name of the State Medical Council under which registered".
- **The text prescribes no format, no placement, no font size, no disclaimer wording and no language.** Confidence: High.

**(b) Interpretation**

- **Every post or bio only?** The words are "while publishing any Electronic media posts", which most naturally means each post. Bio-only is arguable for short-form platforms but is the weaker reading. A full line costs about 60 characters. **Rule: put it in the bio and in every post.** For assets that travel without the bio (video, carousel image, PDF, WhatsApp forward, newsletter, podcast description) the line must be *inside the asset*: on screen for two seconds and in the description. Confidence: Medium-High.
- **Format** (my drafting; no official format exists):
  `Dr Avinash Pathuri, MBBS, MD, DM (Cardiology) | Registered Medical Practitioner, [State] Medical Council Reg. No. [number]`
  Short form for character-limited posts: `Dr Avinash Pathuri, DM (Cardiology), [TGMC] Reg. [number]`
  "Registration status" is read as: registered, with which council, and current. Add the NMR ID if he has enrolled on the National Medical Register.
- **Which number.** Round 1 found every listing showing **No. 74749, Andhra Pradesh Medical Council**, and no Telangana number anywhere. TGMC's April 2024 circular flagged consultants practising in Hyderabad without Telangana registration or renewal. He must settle and then display the number that authorises his current practice. This is the first action item. Confidence: High that the listings show this; his actual register position is not verified.
- **Qualifications.** Only degrees that are NMC-recognised **and** entered against his name on the register. Society fellowships are memberships, not qualifications; list them separately or leave them out of the credential line. TGMC has suspended doctors in 2025 and 2026 for displaying unrecognised qualifications (Round 1, file 02). Confidence: High.
- **Disclaimers.** The Guidelines require none. Carry one anyway because it supports the educational character and matches the CCPA health-influencer guidance (Round 1, Medium confidence): `General health education, not personal medical advice. Please consult your doctor. In an emergency call 108.`
- **Disclosures line.** 8.1(xi) requires disclosure of any "material commercial relationship" in informational communication. Keep a standing line on the profile ("Disclosures: none" or the actual list) and repeat in any post that touches a relevant drug class or device.
- **Language.** No rule in the text. Put the credential line in the language of the post plus English (registration numbers in Latin numerals) so that a Telugu viewer and a council officer can both read it.

**(c) Unsettled**

1. Per-post versus profile-level disclosure, and whether "should" (Explanation III) is mandatory when the operative clauses use "shall".
2. What "registration status" requires beyond the number.
3. Whether the duty applies when a third party (newspaper, TV channel, podcast host) publishes his talk.
4. Whether displaying an APMC number while practising in Telangana satisfies "SMR/NMR Registration number".

### 5. Media: interviews, op-eds, TV, podcasts, YouTube, money

**(a) Text**

- 8.2(iii) (quoted above): "lectures or talks on public health and diseases for public awareness through television, radio and electronic media in their own name and designation, without promoting their employer organization."
- 3.5 lists "Podcasts", "public interviews, news articles intended for promotion" as media of advertisement.
- 2002 Reg. 7.11 (quoted above): no lay-press articles or interviews "regarding diseases and treatments which may have the effect of advertising himself"; yes to writing "under his own name on matters of public health, hygienic living", public lectures, "talks on the radio/TV/internet chat", and he may "send announcement of the same to lay press".
- 5.1: educational content must not be "monetized through promotional marketing".
- 8.1(iv): no "approval, recommendation, endorsement, certificate, report or statement concerning any drug, medicine, remedy, therapeutic article, apparatus, appliance, medical device, diagnostic product, health product or commercial product for use in advertising or publicity in connection with his/her name, signature, photograph, voice or professional status, whether or not monetary or other consideration is received."
- 7.3 Explanation: "All campaigns on programmes/schemes of Government of India and the state Governments, which are in larger public interest, shall be exempted from these guidelines."
- The text says nothing about AdSense, platform revenue-sharing, honoraria, books, affiliate links or boosting by name.

### 6. Hospital-run promotion that features him

**(a) Text**

- 8.3(i): hospitals "may disseminate factual, objective and verifiable information concerning their name, location, contact details, departments, facilities, equipment, services, emergency services, accreditation status, charges and other institutional information".
- 8.3(ii): "Such communication shall not contain patient solicitation, inducement, comparative superiority claims, guarantees of cure or treatment outcome, unverifiable or exaggerated claims, or promotional portrayal of an individual RMP."
- 9.1: "A hospital website containing a directory of its doctors, containing the names, recognised qualifications, specialties, registration details and availability of its doctors, that serves a patient-information function; shall be regarded materially distinct from a paid or promotional advertisement or inducement."
- 9.2: "Use of the photograph/image of an RMP shall not be permitted; where such use constitutes self-advertisement, solicitation or promotional publicity."
- 5.2: hoardings may be used "only for dissemination of factual information in the nature of infrastructure, facilities etc."
- 8.1(iii): an RMP shall not advertise "personal achievements or professional success rates or number of patients treated with illustrative assertions such as 'Guaranteed cures', 'Best doctors' ..." or "any other unverifiable or exaggerated claims".
- 10.1 Explanation and 11.5: hospitals are dealt with under the Clinical Establishments Act or State Act; "Where an RMP has personally authorized, participated in, endorsed or otherwise been responsible for the prohibited communication, the professional conduct of such RMP may separately be examined by the concerned State Medical Council, Ethics and Medical Registration Board or National Medical Commission".
- 4.3: for hospitals, the Clinical Establishment Act or State Act "shall override the provisions of these Guidelines" in a conflict.

**(b) Interpretation**

*Who is liable.* Two tracks. The hospital answers under clinical-establishment law; LexCounsel (23 Oct 2026) notes those laws "generally do not contain specific provisions governing healthcare advertising or penalties for advertising-related violations" and that the NMC's authority to enforce against hospital entities "remains a key legal question". The doctor answers to the State Medical Council if he "authorized, participated in, endorsed or otherwise [was] responsible". **Practical result: the hospital track is weak and slow, so the registered doctor is the reachable party.** Kerala censured 11 doctors over one hospital advertisement in 2020 (Round 1). Confidence: Medium-High.

*His position turns on knowledge and paper.* He is exposed for hospital content he sat for, approved, or knows about and leaves standing. He protects himself by (1) refusing participation in promotional content and (2) a dated written request to correct or remove what already exists.

### 7. Enforcement

**(a) Text**

- 10.1: "actions will be taken by the concerned SMC against the erring RMP in accordance with the provisions of the NMC Act and Regulations made thereunder. The following graded action may be contemplated by the SMC concerned; i. First Violation: Warning and mandatory ethics training. ii. Second Violation: Censure and monetary penalty. iii. Third Violation: Suspension of registration (3-6 months). iv. Serious Violation (Misleading cure claims, inducement for patients, digital mass solicitation, etc.) Suspension of 6-12 months. v. Repeated Violations: Removal from medical register for 1-3 years."
- 10.1 continues: the competent authority "may, where legally permissible, impose such disciplinary or corrective measures ... and monetary penalty, as may be authorized under the NMC Act and applicable Regulations."
- 10.2: penalties "only after issuing a show-cause notice ... and providing an opportunity to explain". 10.3: "a reasoned and speaking order".
- 11.1: appeal to EMRB "within 60 days under Section 30 (3)"; 11.4: second appeal under s.30(4) "within sixty days of communication of such decision".
- NMC Act s.30(2), second proviso (primary): the council "shall give an opportunity of hearing to the medical practitioner or professional concerned before taking any action, including imposition of any monetary penalty".
- 2002 Reg. 8.2: "any complaint with regard to professional misconduct can be brought before the appropriate Medical Council"; the council "may award such punishment as deemed necessary or may direct the removal altogether or for a specified period, from the register"; deletion "shall be widely publicized in local press". Reg. 8.4: decision "within a time limit of 6 months". Reg. 8.5: the council "may restrain the physician from performing the procedure or practice which is under scrutiny" while the complaint is pending.

**(b) Interpretation**

- The ladder is indicative ("may be contemplated"), not a tariff; a council can go straight to suspension for a "serious violation". "Digital mass solicitation" is named as serious, which is why paid ads and bulk WhatsApp are the worst category for him. Confidence: High.
- **Complaint route in Telangana:** anyone, including a competitor, files with TGMC on its "Complaint-cum-Declaration" format (uploaded Sept and Oct 2026); TGMC also acts suo motu (its March 2026 removal order was suo motu). Appeals now go through the NMC online appeal portal (4 Nov 2026). Confidence: High on the documents existing; I did not open them this round.
- **Which council is "concerned".** If he practises in Hyderabad, TGMC will act regardless of where he first registered; an APMC-only registration is itself a TGMC enforcement theme. Confidence: Medium.
- **Enforcement since 6 Oct 2026: none found.** No TGMC circular adopting the Guidelines, no show-cause notice or order citing them from any state council, no NMC advisory. TGMC's published work remains quackery, unrecognised qualifications and registration lapses. The F1000Research opinion piece observes that councils usually stop at a warning for advertising breaches and that "There is no proper flowchart/SOP by the respective state medical councils". Confidence: Medium (absence of evidence after 12 searches and direct checks; unpublished notices are possible).
- **Realistic risk picture.** Probability of action against a quiet, educational channel: low. Trigger events: a peer complaint, a hospital hoarding or testimonial reel with his face, a "best cardiologist" claim, a product mention, or a registration defect. Cost of a first finding: a warning, ethics training and a public record that competitors can cite.

**(c) Unsettled**

1. Whether TGMC has statutory power to levy a monetary penalty under the Telangana Medical Practitioners Registration Act, 1968 (the NMC Act contemplates one; the Guidelines say "where legally permissible").
2. Whether TGMC will issue its own circular or SOP, and whether it will treat the Guidelines' ladder as binding on itself.
3. Whether separate posts count as separate "violations" for the ladder.

Marketing-agency explainers (HMS, Ichelon, MapMyClinic and others) are consistent with each other and with this report but are not legal authority.

**Are the Guidelines legally binding? (primary-text analysis)**

- **(a) Text.** NMC Act s.57(1): the Commission "may, after previous publication, by notification, make regulations". s.57(2)(zd), (zh), (zi) list as matters for *regulations*: "the manner of regulating professional conduct and promoting medical ethics", "the manner of taking disciplinary action by a State Medical Council", and "the act of commission or omission which amounts to professional or ethical misconduct". s.27(1)(b): the EMRB shall "regulate professional conduct and promote medical ethics in accordance with the regulations made under this Act". s.30 Explanation (b): misconduct "includes any act of commission or omission as may be specified by the regulations".
  But s.30(2): a State Medical Council with disciplinary power "shall act in accordance with the regulations made, **and the guidelines framed**, under this Act". s.10(1)(h): the Commission shall "lay down policies and codes to ensure observance of professional ethics". s.10(1)(f): it shall ensure compliance by State Medical Councils "of the guidelines framed and regulations made under this Act".
  The Guidelines describe themselves as operating "without altering, overriding or derogating from any existing statutory or regulatory requirement" (2.1(f)), "read with" the 2002 Regulations (4.2), with regulations prevailing "in case of any irreconcilable conflict" (4.2), and penalties only "where legally permissible" (10.1).
- **(b) Interpretation.** The Guidelines are not regulations: no s.57 notification after previous publication was found. They cannot, alone, create a new head of misconduct or a new penalty. They do have a statutory hook (s.30(2), s.10(1)(h)) and the councils are told to act on them. More importantly, **for an individual doctor almost every prohibition is already in the binding 2002 Regulations**, and Reg. 8.1 makes the list of misconduct non-exhaustive ("Every care should be taken that the code is not violated in letter or spirit"). A council can therefore frame any charge under Reg. 6.1.1 and cite the Guidelines as the standard. Confidence: Medium-High.

- One asymmetry matters. Reg. 6.1.1 is, read literally, *stricter* than the Guidelines: it bars any publicity "of such a character as to invite attention to him or to his professional position, skill, qualification, achievements". The Guidelines' recognition of educational content, electronic media and directories is the liberal part, and because the Regulations prevail in a conflict (4.2, 2.2), **the permissions are the less secure half of the document**. The safe space is the overlap: what both Reg. 7.11 and Guideline 8.2(iii) allow.

**Durability**

- The doctor-facing prohibitions will last; they are 24 years old in substance. A relaxation is unlikely. Confidence: High.
- The hospital-facing parts are the most likely to be litigated or diluted. That does not help him: his exposure under 11.5 is through his own registration.
- The 2023 Conduct Regulations were pulled within three weeks after IMA pressure. Five weeks on, no comparable pushback to the 2026 Guidelines was found, and they were framed for a pending Supreme Court matter, which makes quiet withdrawal less likely. Confidence: Medium.
- More probable next steps: NMC SOPs or FAQs under clause 12; state-council circulars; eventual conversion into gazetted regulations. Plan on the Guidelines being the floor for at least three years, and re-check quarterly.

### 9. Deliverables

The operating model first, because the checklist depends on it.

#### (i) One-page compliance checklist (run before every post, talk or interview)

Any "No" stops publication.

**A. Purpose and subject**
1. Is the subject a condition, risk, test, treatment class or health behaviour, and not him, his skill, his results or his hospital? (3.2; Reg. 6.1.1)
2. Would it be worth publishing if no patient ever came to him because of it? (Expl. I)
3. Is it within cardiology or cardiovascular prevention, with a guideline or peer-reviewed source noted in the log? (2.1(b))

**B. Words and claims**
4. Zero hits on the red-flag word list, in title, thumbnail, caption, hashtags, alt text and on-screen text? (8.1(iii), (ix))
5. No guarantee, success rate, procedure count, "first", "only", "latest", "painless", comparison with other doctors or hospitals? (8.1(iii), (vi), (ix))
6. Risks and limits stated wherever a treatment is described; nothing likely to frighten people into tests or procedures? (8.1(i), (vi))

**C. Patients**
7. No patient name, face, voice, chat, record, image or recognisable detail; any scenario is composite and labelled "illustrative"? (6.1, 6.2, 7.4)
8. No testimonial, thank-you, review, rating, before/after or outcome of his own case, with or without consent? (6.2, 8.1(v), Expl. V)

**D. Money and reach**
9. No sponsor, brand, product, app, lab, insurer or affiliate link named, tagged or shown; any relevant industry relationship disclosed? (8.1(iv), (xi); Reg. 6.8.1)
10. No paid boost, ad, paid slot or paid placement; no monetisation switched on? (3.2, 5.1)

**E. Solicitation and employer**
11. No phone number, address, fee, booking link, "consult me", "DM me", offer or camp promotion? (5.1, 8.1(ii), (x))
12. Hospital not promoted: no logo backdrop, no facility claims; designation plain? (8.2(iii))

**F. Identity and AI**
13. Credential line present inside the asset and in the caption: name, registered qualifications, council, registration number? (Expl. III)
14. Disclaimer line present?
15. No patient data went into any AI tool; any AI-generated image, audio or video is labelled; nothing synthetic is passed off as real? (7.2)

**G. Sign-off**
16. Doctor has read the final version, including metadata, and approved it in the log? (4.4)

**Extra lines for a talk or interview**
17. Who invited him, who pays for the slot, who sponsors the event? If paid or sponsored by his hospital, a pharma or device company or a health brand: decline, or disclose and remove branding as applicable.
18. Has he asked in writing to be introduced by name and plain designation, with no superlatives and no clinic details on screen?
19. Has he told the host he will not discuss his own cases, named patients, a named person's treatment, or brands?
20. For a quote: sent by email so a record exists; request to see how he is described.

**Fixed replies**
- Clinical question in comments or DMs: "I can't advise on individual cases here. Please see your doctor, or call 108 in an emergency."
- Negative or incorrect review: "Thank you for the feedback. We can't discuss any individual's care in public. Please contact the hospital's patient relations desk so this can be looked into."
- Positive reviews and patient posts: no reply, no like, no share.

#### (iii) Red-flag word list

The helper and the AI tool scan every asset, including metadata, for these. A hit means rewrite, not soften. Items in quotation marks in group 1 and 2 are the Guidelines' own examples (8.1(iii), (ix)).

1. **Superlatives and rank:** "best", "No.1", "number one", "top", "leading", "most trusted", "unmatched", renowned, eminent, famous, celebrated, pioneer, expert (as self-description), star, senior-most, most experienced, award-winning (without issuer, date, method), world-class, gold standard (about himself or the hospital).
2. **Outcome and guarantee:** "Guaranteed cures", "100% success", "painless treatment", "Miracle treatment", "Exclusive cure", guaranteed, assured, risk-free, safe (unqualified), permanent cure, no side effects, success rate, zero complications, saved, cured, "gave a new life", flawless, perfect result.
3. **Volume and achievement:** "performed 2,300 angiographies", "over X procedures", "thousands of patients", "X years of excellence", "first in Telangana/Hyderabad/India", "only doctor who", "rare feat", "record", "milestone".
4. **Technology puffery:** latest, most advanced, state-of-the-art, cutting-edge, next-generation, revolutionary, breakthrough (unless quoting a trial and qualifying it), "robotic precision", "US-FDA approved" used as a selling point.
5. **Solicitation:** book now, consult me, visit us, call/WhatsApp for appointment, DM for consultation, limited slots, walk in, "available at", "second opinion from Dr", link in bio for appointments, phone numbers and maps inside educational content.
6. **Inducement:** free, discount, offer, package, camp price, coupon, cashback, EMI, "screening at just Rs", limited period, refer a friend.
7. **Fear:** silent killer (as a hook), "you could die tomorrow", "before it's too late", "ticking time bomb", "shocking", "doctors won't tell you", any unqualified statistic designed to alarm.
8. **Patient-story markers:** "my patient", "one of my patients", "a grateful patient", "success story", "testimonial", "before and after", "see the result", "thank you doctor", patient initials, age-plus-occupation-plus-locality combinations, "VIP", "celebrity".
9. **Endorsement markers:** any brand name of a drug, stent, valve, device, wearable, app, lab, supplement, oil, food, insurer; "I recommend [brand]", "I use", "sponsored by", "in association with", "partner", discount codes, affiliate links.
10. **Employer promotion:** hospital taglines, "at [hospital] we offer", "our cath lab", "our team of experts", hospital hashtags and campaign tags, hospital logo as backdrop.
11. **Unproven therapy:** reversal, detox, cleanse, chelation, "natural cure", "avoid bypass", "dissolve blockages", stem-cell, "ancient secret".
12. **Hashtags and SEO strings:** #bestcardiologist, #topcardiologisthyderabad, #heartspecialistnearme, "cardiologist near me" stuffed into educational content.

**Telugu and transliterated equivalents to scan for** (to be checked by a native-speaker editor): బెస్ట్ / ఉత్తమ (best), నంబర్ వన్ (No.1), ప్రముఖ / ప్రఖ్యాత (renowned), గ్యారంటీ / హామీ (guarantee), 100% సక్సెస్, నొప్పి లేని (painless), అద్భుత చికిత్స (miracle treatment), శాశ్వత పరిష్కారం (permanent cure), ఉచిత (free), ఆఫర్ / డిస్కౌంట్ / ప్యాకేజీ, అపాయింట్‌మెంట్ కోసం సంప్రదించండి (contact for appointment), అత్యాధునిక (most advanced), మొట్టమొదటి (first ever), ప్రాణదాత (life-giver, a common praise word in Telugu media).

#### (iv) Consent and de-identification protocol for teaching cases

**Step 0. Decide the tier. The tier decides everything else.**

| Tier | Audience | Real patient material? | Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | Public (any open platform, press, TV, WhatsApp) | **No.** Composite or hypothetical scenarios, textbook/licensed/simulated images, diagrams | Label "Illustrative scenario, not a real patient". Combine features of several patients or invent them; change age band, sex, occupation, timing; no outcome attributed to him. Not generated by AI as if real |
| T1 | Journal publication | Yes | Steps 1–8 + the journal's own consent form and CARE-style reporting |
| T2 | Conference, CME, grand round, hospital teaching (in the room or behind a registration wall) | Yes | Steps 1–8 |
| T3 | Closed doctor-only online groups | Yes, minimal | Steps 1–8; assume it will be screenshotted; no loops with any text overlay |
| T4 | Open social media "for peers" | Only **after** T1 publication, linking to the paper; still images from the published figure only | Steps 1–8 + the publisher's licence |

**Step 1. Purpose test.** Write one sentence stating the teaching point. If the sentence is about how well the case went, stop. Prefer cases that teach a pitfall, a complication or a decision.

**Step 2. Who asks, and when.** Not the operating doctor where possible; a colleague, nurse coordinator or research staff member. After the patient is stable, never before or during a procedure, never under sedation, never as a favour "in return". State that care is the same whether they agree or refuse.

**Step 3. Written consent, one purpose per form**, in the patient's language (Telugu, Hindi, Urdu or English), read aloud if needed. The form states:
- what material (e.g. "ECG tracings and angiogram images from your admission, and a description of your illness");
- for what use and which audience (journal article / medical conference / teaching of doctors);
- that name, face and identifying details will be removed, but that complete anonymity cannot be guaranteed;
- that published or presented material cannot be fully withdrawn afterwards;
- that it will **not** be used for advertising or on public social media;
- the right to refuse and to withdraw from future use, with a contact person;
- signature or thumb impression, date, witness, name of the person taking consent.
Guardian consent for minors or incapacitated adults; default is not to use. Original in the hospital record; copy to the patient. This meets 6.3 ("specific, informed, voluntary, documented and capable of verification") and anticipates the DPDP consent standard that starts on 13 May 2027.

**Step 4. Hospital permission.** Images belong in the hospital's systems. Use the hospital's process for release of clinical images for academic use; do not photograph screens with a personal phone. Keep the approval email.

**Step 5. De-identification checklist (all must be ticked).**
- Name, initials, UHID/MRN, accession and study numbers, bed and ward numbers removed.
- All dates and times removed or shifted; use "day 1, day 3".
- Hospital name, logo, cath-lab number, machine labels, operator and referring-doctor names removed.
- DICOM metadata stripped; export as new image/video files; burned-in text cropped, not covered with a box that can be lifted.
- ECG headers (name, ID, date, time, device serial) cropped.
- Faces, tattoos, scars, birthmarks, jewellery, clothing, bystanders, room features out of frame (6.4).
- Audio removed from all video.
- Narrative: age as a decade ("in his 50s"); no occupation, employer, locality, religion, community, family details or unusual circumstances (the accident, the wedding, the flight) unless clinically essential; no rare combination that people in Hyderabad would recognise.
- Rare anatomy or a newsworthy event counts as an identifier (7.4 "anomalies"): if the case was in the local news, it cannot be anonymised.
- File names carry a case code only.
- Nothing is uploaded to an AI tool, cloud editor or messaging app in identifiable form at any stage (7.2(d)).

**Step 6. Second-person check.** A colleague who does not know the patient reviews the final slides or figures and signs that they cannot identify the person. ICMJE's standard applies: if there is any doubt about anonymity, consent must cover it; masking the eyes "is inadequate protection".

**Step 7. Framing check.** No "before/after" pair presented as a result; show the decision, the technique problem, the complication, the follow-up. No hospital branding on slides beyond a plain affiliation line. Disclose any industry relationship on slide one.

**Step 8. Register.** Case code, consent form location, date, tier, outlet, hospital approval, checker, withdrawal status. On withdrawal: remove from all future use and from anything under his control within seven days; inform the journal if published.

**What never goes through this protocol because no consent can fix it (6.2):** testimonials, thank-you clips, outcome stories on public channels, before/after images for the public, celebrity or VIP patients.

#### (v) Five worked rewrites

Credential line used at the end of every compliant version (placeholder until his registration is confirmed):
`Dr Avinash Pathuri, MBBS, MD, DM (Cardiology) | Registered Medical Practitioner, [Council] Reg. No. [____] | General health education, not personal medical advice. In an emergency call 108.`

**1. The complex-case post**

- *Promotional:* "Another challenging case! Successfully opened a 100% blocked artery (CTO) in a 62-year-old diabetic patient using the latest techniques at Apollo. Before and after images below. Grateful to my team. #CTO #BestCardiologistHyderabad"
- *What is wrong:* publishes a case for promotion and advertises a result (8.1(v)); before/after (6.2); "latest" (8.1(ix)); employer promoted (8.2(iii)); superlative hashtag; patient details; boasts of a case (Reg. 6.1.1).
- *Compliant:* "A completely blocked heart artery does not always need to be opened. Three questions cardiologists ask first: Is the heart muscle beyond the block still alive? Are symptoms limiting daily life despite medicines? Do the likely benefits outweigh the procedure's risks for this person? When the answers are no, medicines and risk-factor control are the treatment. When they are yes, angioplasty or bypass may be discussed, and each has risks your doctor should explain. Diagram: how a chronic total occlusion forms and how collateral vessels work. Sources: [current ESC/ACC chronic coronary syndrome guideline]." + credential line.

**2. The grateful-patient video**

- *Promotional:* A 40-second clip of a patient's daughter: "My father had a massive heart attack. Dr Avinash saved his life. We are so thankful. Best doctor!" Caption: "Words like these keep us going."
- *What is wrong:* testimonial, barred even with consent (6.2); sharing it (Expl. V); permitting a patient to promote him (8.1(xi)); "saved", "best"; confirms a patient relationship.
- *Compliant:* No patient at all. "Heart attack: what a family can do in the first 10 minutes. 1. Call 108. Do not drive the patient yourself if an ambulance can reach you. 2. Have them sit, loosen clothing, keep them still. 3. If they are not allergic and a doctor has not told them to avoid it, an adult aspirin may be advised by the emergency service. 4. If they collapse and are not breathing normally, start chest compressions and ask for an AED. 5. Take previous reports and a list of medicines. Every minute matters because heart muscle is being lost." Telugu version alongside. + credential line. (The family's gratitude is answered in person.)

**3. The camp announcement**

- *Promotional:* "FREE Heart Check-up Camp this Sunday at [Hospital], Financial District! ECG, sugar and consultation with Dr Avinash absolutely free. Angiogram at 50% off for camp patients. Limited slots, call 98xxxxxxx to book."
- *What is wrong:* "free procedures", discounts, limited-period offer (8.1(x)); solicitation and phone number (8.1(ii)); promotes employer; creates demand for a procedure (8.1(i)).
- *Compliant:* "World Hypertension Day, 17 May: many adults with high blood pressure do not know they have it [add a sourced Indian figure before publishing]. Get a reading this week at any primary health centre, Basti Dawakhana, pharmacy with a validated monitor, or at home. Sit for five minutes, feet flat, arm supported, two readings a minute apart. If the average is 140/90 or above on two different days, see a doctor near you. A high reading on one day is not a diagnosis." If he joins a screening event, it is one run by a government body, society or NGO, announced by them, with no packages. + credential line.

**4. The milestone post**

- *Promotional:* "Proud to share that I have completed 2,300 angiographies and 150 primary angioplasties for heart attacks with excellent outcomes. Thank you to my patients for their trust. Onwards!"
- *What is wrong:* advertises personal achievements and number of patients treated (8.1(iii)); "excellent outcomes" is an unverifiable success claim; subject is him (3.2, Reg. 6.1.1).
- *Compliant:* "Six questions people ask before an angiogram. 1. Why is it being advised, and is there a non-invasive alternative for me? 2. Wrist or groin, and why? 3. What are the risks (bleeding, kidney effects of dye, rarely stroke or heart attack) and how common are they? 4. What happens if a block is found: will a stent be placed in the same sitting, and can I decide after seeing the result? 5. How long will I stay? 6. Which medicines should I stop or continue beforehand? Take this list to whichever cardiologist you see." + credential line.

**5. The newspaper story**

- *Promotional (hospital press release):* "Doctors at [Hospital], led by renowned interventional cardiologist Dr Avinash Pathuri, saved a 34-year-old software engineer who suffered a massive heart attack, performing a complex angioplasty within the golden hour using state-of-the-art technology. The patient was discharged in three days."
- *What is wrong:* publication of a case report in the lay press (Reg. 6.1.1); "news articles intended for promotion" (3.5); "renowned", "state-of-the-art"; patient identifiable by age, job and timing (7.4); promotional portrayal of an individual RMP (8.3(ii)).
- *Compliant:* He declines to be named in the release and instead offers the newspaper an op-ed under his own name: "Why heart attacks in the thirties are no longer rare in Hyderabad's tech corridor, and the five things that lower the risk." Content: what the Indian data show on premature coronary disease; smoking and vaping, untreated cholesterol, diabetes, sleep loss, family history; why chest discomfort lasting more than a few minutes needs an ECG the same night; why "gas" is the commonest wrong self-diagnosis; an illustrative, labelled composite scenario; what employers can do (AEDs, CPR training). Byline: "The writer is an interventional cardiologist in Hyderabad." No hospital facility talk, no case, no numbers about himself. + credential line where the outlet permits.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Treating interpretation as text.** Sections marked (b) are my readings. The friend/AI position, per-post disclosure, employer naming, monetisation and reviews are all unconfirmed until a lawyer or the council answers.
- **Relying on the Guidelines' permissions against the 2002 Regulations.** Where they differ, the Regulations win (4.2), and Reg. 6.1.1 and 7.11 are stricter. Stay in the overlap.
- **Passive breach through the hospitals.** The likeliest violation is one he never authored: a reel title, a hoarding, a press note, a review quote. Clause 11.5 reaches participation.
- **The helper's own accounts.** An influencer friend recommending him is the textbook "third party as an intermediary". The rule is simple: the friend may share the teaching, never the teacher's merits.
- **Scale and cadence.** High-frequency, multi-platform output can look like a "promotional campaign" even when each item is clean. Keep volume at a level he can read personally; the log must show real approval.
- **Procedure content.** Explainers on angioplasty, devices and structural interventions are where "public health" shades into "treatments which may have the effect of advertising himself". Always include who does not need it, risks and alternatives.
- **Composite cases that are not composite.** A "composite" that is one real patient with the name removed is patient data. Build them from features, not from memory of one person.
- **Metadata.** Titles, thumbnails, hashtags, alt text and playlist names are part of the post. Hospital and agency SEO habits put superlatives there.
- **AI drift.** A model asked to "improve engagement" will add hooks from the red-flag list. Instructions to the tool must describe an education programme and include the word list as hard exclusions.
- **Unverified facts about himself.** Inconsistent experience years, procedure counts and an undocumented award are the kind of "unverifiable" claim councils act on, and TGMC's record is strongest on qualifications and registration.
- **Assuming silence means safety.** No enforcement has been found yet. The first cases will define the unsettled points; he should not be one of them.
