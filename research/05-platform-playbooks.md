# Platform-by-platform playbooks for a doctor in 2026 (India / Telangana / Telugu + English)

**Executive summary**

1. Every major feed (LinkedIn, X, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, Threads) is now an interest-based AI recommender, not a follower feed. What matters is topic clarity, time spent on the piece, and private shares/saves/replies; hashtags, posting-time tricks and follower counts matter far less than vendor blogs claim.
2. In India the public lives on YouTube (573M ad reach), Instagram (515M), Facebook (400M) and WhatsApp (87.7% of internet users); LinkedIn (210M members, +23.5% in a year) holds peers, HR/corporate buyers and policymakers; X is tiny (22.9M) but holds journalists and the global cardiology community.
3. YouTube is the only platform with a formal, licence-verified "health source" programme open to individual Indian doctors (form exists at health.youtube), but it requires 1,500 public watch hours in 12 months or 1.5M Shorts views in 90 days first, so it is a month-6-to-12 milestone, not a day-1 action.
4. Recommended portfolio: **Primary = YouTube (Telugu-first, with English)**; **Secondary = LinkedIn (English, written) and a WhatsApp Channel (Telugu/English, patient distribution)**; Instagram/Facebook Reels and X run as helper-operated syndication; everything else is claimed and parked.
5. One interviewer-led 45-60 minute recording per week (he only answers questions) can feed every channel; his time is about 2.5 hours/week, with a helper/AI doing 5-7 hours. He must personally approve every medical statement before it is published.

**Method note and a limitation.** Research was done on 12 November 2026. Web search for the whole research session ran out part-way through this topic (a shared cap), so the later sections rely on direct fetches of official pages and of articles already surfaced, not on fresh searches. Where I could not verify something, it says so. Most "algorithm" numbers on the web come from tool vendors (SocialPilot, vidIQ, Hootsuite, Buffer, Metricool); I have separated what platforms themselves published from what vendors claim. Confidence tags: **High** = platform's own document read directly; **Medium** = credible secondary source or consistent vendor data; **Low** = single vendor claim, inference, or prior knowledge not re-checked this session.

### 2. Platform playbooks

#### 2.1 LinkedIn

**How ranking works (official).** LinkedIn's engineering blog (12 March 2026) describes a rebuilt feed: one retrieval system using fine-tuned LLM embeddings to match members to posts by meaning (not keywords), and a transformer "Generative Recommender" that reads a member's last 1,000+ interactions as a sequence. Signals named: long dwells, likes, comments, shares, clicks, skips, the viewer's profile (industry, skills, geography), the post's engagement percentile, and affinity to the author. The feed now explicitly surfaces posts "from authors you don't follow" when they match interests, and new posts are indexed within minutes. https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/engineering-the-next-generation-of-linkedins-feed (**High**). Background papers: 360Brew https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16450v1 and LiGR https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03417v1 .

**What that means in practice.** The model understands what a post is about. A cardiologist who posts consistently about a narrow set of topics (heart attack care, prevention, cath-lab decisions) becomes easy to match to people interested in them. Topic drift hurts.

**Third-party data (treat as directional).**
- Richard van der Blom's Algorithm Insights 2025 (1.8M posts): views down about 47%, engagement down about 39% year on year; saves strongly raise distribution; fully AI-written posts got about 2.8x less reach. Reported via SocialPilot https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm (**Low-Medium**; not read at source).
- LinkedIn added Saves and Sends to post analytics in late 2025 (same source, **Medium**).
- External links reduce reach (about 60% per one vendor; **Low**). Safer practice: put links in the first comment or in a newsletter edition.
- Hootsuite reports LinkedIn confirmed in mid-2025 that posts up to 2-3 weeks old can resurface when relevant, and that "LinkedIn data shows" weekly posting gives a 2x engagement lift https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-algorithm/ (**Medium**).

**Formats, length, cadence.**
- Text post with one image (an ECG strip, a diagram, a photo from the cath lab with no patient identifiers): 150-300 words, first two lines carry the hook because the rest sits behind "see more". Document carousels (PDF, 8-12 slides) for explainers. Native vertical video under 3 minutes (reuse the Shorts).
- Cadence: 2 posts a week is enough. Third-party guidance ranges 2-5 a week (SocialPilot, **Low**).
- Hashtags: 0-3; they are no longer a discovery mechanism of any weight (**Medium**, consistent across vendor sources and with the LLM-retrieval design).
- Comments: reply to every substantive comment in the first hours; the engineering post lists comments and dwell among the sequence signals. One marketer's analysis claims top-1% creators reply far more than average (**Low**).
- Timing: Buffer 2026 (about 5M LinkedIn posts, local time): 3-6 pm Wed-Fri best https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-social-media/ ; Sprout (Mar 2026): 11 am-5 pm Tue-Thu https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-social-media/ . Neither is India-specific. Practical India slot: Tue and Thu, 8:00-9:00 am or 5:00-6:00 pm IST, then test (**Low**, inference).

**Features status in 2026.**
- **Creator mode:** the toggle was removed in March 2024; its tools (follow button, newsletters, LinkedIn Live, analytics) are available without it. Reported by a glossary quoting LinkedIn Help https://contentin.io/glossary/creator-mode/ (**Medium**; the LinkedIn Help page I tried returned 404).
- **Top Voice:** only the blue badge exists. The gold "Community Top Voice" badge was retired on 8 Oct 2024 (Buffer https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-top-voice/ ). The blue badge is invitation-only, chosen by LinkedIn's editorial team; criteria reported as platform presence, quality and originality, subject expertise, trust/professionalism, prominence in field; reviewed twice a year; a nomination form exists and LinkedIn suggests no more than one nomination every six months (Metricool, Apr 2026 https://metricool.com/top-voices-linkedin/ , **Medium**). It is not something to plan around; it follows from 12+ months of consistent expert posting.
- **Newsletter:** LinkedIn Help says members who meet access criteria can create one; on the first edition "your connections and followers will be invited by notification to subscribe", and subscribers get a notification or email for each edition https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a517925 (**High**). Third parties cite roughly 150 followers as the threshold (**Low**). This built-in invitation makes a LinkedIn newsletter the cheapest way to start an email-like list.

**Doctor fit.** High for an introvert: it is written, asynchronous, and the friend who will run the system already knows the platform. Audience: peers, referring physicians, hospital leadership, HR/wellness buyers, policymakers.

#### 2.2 X (Twitter)

**How ranking works (official).** X publishes its For You code at https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm (README last updated 26 Oct 2026). Key facts read directly from the README (**High**):
- Candidates come from accounts you follow (Thunder) and from accounts you do not (Phoenix retrieval, SimClusters, popular posts). One model (Phoenix) ranks both.
- The score is a weighted sum of predicted probabilities for: like, reply, repost, quote, share, share via DM, copy-link share, clicks (post, profile, link, photo, video), dwell and dwell time, video continuation, profile-visit time, follow-author; negatives: not interested, mute, block, report, "not dwelled".
- Out-of-network posts are discounted; a new-author boost lifts authors with few impressions; each additional post from the same author in one feed is multiplied by a decaying factor (so volume has diminishing returns); posts older than 48 hours are filtered out.
- October 2026 updates: the model now better estimates time spent, and retrieval is trained to surface posts "people read through fully".
- The README does **not** mention a Premium ranking boost or a link penalty. Vendor blogs claim both (SocialPilot https://socialpilot.co/blog/twitter-algorithm ), and they contradict each other. Treat "links are penalised" and "Premium boosts reach" as unproven (**Low**).

Other reported changes (via SocialPilot citing Social Media Today / TechCrunch, **Medium**): from 30 Nov 2025 the Following feed is also ranked by default; in July 2026 X's head of product said ranking was being adjusted to show more posts from mutuals. Business Today's summary of the Jan 2026 release: https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/decoding-xs-algorithm-heres-how-grok-open-sources-and-curates-the-for-you-feed-511751-2026-01-20 .

**Verification / Premium for professionals.** There is no free professional verification. The blue checkmark comes with Premium or Premium+ after an account review; Basic has no checkmark. India prices after the July 2025 cut: Basic ₹170/month (web); Premium ₹427/month web, ₹470 mobile; Premium+ ₹2,570 web, ₹3,000 mobile (Business Today, 16 Jul 2025 https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/x-premium-is-now-up-to-48-cheaper-on-mobile-and-desktop-here-are-the-new-prices-484823-2025-07-16 ). Later pages show conflicting prices, and X's own help page blocked my fetch (HTTP 403), so check the price at checkout (**Medium**). Premium is worth it for a doctor mainly for the checkmark (impersonation defence) and long posts; buy on web, not in-app.

**Formats, length, cadence.**
- Peer content: "tweetorials" (8-15 post threads) on a trial or guideline, one image per post (figure, ECG, angiographic still, de-identified); or a single long post. Since the model rewards read-through and dwell, one well-structured long post or thread beats many short ones.
- Cadence: 3-5 posts a week plus replies. Vendors recommend 3-5 a day (**Low**); the author-diversity decay in the code means that is wasted effort for a time-poor doctor.
- Hashtags: use the community tags on peer content only (#CardioTwitter / #CardioX, #MedTwitter, #ACC26, #ESCCongress). They work as community search labels, not as ranking levers (**Low**, inference).
- Timing: Buffer 2026 (8.7M posts): weekdays 8-11 am local, best Tue-Wed https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-social-media/ . For global cardiology peers, 6:30-8:30 pm IST overlaps Europe's afternoon and the US morning (inference).

**What the evidence says about cardiologists on X.** In a study of 1,500 cardiologists at the top 100 US hospitals, only 16.3% had a Twitter handle; follower counts tracked posting activity, not citation counts; the most "over-followed" group had a median 5,083 followers and posted a median 392 times in 12 months (Eur Heart J Digital Health 2021; data 2019-20, so **stale**) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9708001 . The #TweetTheJournal randomised work found X promotion raised Altmetric attention but did not significantly change citations https://herzmedizin.de/congress/abstract/99476 (**Medium**). So X builds peer visibility and conference relationships, not citations, and a few thousand engaged followers is already a top-tier presence for a cardiologist.

**Doctor fit.** Medium. Small Indian audience, combative culture, but it is where health journalists look for quotable experts and where trial debates happen in real time. Best run as a low-volume, high-signal peer channel with the friend's help.

#### 2.3 YouTube (long-form and Shorts)

**How ranking works (official).**
- YouTube says recommendations use "viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and feedback, including from satisfaction surveys", plus "the reputation and the quality of a channel"; for medical and scientific topics it will "elevate high-quality voices"; search is ranked on "relevance, engagement and quality" https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/ (**High**).
- Todd Beaupré (YouTube's recommendations lead), Jan 2025: the system is "pulling" videos for a viewer rather than pushing them out; satisfaction signals include likes, dislikes and survey responses; context such as time of day and device matters; an old video can become "relevant again" months later; LLM technology is used in recommendations https://searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379 (**Medium-High**).
- Shorts: up to 3 minutes; since 31 March 2025 a Shorts "view" counts whenever the Short starts or replays; the older stricter metric is now "engaged views" https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en (**High**). Read "engaged views", not "views", when judging Shorts.
- Vendor heuristics (vidIQ, June 2026, **Low**): click-through under 4% suggests a weak title/thumbnail; retention above 50% is good; Shorts are ranked on watched-versus-swiped, replays and shares https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-algorithm/ .

**Why this matters for a doctor.** YouTube is search-and-pull. A clear Telugu video titled with the question patients actually type ("గుండెపోటు లక్షణాలు", "stent or bypass", "angiogram తర్వాత జాగ్రత్తలు") keeps getting found for years. Upload time and frequency matter much less than on feed platforms.

**Formats, length, cadence.**
- Long-form: 6-12 minutes, one question per video, answer in the first 30 seconds, chapters, spoken in plain Telugu (or English) with on-screen diagrams. One a week.
- Shorts: 30-60 seconds, one fact or myth, burned-in captions. 3-5 a week, cut from the long recording.
- SEO over hashtags: title, description, spoken words (auto-transcribed) and chapters carry discovery. 2-3 hashtags at most.
- Timing (Buffer 2026): long-form best Sunday 9-10 am and mornings 8-11 am; Shorts best Friday and weekday evenings 6-11 pm, local time https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-social-media/ (**Medium**, not India-specific).

**Telugu and dubbing (official).** YouTube automatic dubbing supports Telugu to English (with "expressive speech") and English to Telugu (without it). Dubs can be set to "Publish manually" for review, cannot be edited (you fix the original and regenerate), are labelled "auto-dubbed", and YouTube warns that proper nouns, idioms and jargon may be translated poorly https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15569972?hl=en (**High**). Auto-dubbing was opened to all channels in February 2026 (India TV https://www.indiatvnews.com/technology/news/youtube-auto-dubbing-feature-expands-to-all-users-now-supports-27-languages-2026-02-05-1029070 , **Medium**). Implication: record in Telugu and let YouTube add an English track (reviewed by him before publishing), or record both takes. Do not rely on machine English-to-Telugu for medical terms.

**YouTube Health: licensed-doctor verification in India.**
- Announced for India on 9-10 Dec 2024: registered doctors, nurses and mental-health professionals can apply; approved channels get a health source information panel under videos and inclusion in health content shelves in search. Health content partnerships cover 140+ conditions in 10 languages including Telugu. Named institutional partners include Apollo Hospitals https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/new-ways-for-registered-healthcare-professionals-in-india-to-reach-people-on-youtube/ and https://telanganatoday.com/youtube-launches-new-feature-for-registered-health-professionals-to-reach-people (**High**).
- Eligibility (YouTube Help, read 12 Nov 2026): be a licensed doctor eligible to practise in the country; channel must follow monetisation policies; **more than 1,500 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months, or 1.5 million valid public Shorts views in the past 90 days**; channel primarily about health; no active Community Guidelines strikes; attest to health-information-sharing principles (CMSS). Licence verification is coordinated by LegitScript, a third party. Decision typically 1-2 months after review; features appear 2-3 months after acceptance; channels are reassessed periodically https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12796915?hl=en-GB (**High**).
- How to apply: https://health.youtube/features-application/ lists an "India (English)" form at https://services.google.com/fb/forms/youtubehealthapp-in-en/ ; one application per channel (**High** that the form link exists; I did not open or submit it).
- Two caveats. (a) The Help page's own country list for applications does not name India even though the application site does; (b) the application site carries a notice: "We are currently updating our Health Features Program." Re-check both pages on the day of applying (**Medium** on current availability).
- Exactly which Indian document is accepted (state medical council registration number vs NMC Indian Medical Register entry) is not stated anywhere I could read. Could not verify.
- Watch-hour arithmetic: 1,500 hours is 90,000 minutes. At an average view duration of 3.5 minutes that is about 25,700 views in 12 months, or about 540 views per video across 48 weekly videos. This is a realistic first-year target for a Telugu cardiology channel. I assume Shorts-feed watch time does not count toward the hours, as with the Partner Programme; could not verify.
- Shortcut: accredited hospitals already in the programme do not need to apply, and Apollo is a named partner. Appearing in videos on Apollo's own channel would place him under an existing health-source panel. Whether the Hyderabad unit's channel carries the panel was not verified.

**Medical misinformation policy (official).** YouTube removes content contradicting local health authority guidance on prevention and treatment of specific conditions: harmful "cures", vaccine safety/efficacy/ingredient falsehoods, unapproved cancer treatments or claims that approved ones never work, guaranteed cures. It applies to video, description, comments and links, and to AI-generated content. Discussing a specific study's results, condemning or debunking a false claim, and personal experience are allowed with context. First violation is usually a warning; repeat within 90 days is a strike https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13813322?hl=en (**High**). Relevance: myth-busting videos are safe if the false claim is clearly framed as false in the title, spoken audio and description.

**Doctor fit.** High, provided the format is interviewer-led or screen-led (see section 5). It is the only platform where one piece of work keeps teaching for years.

#### 2.4 Instagram (Reels and carousels)

**How ranking works.** Instagram's official explainer (May 2023) says each surface has its own ranking: Feed weighs your activity, post popularity and speed of engagement, and your history with the author; Reels weighs your activity, history with the author, and the reel's audio/visual signals and popularity. Content against the Recommendations Guidelines stays up but is not recommended; Account Status shows eligibility https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained (**High**, but 2023).

Adam Mosseri's later statements as reported by Buffer (2026 guide): the three most important signals are **watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach**; likes matter slightly more for followers, sends for non-followers; "hashtags don't help reach" and are capped at five; accounts that mostly repost are not recommended to non-followers (since April 2026); "Your Algorithm" topic controls launched December 2025; trial reels show a reel to non-followers first https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/ (**Medium**; some vendor blogs claim likes now have near-zero weight, which I could not confirm).

**Formats.** Buffer's analysis of 4M+ posts: carousels earn more engagement per person reached than Reels; Reels get about 36% more reach (same source, **Medium**). So Reels to be discovered, carousels to be saved and sent. For a doctor: Reels are the YouTube Shorts re-exported without watermark; carousels are the LinkedIn document re-cut to 4:5 with 6-10 slides ("5 signs your chest pain needs an ECG today").

**Cadence and timing.** 3 Reels and 1 carousel a week is plenty; spread posts out rather than posting several in a row (Buffer). Timing: Buffer says 9 am Thursday and around 6 pm; Sprout says midday to evening Tue-Wed. Not India-specific (**Low**). Use keywords in captions and profile; 3-5 specific hashtags at most.

**Health-specific rules.** Meta's Recommendations Guidelines limit recommendation of some health content. From prior knowledge these include exaggerated health claims such as "miracle cures", selling products on health claims, cosmetic-procedure promotion and regulated products such as pharmaceutical drugs; the official explainer confirms "regulated products like tobacco, vaping products, and pharmaceutical drugs". I could not load the guidelines page (https://help.instagram.com/313829416281232) or Meta's misinformation standard (https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/misinformation/) this session, so treat the detailed list as **Low** until re-read. Practical reading: naming drug brands, before/after framing and graphic procedural footage risk reduced reach.

**Doctor fit.** Medium-low for him personally (trend-driven, personality-led), but nearly free as a syndication target because the Shorts already exist. Press reporting on Indian health creators shows the format pressure clearly: 15-30 second reels, trending audio, bold claims (ThePrint, Aug 2025 https://theprint.in/ground-reports/indian-health-influencers-whatsapp-university-fact-checkers/2709818 ). He should not play that game; calm authority is the differentiator.

#### 2.5 Facebook

- Ranking: AI-recommended content from accounts you do not follow is now a large share of the feed (one analysis: 36.6% of US feed views, Apr-Jun 2026), and Meta announced in March 2026 that Feed and Reels prioritise original content and limit unedited reposts https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/413530/ and https://socialpilot.co/blog/facebook-algorithm (**Medium**; Meta's own pages not read).
- India: 400M ad reach, flat to slightly declining, 72% male (DataReportal). Age breakdown not retrieved; the common view that Facebook skews older in India is plausible but unverified here.
- Play: a professional Page (also needed for Meta Business Suite scheduling), cross-post every Reel from Instagram with one click, post the Telugu long video natively once a week, and share into relevant local/community groups only when asked. No separate creative effort.
- Timing (Buffer 2026): weekdays 8 am-noon. Sprout: midday Mon-Thu.
- Doctor fit: syndication only. Zero doctor minutes.

#### 2.6 WhatsApp Channels and Communities

- Scale: 87.7% of Indian internet users 16+ use WhatsApp monthly (GWI via DataReportal). WhatsApp says the Updates tab (Channels + Status) is used by 1.5 billion people a day globally (WhatsApp blog, 16 Jun 2025 https://blog.whatsapp.com/helping-you-find-more-channels-and-businesses-on-whatsapp , **High**).
- How it works: a Channel is one-way broadcast; there is no interest-ranking feed like Instagram's, so reach equals followers who open the Updates tab plus forwards. Growth levers reported: forwards carry a link back to the channel, QR codes, sharing updates to Status, directory listing, and (since June 2025) paid "promoted channels" and optional paid channel subscriptions. Admins see 30-day metrics. Sources: WhatsApp blog above for promoted channels and subscriptions; the other features come from search-result summaries of news coverage (for example Telangana Today https://telanganatoday.com/whatsapp-rolls-out-new-features-for-group-chats-events-calls-and-channels , which I did not open). I could not load WhatsApp's own help pages, so feature details are **Low-Medium**.
- Why it suits a doctor: followers' phone numbers are not shown to each other or (by design at launch) to the admin, which avoids the privacy and boundary problems of patient WhatsApp groups (**Medium**, from launch coverage and prior knowledge; verify in-app). It is where Telugu families already forward health content, so a verified-source channel competes directly with "WhatsApp University".
- Play: one Channel, bilingual (Telugu first). 2-3 updates a week: the week's Short (uploaded natively, under 60 seconds), one text card, one link to the long video. Grow it offline: QR code on the OPD desk, prescription pad, discharge summary and visiting card; link in YouTube descriptions and Google Business Profile.
- Communities/groups: use for peers only (a referring-physician group with an ECG-of-the-week, or existing cardiology groups). Do not run patient groups: numbers are exposed, and questions become individual medical advice.
- Timing: no data found. Suggest 7:00-8:00 am or 8:00-9:00 pm IST and compare 30-day reach (**Low**).
- Doctor fit: High. Zero performance, zero comments to manage, helper-run.

#### 2.7 Threads

- 40.4M ad reach in India, up 52% in a year (DataReportal). Ranking predicts replies, profile clicks and follows; Instagram activity feeds into it; content must meet Instagram's recommendation rules. Buffer's analysis of 128,000 posts found replying to comments lifted engagement about 42% https://buffer.com/resources/threads-algorithm/ (**Medium**; Meta's own Threads ranking page returned 404).
- Play: claim the handle through Instagram, let the helper cross-post the LinkedIn text once or twice a week. No doctor time. Park.

#### 2.8 Personal newsletter (LinkedIn newsletter / Substack / beehiiv)

- LinkedIn newsletter: built-in distribution (connections and followers are invited on the first edition; subscribers are notified or emailed each edition) https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a517925 (**High**). Downside: LinkedIn owns the list; no email export (prior knowledge, **Low**).
- beehiiv: free plan up to 2,500 subscribers with unlimited sends, website, analytics; paid from $49-59/month https://www.beehiiv.com/pricing (**High**).
- Substack: free to publish; whether Indian writers can currently take paid subscriptions (it depends on Stripe) could not be verified; Substack's support page returned 403. Not relevant unless he wants paid subscribers, which he should not.
- Play: a monthly LinkedIn newsletter (600-900 words, e.g. "one case, one trial, one myth"), mirrored on his own website for search (see 2.11). Add an owned email list (beehiiv free) only when there is a reason to email patients or peers directly. Open rate and growth benchmarks for doctor newsletters in India: none found.

#### 2.9 Podcasting (own vs guesting)

- Could not retrieve 2025-26 India podcast audience data this session.
- Guest slots exist in Telugu: "The Health Expert" (Telugu health podcast hosted by Hareesh Dachepalli, on Apple Podcasts https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/id1848842886 ), TGV Telugu (an episode with an interventional cardiologist on heart myths listed on Castbox as episode TGVT171 with Dr Mukharjee Madivada; the show's YouTube handle is given as @tgvtelugu; I did not open either page), and hospital podcasts such as CARE Hospitals' https://www.carehospitals.com/ms/podcast-detail/understanding-heart-health-with-dr-srinivasa-rao-maddury (**Medium**; audience sizes not verified).
- Recommendation: guest, do not host. A guest appearance costs 60-90 minutes, borrows someone else's audience, and yields clips. Target one every 6-8 weeks. An "own podcast" can later be created at no extra effort by publishing the audio of the weekly YouTube interview as a feed (Riverside's Pro plan at $29/month includes hosting https://riverside.com/pricing ), but it is not a priority.

#### 2.10 Quora and Reddit

- Reddit's reported India ad reach (170M) is distorted by reporting changes (DataReportal). No reliable 2026 India data for Quora was retrieved.
- Both are anonymous Q&A environments where answers easily become individual medical advice, and neither builds a named reputation efficiently.
- Play: claim the names; do not post. Use them as listening posts: the helper/AI mines r/india, city subreddits and Telugu Quora for the questions people actually ask about heart disease and feeds them into the video topic list. A one-off verified "AMA" can be considered after year one.

#### 2.11 Medium / blog and Google E-E-A-T

- Google's guidance (read 12 Nov 2026): systems give extra weight to experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust for topics that "could significantly impact the health, financial stability, or safety of people" (YMYL); "trust is most important"; E-E-A-T "itself isn't a specific ranking factor"; Google "strongly encourage[s] adding accurate authorship information, such as bylines" that lead to information about the author; YMYL content must be accurate and consistent with expert consensus; mass AI text without oversight is low-effort; disclose AI use where a reader would wonder https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content (**High**).
- Implication: the search asset is a personal website on his own domain with a real author page (degrees, registration number, hospital affiliations, publications, talks), and articles carrying his byline and a "medically reviewed/updated on" date. Each YouTube video gets a transcript-based article there. AI can draft from his recording; he reviews; the page says so.
- Medium: no advantage over his own domain for a YMYL author. Claim the handle and park. If used, republish with a canonical link to his site.

### 5. Production minimums

**Format that suits an introvert.** Interviewer-led recording: the friend or helper asks 6-8 pre-agreed patient questions; he answers as he would in clinic. No script, no performance, no talking to an empty lens. Alternative for peer content: screen recording of an ECG or angiographic still with voice-over.

**Equipment (indicative Indian street prices; my estimates, not verified this session).** Existing smartphone on a tripod (₹1,000-2,500); wireless lavalier microphone (₹2,000-6,000 budget, ₹15,000-25,000 premium); one soft LED panel (₹1,500-4,000); quiet room with a plain or bookshelf background. ## Risks & things to avoid

- **Patient privacy.** No identifiable patient in any image, ECG, angiogram, cath-lab clip or story without written consent; remove burned-in names, dates, hospital numbers; alter non-essential case details. India's data-protection and professional-conduct obligations apply (see the regulatory research file; not researched here).
- **Advertising and solicitation rules for Indian doctors.** No patient testimonials, outcome boasts, "best cardiologist" claims, paid follower growth or engagement pods. Educational framing only. Verify exact current rules in the regulatory file.
- **Individual medical advice in public.** Comments, DMs, Quora and Reddit pull doctors into diagnosing strangers. Standard reply: general information only, see a doctor, emergency number. Keep the WhatsApp Channel one-way.
- **Misinformation-policy tripwires.** When debunking, state that the claim is false in the title, opening line and description so automated review reads it as correction, not promotion.
- **Brand deals.** Indian reporting is clear that "excessive brand collaborations can erode trust" (Dr Mithal). For a cardiologist, any pharma, device, supplement or diagnostics sponsorship is a conflict. Decline all in year one; disclose any institutional relationship.
- **Unreviewed AI output.** AI-drafted posts, auto-dubs and auto-captions will contain medical errors, especially in Telugu terminology. Nothing is published without his sign-off. Do not use voice clones or lip-sync avatars of him; if any AI dubbing is used, label it.
- **Trend-chasing.** Trending audio, shock hooks and daily posting are how low-credibility health creators win reach. They are also how a doctor loses peers' respect. Calm, specific and repeatable beats viral.
- **Platform dependence.** Reach on LinkedIn fell sharply in 2025 by third-party measures; X policies change monthly; YouTube's health programme is "being updated". The website and the WhatsApp/email lists are the owned assets.
- **Vendor "algorithm secrets".** Specific multipliers (a reply is worth 150 likes, links cut reach 60-80%) are unverified and often contradicted by the platforms' own documents. Do not build rules on them.
- **X-specific.** Pile-ons and impersonation. Keep X for peer science; do not argue with alternative-medicine accounts; use the paid checkmark if impersonation appears.
- **Over-extension.** More than one primary and two secondaries will break a 2-3 hour budget. Parked channels stay parked for 12 months.
