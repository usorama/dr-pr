# 24 — The three lighthouse flagships made executable: Heart School, Patients for life, and the open teaching series

**Research date:** 12 November 2026. Builds on files 11, 15, 16, 18 and 19; does not repeat their evidence tables.

## Executive summary

1. **Heart School should be a measured 12-week cohort of about 50 people in pods of 8–10, not a lecture series.** Every Indian precedent says the same thing: D-CLIP cut diabetes by a third but 72% of participants needed metformin as well; the Kerala peer programme cost US$22.5 a head and missed its primary endpoint; INDIA-WORKS (2,108 employees, 11 worksites; registry results read this session) lost 36% of participants and saw its year-1 gains fade by year 2. The design therefore fixes measurement at entry and at 3, 6 and 12 months, a written route to the participant's **own** doctor, a nurse or coach for follow-up, and a family partner.
2. **Patients for life is the flagship with the best evidence and the lowest regulatory risk, and it should start first.** Nurse-led follow-up with authority to trigger drug titration lowered LDL-C by 0.28 mmol/L at 36 months (NAILED-ACS) and deaths at 4.7 years (Grampian clinics, HR 0.75). Education, pharmacist counselling or text reminders without titration mostly did not move LDL or blood pressure. The design is a one-page passport, a doctor-verified Telugu explanation, calls at day 3/30/90/180/365, and a 40-field registry built in his **own** practice, where he is the data fiduciary.
3. **The teaching series fills a real gap, but real cases cannot go on open YouTube.** The largest open Indian channel for DM/DrNB trainees (26,100 subscribers, with a Telegram group) stopped uploading about three years ago; the CCDC Masterclass is at its 79th session but lists a pharmaceutical supporter. Under the consent tiers in file 11, live sessions with real cases must sit behind a doctor-only registration wall; the public layer is an atlas of published or redrawn cases.
4. **Money and credits are the two traps.** A paid public course is not addressed by the NMC guidelines and is probably taxable at 18% GST (a Karnataka advance ruling taxed paid online courses for doctors); keep year 1 free, with laboratories paid directly by hosts. Telangana Medical Council CPD credit is built for in-person events (live photograph and location of each delegate; about half a credit hour for two hours), so accredit a quarterly half-day, not the weekly session.
5. **Each flagship has one metric, a 12-week pilot, and a list of what is not promised.** Heart School: share of enrolled people with a raised number who are at target at 12 months. Patients for life: LDL-C below 55 mg/dL at 12 months, missing counted as not at goal. Teaching: trainees attending at least half the sessions in a quarter. Total cash for all three pilots is about Rs 1.0–1.3 lakh excluding laboratory tests and ethics-committee fees (my estimate), and about 4 hours a week of his time if the pilots are staggered.

### A3. "Mini-med school", faith and community-worker models, and WhatsApp delivery

- **Mini-med schools** (university lecture series for the public): a Canadian programme improved 7 of 9 health-literacy scales six weeks later; 63.6% of participants preferred virtual seminars. No study measured a biomarker. Use the format for reach and literacy only (PMID 33349756, 37465752; High).
- **Faith and community settings:** the FAITH trial trained lay health advisers to run 11 weekly group sessions plus motivational interviewing in 32 Black churches in New York (design paper PMID 24576512; High). Its result, as I recall it, was a net systolic fall of about 5.8 mm Hg at 6 months that was not maintained at 9 months **[BK, Medium]**. "Lifestyle Africa" adapted the DPP for delivery by community health workers in a Cape Town township and lowered HbA1c (PMID 35427357; High). File 19 records the barbershop trial (−21.6 mm Hg with a pharmacist prescribing on site) and the Kerala self-help-group trial (−4.09 mm Hg). The gradient is clear: the more the setting includes someone who can change treatment, the larger the effect.
- **WhatsApp in India:** the only randomised trial found of WhatsApp messaging for a cardiovascular risk factor was null (follow-up attendance 21.8% vs 19.6%; myths not corrected) (PMID 39245777; High). A small trial in type 1 diabetes found weekly educational WhatsApp messages no better than weekly greeting messages for HbA1c, though knowledge improved (conference abstract, PMC9067837; Low). A WhatsApp-led hypertension study in Kerala (240 participants) has finished data collection; results were expected in mid-2026 and were **not found** (PMID 42008756). Text messages did cut diabetes incidence in Indian men at high risk (HR 0.64; file 19). **No trial of a WhatsApp peer group as the intervention was found.** WhatsApp is therefore the pipe for reminders and pod contact, not the programme.
- **Coaching by non-doctors:** COACH (Australia; 792 coronary patients **[BK for the number]**) used telephone coaching by nurses and dietitians and lowered total cholesterol by 21 vs 7 mg/dL (PMID 14662633; High). An Indian insurer's health-coaching programme has a retrospective cohort study only (PMID 41788108; Medium). **No statutory Indian training standard for a "health coach" was found.** The US National Board for Health and Wellness Coaching appears in Indian search results only through one vendor's own claims (Weljii); a Healthcare Sector Skill Council link is likewise a provider claim (Low). Indian diabetes-educator certificates exist **[BK]** but were not verified.

## B. Heart School: design choices and their sources

### B1. Curriculum: eight sessions on "Know your numbers"

Why eight weekly sessions plus boosters: the DPP family runs 13–16 core sessions and loses most people (19% completion in England; 64% in INDIA-WORKS); K-DPP ran 15 sessions over a year. A shorter core raises completion; the monthly boosters carry the 12-month measurement, which is where INDIA-WORKS and Omada show decay.

| # | Session (45 min: 15 teach, 10 skill, 15 pod, 5 action) | Number recorded | Family task | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why Indian hearts fail six years early; your five numbers and one lifetime test. **Measurement day** | All five, plus Lp(a) once | Tell one relative what you measured | INTERHEART South Asia; CSI 2024 (file 19) |
| 2 | Blood pressure: measuring at home properly; what each reading means; when it is urgent | 7-day home BP average | Measure a parent's or spouse's BP | Ettehad meta-analysis; self-monitoring works only with a co-intervention (file 19) |
| 3 | Cholesterol-years: LDL and non-HDL; what statins do and do not do; Lp(a) | LDL, non-HDL, target for your category | Ask about early heart disease in the family | CTT; Ference; CSI/LAI targets (file 19) |
| 4 | Sugar and the waist: prediabetes, HbA1c, what changes it | HbA1c, waist | Tape-measure the household | IDPP-1; D-CLIP |
| 5 | Tobacco in every form; alcohol; how quitting is actually done | Tobacco status, quit date if any | Family agrees a smoke-free home | Jha 2008; quitline data (file 19) |
| 6 | The plate: salt, oil, rice, fruit; the salt substitute and who must not use it | Salt habit, fruit and vegetable servings | Change one kitchen default | SSaSS; Indian salt trial; K-DPP diet result |

Boosters: one 30-minute session a month from month 4 to month 12, with re-measurement at months 3, 6 and 12.

Content rules carried over from files 11 and 19: targets are quoted as "what Indian cardiology societies recommend"; classes of drug, never brands; composite scenarios, never patients; no fear imagery; the salt-substitute caveat for kidney disease every time.

### B3. The referral route and the solicitation conflict

The conflict is plain: a cardiologist who screens the public will find people who need a doctor, and he is one. The rules that bite are clause 5.1 (education must "not promote personal practice" or "solicit patients"), 8.1(ii) (no soliciting "directly or indirectly"), and 8.1(x) (no "free procedures or similar inducements ... likely to encourage unnecessary medical consultation, diagnostic investigation or treatment") (files 11 and 15, read from the NMC text).

Design, in order of importance:

1. **He does not consult at Heart School.** Measurement is done by the laboratory and a nurse. He teaches. No one-to-one advice is given in sessions or in WhatsApp pods (Telemedicine Practice Guidelines; file 18).
2. **Every participant gets a results letter addressed "To your doctor".** It lists the numbers, the CSI 2024 risk category by factor count, the society target for that category, and the sentence "This is a record of measurements, not a diagnosis or a prescription." It carries his name, qualifications and registration number as the programme's medical lead, and **no clinic name, address, phone or booking link**.
3. **Three named routes, none of them his:** the participant's own family physician; any physician or cardiologist of their choice; the nearest government non-communicable-disease clinic. A printed list of government facilities for the locality is attached. (The NP-NCD and Basti Dawakhana networks are the obvious entries **[BK]**; build the local list from the district health office.)
4. **A red-flag rule:** BP at or above 180/110 with symptoms, or chest pain or breathlessness on the day, goes to the nearest emergency department, whichever hospital that is.
5. **A closed loop without capture:** the nurse phones every high-risk participant at day 30 and records only "saw a doctor: yes or no" and "treatment started or changed: yes or no". Which doctor is not recorded, with one exception in point 6.
6. **A published firewall statement:** "The programme does not book, recommend or record appointments with its medical lead. A participant who chooses to consult him does so as any member of the public would. The number who did is reported each year." Counting and publishing that number is the strongest protection against the appearance of a funnel.
7. **If asked directly "can I come to you?"** the script is: "You are free to consult any doctor, including your own. This programme cannot make appointments."
8. **Free measurement is the amber point.** A free blood-pressure and laboratory check run in his name could be read as an inducement under 8.1(x). The safer structure is that the host's existing health-check vendor or an independent laboratory performs and bills the tests, and Heart School supplies teaching and follow-up only. Put this question to the lawyer (file 11's list).

Confidence: Medium. This is my reading of the clauses; no council interpretation exists yet (file 11).

### B4. Units, cohort size and delivery

- **Pod of 8–10 with a peer leader.** K-DPP ran groups under lay leaders and found the leaders benefited most; the Kerala self-help-group trial and the barbershop trial used existing social groups (file 19). Ask each pod to choose its own leader in session 1.
- **One family partner per participant,** enrolled by name. HOPE 4 used a "treatment supporter" and PROLIFIC the whole family (file 19).
- **Pilot size: 48–60 people (six pods).** Large enough to test recruitment and retention against INDIA-WORKS's 64%; small enough for one nurse.
- **Delivery: hybrid.** Measurement days in person at the host site (gated-community clubhouse or an office floor). Weekly sessions in person where the host can give a room, otherwise on a video call; 63.6% of mini-med-school participants preferred virtual. WhatsApp: one announcement-only community for the cohort and one small group per pod with written rules (no medical advice, no forwarding, no photographs of reports).
- **Gated community vs IT park.** A residential host reaches families and older parents and fits the family-partner design. An employer host reaches younger adults, can fund tests, and can change the canteen, but raises the confidentiality issues in B6. Run the pilot in a residential community, the second cohort with an employer.

### B5. Facilitators

- **Who can run follow-up:** in every positive trial the follower-up was not the doctor: lay peer leaders (K-DPP), non-physician health workers (PROLIFIC), community health workers (SPREAD), nurses and dietitians (COACH), village doctors with a protocol (CRHCP). A registered nurse, or a dietitian or physiotherapist, can do it.
- **Training standard (proposed, since no Indian standard was found):** a nurse registered with the state nursing council; 16 hours of in-house training (measurement technique, the eight scripts, motivational-interviewing basics, red flags, confidentiality); two observed sessions; a written scope: **measures, reminds, encourages, refers; does not diagnose, interpret for treatment, or advise on medicines.**
- **Peer leaders:** two 90-minute briefings (K-DPP trained its leaders; the exact hours were not retrieved).

### B7. Free or paid; tax

- **Rule:** clause 5.1 bars education that is "monetized through promotional marketing"; "monetized" and "promotional marketing" are undefined, and file 11 lists a paid public course among the questions no one has answered.
- **GST:** the health-care exemption covers "diagnosis or treatment or care" by a clinical establishment or authorised medical practitioner. The Karnataka Authority for Advance Ruling held that paid online courses for doctors are taxable even when a medical council mandates them, because they are not a medical facility (Medical Dialogues, Feb 2022; the order itself was not read). A paid public course would on that reasoning be an ordinary taxable service at 18% once his aggregate taxable turnover crosses the registration threshold **[BK: Rs 20 lakh for services]**. Public-health awareness appears in the definition of "charitable activities" for registered charitable entities, which is why a not-for-profit vehicle matters. Confidence: Medium; **for the chartered accountant and the lawyer.**
- **Recommendation:** year 1 free to participants; tests paid directly to the laboratory by the host or participant; his time unpaid. If scale needs money in year 2, the route is a registered not-for-profit (trust or Section 8 company) that charges hosts at cost and publishes accounts, not a fee to him. His Apollo contract's outside-activity clause must be checked either way (file 17).

### B9. Data protection and consent

- **Law:** the DPDP Act's notice, consent and penalty provisions start on 13 May 2027; until then the SPDI Rules 2011 treat health information as sensitive personal data needing consent (file 02). Section 17(2)(b) relaxes the Act for research only if no decision specific to the person is taken from the data and prescribed standards are met (file 16). Heart School does take person-specific decisions (the referral letter), so plan for full consent.
- **Practice:** collect the minimum (no Aadhaar, no employer ID, no address beyond locality); a study number on every record; the name-to-number key held by the nurse on one encrypted device; the helper and any AI tool see coded data only; laboratory reports go from the laboratory to the participant, with a copy to the programme only if the participant ticks that box; delete identifiers 12 months after the last follow-up.

Consent form outline (Telugu and English, one purpose per tick-box):

1. What Heart School is, who runs it, and that it is education and measurement, not treatment.
2. What will be measured and when; that blood tests are done by a named laboratory under its own terms.
3. What you will receive (your numbers and a letter for your doctor) and what you will not (diagnosis, prescription, appointment).
4. Risks: a needle prick; worry from an abnormal result; other pod members will know you take part.
5. Who sees your data: the nurse (identified); the medical lead (identified, for safety review); the evaluator and helper (coded only); your employer or residents' association (nothing individual, ever).
6. Separate tick-boxes: (a) follow-up calls and messages; (b) use of coded data for evaluation and publication; (c) a copy of laboratory results to the programme.
7. The right to leave, to withdraw data, and whom to contact; grievance route.
8. The statement that the medical lead earns nothing from the programme, from the laboratory, or from any participant's later choice of doctor.
9. Signature, date, witness; a copy to the participant.

### B10. What honest public reporting looks like

One page per cohort, published on his own site and sent to the host:

- **The funnel:** invited, enrolled, attended at least 6 sessions, re-measured at 3 months, re-measured at 12 months.
- **Entry numbers** and how many were raised.
- **Change,** labelled as change, with the sentence: "People chosen for high readings tend to have lower readings next time even with no programme; without a comparison group we cannot say how much of this change the programme caused."
- **At-target shares** with all enrolled as the denominator and with completers as the denominator, side by side.
- **Referral loop:** high-risk participants; share who saw a doctor in 30 days; number who chose to consult the medical lead.
- **Harms and complaints.**
- **What did not work** and what changes in the next cohort.
- **Money:** who paid for what.
- **No names, no quotes, no photographs of participants, no testimonials** (clause 6.2).

### Flagship 1 — Patients for life

**What it is.** A standing system in his own practice so that every patient with an acute coronary syndrome, angioplasty, bypass or heart failure leaves understanding their condition, is followed to target, and is counted.

**What every patient leaves with**

1. **Heart passport** (one page, Telugu and English): diagnosis, procedure and date, stent type, ejection fraction, medicines with one line on why each matters, five targets (LDL-C, BP, HbA1c, waist, tobacco), next review dates, red-flag symptoms and the emergency number.
2. **A plain-Telugu explanation** of their own condition, drafted by AI from de-identified structured fields, read, corrected and **signed by him** (section E).
3. **Medicine card** with the sentence "Do not stop any of these without speaking to us first", and the fewest pills that achieve the regimen (UMPIRE, SECURE).
4. **Teach-back** by the nurse before leaving: three questions (What is this tablet for? What would make you call us today? When is your next test?), answers recorded.
5. **Family note** for first-degree relatives where coronary disease appeared before 55 (men) or 60 (women): the five numbers and Lp(a), to be taken to the relatives' own doctors (PROLIFIC; file 19).
6. **Open-door line:** a second opinion is welcome; images and reports are handed over.

**Risks.** The nurse leaves and the calls stop (write the scripts so a replacement can start in a day). Titration inertia: the list is reviewed but prescriptions do not change (audit the share of off-target results that led to a change; nationally it is 37%). Patients followed elsewhere after discharge (the passport asks the other doctor to keep the targets). Data drift into WhatsApp. An unreadable or wrong Telugu sheet (the signature step and the error log exist for this).

**Not promised.** No promise that any patient will avoid another heart attack, a repeat procedure or death. No promise of a cure. No claim that the passport or the Telugu sheet improves outcomes; they are for understanding and continuity. No claim that results are better than another doctor's. Targets are goals, not guarantees.

### Flagship 2 — Open weekly teaching series for trainees and young cardiologists

**Format.** 45 minutes, same evening every week (a weekday at 8 pm avoids the CCDC Saturday slot), on a video call.

| Minutes | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | The question of the week and the rule for the session |
| 5–20 | Case 1: history and ECG or angiogram; **everyone commits to a decision in a poll before the reveal**; what was done; what he would do differently |
| 20–35 | Case 2, presented by a trainee from the third month onward |
| 35–40 | The evidence in one slide: the trial or guideline line that decides the question, with the reference |
| 40–45 | Questions triaged from the chat; errata from last week |

**Two layers, because of the consent tiers (file 11).**

- **Closed layer (tier T2/T3):** live sessions and recordings with real, consented, de-identified cases, open free of charge to any doctor who registers with name, institution and medical-council registration number. Recordings sit behind the same registration, with the reminder that anything shown may be screenshotted.
- **Open layer (tiers T0 and T4):** a public atlas page for each session containing the teaching point, the decision rule, the references, and images only if they are redrawn, licensed, or taken from his own already-published case reports. This is what search engines and non-registered trainees find.

Putting real angiograms on open YouTube "for peers" is the one thing the protocol does not allow before journal publication.

**Topics where he has standing (first twelve weeks)**

| Week | Topic |
|---|---|
| 1 | Primary PCI: the decision in the first ten minutes |
| 2 | ECG for emergency doctors: ST elevation and its mimics |
| 3 | Imaging-guided PCI: three cases where IVUS or OCT changed the plan |
| 4 | A complication, told straight: no-reflow |
| 5 | The stable-angina conversation: what ISCHEMIA and ORBITA-2 let you promise |
| 6 | First trainee-presented case |
| 7 | AI literacy: how to read an AI-ECG paper (EAGLE against TRICORDER) |
| 8 | After the stent: LDL targets and why patients stop their tablets |
| 9 | Consent for angioplasty in Telugu: saying risk in numbers |
| 10 | A complication, told straight: access-site bleeding and perforation |
| 11 | Starting practice: contracts, registration, records, the advertising rules |
| 12 | Trainee case; review of the quarter; errata |

**Platform.** A standard video-meeting licence with registration, polls and cloud recording (about Rs 15,000 a year, est.; Zoom's own page confirms simultaneous broadcast to YouTube by RTMP is possible, which is for the open-layer sessions that use no patient material). Registration form and attendance sheet on a free form tool. Atlas on his own domain.

**Sharing credit.** A rotating chair from a different institution each month. Trainee presenters named on the slide and in the atlas ("Case: Dr X, DrNB resident, Hospital Y; discussant: Dr Z"). An editorial group of three or four early-career cardiologists by the end of year 1, so the series is not one man's lecture. Hospital names appear only as presenters' affiliations.

**How trainees find it.** Personal emails to the DrNB programme coordinators at the 24 Hyderabad-area hospital entries and to the cardiology departments at NIMS, Osmania and Gandhi; the same for Andhra Pradesh once the college list is confirmed; a note to the CSI Telangana secretary; one post, if its moderators agree, in the Telegram group attached to "Cardiology Lectures DM DNB"; and a factual page on his own site. No paid promotion.

**CME/CPD.** Do not accredit the weekly session (section D). Accredit a quarterly in-person half-day with a society as organiser, include the mandatory ethics class, and use it for the hands-on parts that cannot be done online.

**Mentorship add-ons**

- **Research-methods clinic:** 60 minutes a month; one mentee's protocol or abstract is worked through in front of the group.
- **Case-report help:** a checklist based on the CARE reporting guideline and the journals' consent forms **[BK]**; he reads one draft a fortnight. Mentees chosen by open application (file 19).
- **Observerships at his clinic:** no regulation on clinical observerships was found (file 19). Conditions to adopt: the observer is a registered doctor or enrolled postgraduate; each patient is asked for consent to the observer's presence and may refuse; the observer does not examine, advise or record; a signed confidentiality undertaking; no fee in either direction; no certificate that looks like a training qualification. Observerships in Apollo premises need Apollo's written approval. Ask the lawyer whether clinical-establishment rules add anything.
- **PG-teacher status** remains tied to full-time work in an accredited department (file 19).

**Funding without industry capture.** The running cost is under Rs 50,000 a year, which he can carry himself; that is the simplest protection. Rules to publish in the charter: no company logo, slide, speaker fee or "supported by" line; no vendor demonstration slots; devices and drugs are discussed by generic name and evidence; any society-run workshop discloses its funding; a yearly one-line statement of costs and who paid. UCPMP 2024 and the device code allow companies to pay travel and hospitality only for CME speakers (and, for devices, training participants) (file 16), so a sponsor could not lawfully fund the audience in any case.

**Risks.** A patient is identified from a case (tier rules, second-person check, no open posting). Hospital objects to image use (written permission or published cases only). Attendance collapses after the novelty (trainee presenters and a fixed slot are the countermeasure; "Cardiology Lectures DM DNB" shows what lapse looks like). The stable-angina session irritates colleagues (present trial results, never other doctors' practice). Drift toward public content for reach (the single metric is regulars, not views). A sponsor offers money (the charter answers before he has to).

**Not promised.** Not a degree, fellowship or accredited training post. No CPD credit for weekly sessions. No examination coaching. No guarantee of a publication, a job or a referral relationship. Cases teach decisions; they do not advertise results.

### Flagship 3 — Heart School

**What it is.** A free, measured, 12-week Telugu and English cohort for adults who have not had a heart attack, run in pods with a family partner, with follow-up measurements at 3, 6 and 12 months and a written route to each person's own doctor. Sections B1–B10 give the design and its sources. The programme is not named after him; his name, qualifications and registration number appear as medical lead; no clinic or hospital name appears.

**Preparation (the four weeks before week 1)**

- Host agreement signed (residents' association for the pilot); room and two measurement dates fixed.
- Laboratory chosen (NABL-accredited; on-site collection; bills the host or participants directly).
- Lawyer's view on clause 8.1(x) and on who may fund tests; Apollo's written no-objection to outside teaching.
- Protocol and consent form to an ethics committee; pilot registered (OSF, or CTRI if the committee asks).
- Nurse or coach engaged and trained (16 hours); six pod-leader places advertised.
- Scripts and workbook for sessions 1–4 approved; BP devices checked against the STRIDE BP list and bought with two cuff sizes.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Running all three pilots at once.** Stagger them. A failed first cohort or a lapsed weekly series costs more trust than a late start.
- **Any path from Heart School to his consulting room.** No clinic details on any Heart School material; no appointment desk; the count of participants who later consulted him is published.
- **Charging the public in year 1.** The NMC position on paid public courses is unsettled and GST probably applies. Free now; a not-for-profit vehicle later if needed, after legal and tax advice.
- **Taking money from a laboratory, a device seller or an app.** No commission, discount code, affiliate link or "preferred partner". Name the validated-device list, not a shop.
- **The word "reversal".** The commercial diabetes programmes own it and senior Indian endocrinologists object to it. Use "at target" and "remission" only where a guideline defines it.
- **Reporting change as effect.** A before-after cohort cannot separate the programme from regression to the mean. Say so in the same sentence as the number.
- **Individual advice in groups.** No treatment advice in pods, chats or session questions; the nurse's script and the red-flag rule cover what to say.
- **Real cases on open platforms.** Angiograms and ECGs of his patients stay behind the doctors-only wall until published in a journal; even then, published figures only.
- **Apollo's data.** Do not export records, images or contact lists from Apollo systems into his own registry, AI tools or WhatsApp. Build the registry from own-practice patients.
- **Unvalidated AI output in front of patients or in a paper.** Every Telugu sheet is signed by him; every extracted number is checked against manual abstraction first.
- **WhatsApp as the intervention.** The one Indian trial was null. Use it to remind and to keep pods in touch.
- **Wrong-size cuffs and unvalidated monitors.** A standard cuff under-fits arms above 32 cm and over-reads. Buy both sizes; check the model on the validated list.
- **Portrait publicity by hosts and employers.** Supply the approved text; no photograph banners; no "renowned cardiologist".
- **Weekly CPD accreditation.** High effort, little credit, and built for halls. Quarterly half-day only.
- **A named "method" or branded school.** Heart School is a programme with a medical lead, not "Dr Avinash's" anything.
- **Sponsored teaching.** One sponsored session spends the distinction from the existing Indian series.
- **Observers in clinic without patient consent,** or observerships presented as training posts.
- **Quoting items marked [BK] or (est.) from this file** without checking: the STRIDE listing of a specific Omron model, HbA1c prices, the GST threshold and Regulation 6.4 wording, FAITH and REACH-HF result figures, the Cochrane home-rehabilitation conclusion, the NHS programme effect sizes, staff hourly rates.
