# 26: Independent interventional practice in Hyderabad's western corridor — market and access (Round 4, task R4-2)

**Executive summary**

1. **The clinic-plus-visiting model is already normal in this corridor, and it is cheap to equip.** Practo shows six stand-alone heart-clinic sites (five owners) between Manikonda and Chanda Nagar run by interventional cardiologists who also operate at hospitals (one says so in terms: a "senior interventional cardiologist from CARE hospital, Hitech city heads the unit"). Indian listing prices put a full non-invasive set (ECG, colour-Doppler echo, TMT, Holter, ABPM) at roughly ₹14–32 lakh, which narrows file 17's ₹15–50 lakh estimate.
2. **Cath-lab access terms are not published anywhere.** No Hyderabad per-case lab fee, revenue-share percentage, minimum guarantee or block-booking tariff could be found in trade press, hospital sites or investor documents. "Cath-lab-as-a-service" exists in India only as company-to-hospital or company-to-government deals (Meditrina; vendor revenue-share; Medtronic's managed labs abroad), never as a published product for an individual visiting operator. The real terms must come from about ten interviews (list in section 7.3).
3. **Every payer attaches to the hospital, not to him.** CGHS (rates re-notified 3 Oct 2025, in force 13 Oct 2025) pays the empanelled hospital one package that already contains the "surgeon's fee": PTCA ₹83,725 (NABH, Tier I, excluding stent), coronary angiography ₹13,400, super-specialist OPD ₹700. Aarogyasri, EHS and insurer cashless work the same way. His hospital choice therefore decides which patients he can treat without them paying cash.
4. **Aarogyasri is a receivables problem, and most corridor corporates are outside it.** Network hospitals reported ₹1,400 crore unpaid in Sept 2025 and about ₹2,000–2,100 crore by July–Sept 2026, with bills pending 11–15 months; Telangana Today reports most Hyderabad corporate hospitals are not empanelled. The current Aarogyasri PTCA rate could not be verified (the Trust's portal exposes no rate list to a non-logged-in reader).
5. **The corridor is hospital-dense and still growing.** Thirteen hospitals with a stated (eight) or very likely (five) cath lab sit inside the 30-minute ring, four of them in the Financial District alone (Apollo, Continental, Star, Medicover's new 550-bed unit). Consultation fees listed on Practo run ₹450–1,200 (median ₹800 in hospitals, ₹1,000 in clinics). Telangana counts 9.39 lakh IT jobs (2026), and Narsingi, Manikonda and Tellapur were merged into GHMC in Nov 2025. No count of residents or of households for these localities newer than Census 2011 was found.

#### 2.2 Plain statement: what could and could not be verified about access terms

**Verified**
- Interventional cardiologists in this corridor routinely hold listings at more than one hospital and run their own clinics alongside (section 1.2).
- For CGHS (and by adoption ECHS), the operator's fee sits inside the hospital's package; no payer deals with the consultant directly (section 3).
- The revenue-share / pay-per-use model for the lab itself exists in India between equipment vendors or operators and hospitals or governments.
- Hospitals publish wide self-pay ranges for angioplasty, not a tariff a visiting operator could plan from.

**Not verified (no public source found)**
- Any Hyderabad hospital's professional-fee share for a visiting interventionalist, for cash, insured, CGHS or Aarogyasri cases.
- Any per-case "lab rent" or facility fee where the doctor bills the patient and pays the hospital.
- Any block-booking product (fixed lab sessions per week for an outside operator).
- Minimum-guarantee amounts or exclusivity premiums for cardiologists in Hyderabad.
- Whether Apollo, AIG, Continental, Star or Yashoda credential outside operators at all.
- Who holds stent and hardware inventory for a visiting operator's cases, and how after-hours STEMI calls are staffed and paid.
- A stand-alone day-care angioplasty centre or a cath-lab-as-a-service company operating in Hyderabad.

**Illustrative arithmetic only (not a finding):** if the podcast's 20–23% applied to the CGHS package of ₹83,725, the operator's share would be about ₹16,700–19,300 per PTCA; applied to PACE's single-stent self-pay range it would be ₹30,000–63,000. Whether hospitals compute a share on the bill including or excluding the stent, and whether cardiology shares differ from the podcast's average, is unknown. Do not plan on these figures; get three written offers.

#### 3.2 What this means for an independent doctor and his patients

1. **He cannot be "on the panel".** No scheme or insurer empanels a consultant. A patient's cover works only inside a hospital that holds the agreement, and only for the admission.
2. **His own clinic is a cash clinic.** CGHS pays a super-specialist OPD consultation at ₹700 and an echo at ₹1,475, but only at an empanelled HCO. At his own clinic the CGHS, Aarogyasri, EHS or insured patient pays out of pocket (some retail policies have OPD riders; not researched).
3. **Hospital choice is payer choice.** Per the third-party CGHS directory (cghshospitals.com, 93 Hyderabad hospitals, 2026), corridor hospitals listed are CARE Hitec City, Continental, Citizens, KIMS Kondapur, Medicover Hitec City and Chandanagar, PACE and Himagiri. Apollo Financial District, AIG, Yashoda Hitec City, KIMS Gachibowli and Medicover Financial District do not appear. Because every MoA was reset on 13 Oct 2025, this list must be re-checked on the CGHS site (it was down). Confidence: Medium-Low.
4. **Aarogyasri patients need a second, different hospital.** Telangana Today (22 Aug 2025) reports that "an unwillingness to revise Aarogyasri health packages" is a major reason most Hyderabad corporate hospitals are not empanelled, and that the 360-plus network hospitals are "mostly in districts and run by individual doctors". To treat Aarogyasri patients he needs privileges at a network hospital or a public institution; which corridor hospitals are in the network could not be read from the Trust's portal.
5. **His fee inherits the hospital's receivable.** Under a package the hospital is paid late and then pays him. Bills were pending "11 to 15 months" in Sept 2026; hospitals reported "zero profit margins" on scheme work. Any Aarogyasri or EHS share he is promised should be written with a payment date that does not depend on the Trust paying the hospital.
6. **Emergency care does not wait for panels.** His clinic's STEMI pathway (file 17 §8.3, item 2.7) should name the nearest 24x7 primary-PCI hospital regardless of where he holds privileges, and say so to patients.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Planning on a percentage nobody has confirmed.** The 20–23% figure is one podcast remark. Budget year 1 on clinic income alone and treat procedure income as upside until a written offer exists.
- **Signing with one hospital because it answered first.** Access can be withdrawn; two hospitals in different ownership are the minimum.
- **Scheme receivables by the back door.** A fee share payable "when the hospital is paid" imports the 11–15 month Aarogyasri delay into his household budget.
- **Assuming his patients' cover follows him.** A CGHS, EHS or insured patient who loves his clinic still needs an empanelled hospital for the procedure; say this plainly at the first visit.
- **Discussing access while still bound by Apollo's terms.** In-term exclusivity is generally enforceable (file 17 §2). Conversations stay informational until the contract has been read.
- **Fee-share language that looks like a referral payment.** Shares must be for work he performs, documented as professional fees (2002 Reg. 6.4; file 17 §6).
- **Letting a device or equipment company broker the hospital deal.** UCPMP 2024 and the perception of inducement (file 16).
- **Relying on directory data as fact.** Practo CVs and third-party CGHS lists are leads. Bed counts differ between a hospital's site and Practo (Citizens: 300 vs 250; Medicover Financial District: 550 vs "300+").
- **Copying competitors' profile language.** At least one corridor profile calls its owner "one of Hyderabad's leading" cardiologists; the 2026 Guidelines bar that wording in anything he publishes or controls (file 11).
- **Reading thin supply as proven demand.** No cardiologist's clinic is listed in Narsingi, Kokapet or Tellapur on Practo, but Practo is partial and four large hospitals sit ten to twenty minutes away.
