#### 2.2 How NMC counts publications for teaching posts

- The **Medical Institutions (Qualifications of Faculty) Regulations, 2025** were notified on 30 June 2025 and apply from that date; NMC's Postgraduate Medical Education Board issued FAQs on 23/28 October 2025. [V, FAQ PDF]
- A third-party summary table "updated up to latest gazette 30.06.2025" (Government Medical College, Jammu site) gives the current rule as: **only original research papers, meta-analyses, systematic reviews and case series** count; journals must be indexed in **MEDLINE, PubMed Central, Citation Index, Science Citation Index Expanded, Embase, Scopus or DOAJ**; **only the first three authors** get credit; two such publications are needed at each promotion step; the Basic Courses in Medical Education and in Biomedical Research (BCME, BCBR) are mandatory. A search extract of the regulation's definition of "research publication" matches the article types and index list. [Medium: secondary sources agree; the Gazette text itself was not opened]
- Earlier rule sets for comparison (same table): 2020–2022 required first, second, third or corresponding author with the same index list; the February 2022 gazette did not specify authorship.
- **Consequences for him:** single case reports never count for NMC purposes; a **case series** does. A paper in a journal that is only in DOAJ counts for NMC but will not impress an ACC or ESC credentials committee, which ask for PubMed, Scopus or Web of Science. NBEMS adopted the NMC 2025 faculty criteria "subject to modifications" on 11 Nov 2025 [V].
- The 2025 FAQ's routes for non-teaching specialists into professor or associate-professor posts are written for **government** institutions (for example 10 years in a 220-bed government hospital for associate professor) [V]. They do not open a route for a private-hospital consultant.

#### 2.3 Predatory-journal red flags

Use this as a five-minute check before any submission [Judgment, consistent with file 07's sources]:

1. Not in the NLM Catalog as "currently indexed", and not findable in Scopus's source list, yet claims an "impact factor".
2. Promises a decision or publication in days. A journal family reached this session advertises publication "within 2 weeks of manuscript submission" as a fast-track service; that is the pattern to avoid.
3. Markets itself to authors around "NMC publication criteria" rather than around readers.
4. Unsolicited email invitations to submit or to join an editorial board.
5. APC not stated up front, or requested before peer review.
6. Editorial board with no verifiable institutional pages; no COPE membership; no retraction policy.
7. Scope covering all of medicine and several unrelated fields.
8. His own 2018 paper is in a journal that is not in PubMed; do not add more of that kind.

#### 3.1 How ethics review works at Apollo

All from Apollo Research and Innovations (ARI) pages and the generic IEC-BMR Standard Operating Procedure (version AH-02, 2022). Confidence High for what the SOP says; the Hyderabad committee's current local version, fee table and meeting dates were not available.

- Apollo has **two committees at each research centre**: the Institutional Ethics Committee – Clinical Studies (IEC-CS) for regulatory trials, and the **Institutional Ethics Committee – Bio Medical Research (IEC-BMR)** for "clinical studies including but not restricted to observational/retrospective/disease registry" work, academic studies and "academic studies towards a degree or publication". #### 3.2 ICMR 2017 National Ethical Guidelines: the parts that apply

Read from the full text. Confidence High.

- **Waiver of consent (section 5.7):** a researcher can apply for a waiver "if the research involves less than minimal risk to participants and the waiver will not adversely affect the rights and welfare of the participants". Box 5.2 lists the situations, including "**retrospective studies, where the participants are de-identified or cannot be contacted**" and "research on anonymized biological samples/data". The committee grants the waiver; the investigator cannot assume it.
- **Types of review (Table 4.2):** exemption for less-than-minimal-risk proposals without linked identifiers; expedited for minimal risk; full committee for the rest.
- **CTRI (section 3.7):** registration has been mandatory since 15 June 2009 for clinical trials regulated under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act; it "is voluntary for other biomedical and health research"; observational studies "may be registered prospectively". CTRI is free and requires the ethics approval letter [BK]; since 2018 it accepts only prospective registration [BK, Medium], so a registry must be registered **before** the first patient is enrolled. The CTRI site was unreachable this session.
- Ethics committees themselves must be registered with the relevant authority (section 4.1.5); journals increasingly ask for the committee's registration number.

#### 3.3 Data protection

- **DPDP Act 2023, section 17(2)(b)** [V, Gazette text]: the Act's main obligations do not apply to processing "necessary for research, archiving or statistical purposes if the personal data is not to be used to take any decision specific to a Data Principal and such processing is carried on in accordance with such standards as may be prescribed". The standards are in the DPDP Rules 2025 [BK for the schedule number].
- Substantive obligations (notice, consent, security, breach reporting, penalties) begin **13 May 2027** [file 02].
- **The hospital is the data fiduciary** for records and images [file 02]. Practical rules that follow: extract inside the hospital system; strip name, hospital number, dates of birth and admission, phone and address at extraction; keep the re-identification key only on a hospital machine; store the analysis file on hospital-approved storage; never paste identifiable data into a consumer AI tool, personal email or WhatsApp.
- Apollo's own patient authorisation form (seen in a search result, not opened) says treatment data "can be shared with research scientists without disclosing my identity". That supports de-identified retrospective research but does not replace IEC approval. [Medium]

#### 3.5 Reporting, authorship and AI disclosure

- **Reporting checklists** [BK, High]: STROBE for observational studies (strobe-statement.org); CARE for case reports (care-statement.org); CONSORT only if he ever randomises. Attach the completed checklist at submission; most target journals ask for it.
- **ICMJE authorship** [V]: all four are required: substantial contribution to conception/design or to acquisition, analysis or interpretation of data; drafting or critically revising; final approval; and agreement to be accountable for all aspects. Contributors who meet fewer than four "should not be listed as authors, but they should be acknowledged". EHJ-CR adds that every case-report author must have been involved in the patient's care [07].
- **AI-assisted writing, ICMJE** [V]: authors who use AI-assisted technology "should describe, in both the cover letter and the submitted work in the appropriate section if applicable, how they used it"; chatbots "should not be listed as authors because they cannot be responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and originality of the work"; "humans are responsible for any submitted material that included the use of AI-assisted technologies"; AI must not be cited as an author or as a primary source; authors must be able to affirm there is no plagiarism in AI-produced text and images; non-disclosure may be treated as misconduct.
- **Journal-specific** [V]: EHJ-CR requires disclosure in the cover letter and a full explanation in Methods or Acknowledgements, and cites COPE. IJME requires full disclosure following WAME's recommendations. [BK, Medium–High]: Elsevier titles (IHJ, JACC family, IPEJ, CRM) ask for a separate "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the writing process" above the references, permit AI for language and readability only under human oversight, and do not permit AI-generated figures. COPE's position statement (February 2023) says AI tools cannot be authors and their use must be transparently described; the COPE page was blocked this session.
- **Working rule for the planned Claude-based system:** AI may check word limits, format references, tighten English on **de-identified** text and build submission checklists. It may not receive identifiable patient data, generate or alter clinical images, write the discussion unreviewed, or supply references that he has not opened. Every submission carries a disclosure sentence, for example: "The authors used [tool, version] to improve the readability of the manuscript. The authors reviewed and edited the output and take full responsibility for the content."

**Pipeline stages** (one shared tracker, kept by the friend/AI system): Logged, Consented, Conference submission, Manuscript drafted, Submitted, In revision, Accepted, Published and filed (PDF, DOI, ORCID updated, Apollo Honors List notified).

**Rule of two uses:** every case is prepared once and used twice: a conference submission, then a journal manuscript within 4 weeks of the conference decision, whichever way that decision goes.

**Weekly case-capture habit (20 minutes, same slot every week, for example Friday after the last case).**

1. Open the case log on a **hospital-approved device and storage location** (not a personal phone gallery or consumer cloud).
2. Add any case from the week that meets one of five triggers: a complication and its bail-out; an unusual presentation; imaging that changed the plan; a device problem; a teaching point he explained to a junior.
3. Record only: internal case code (for example AP-2026-047), age band (decade), sex, date as month and year, one-line presentation, one-line "why interesting", key images to keep, consent status, target meeting, target journal.
4. **De-identify at the point of saving:** export angiographic and echo loops without the patient banner; crop or mask name, hospital number, accession number, dates and institution name burnt into images; remove DICOM header fields; no faces, no voices from the cath lab; describe occupation and place only in general terms. The link between case code and hospital number stays in one password-protected sheet on the hospital system.
5. **Consent before discharge**, on the target journal's own form where one exists (EHJ-CR and BMJ require the patient to have seen the description). File the signed form with the hospital record and note its location in the log. No consent, no publication, and under the 2026 advertising guidelines even de-identified educational case publication expects anonymised patient consent [file 02].
6. First Friday of each month: 30-minute triage with the co-author (DrNB resident or registrar). Pick at most two cases to move forward; match them to the next two deadlines in the calendar; archive the rest.
7. Quarterly: check that every log entry has either a consent form or a decision not to use it.

### The rules that govern the calendar

1. **Merit routes only.** Podium time comes from open case and abstract calls, never from paid slots or sponsored "awards".
2. **One name, one iD.** Every submission uses the fixed byline and ORCID iD.
3. **Ethics before data.** No dataset leaves the hospital system or becomes a slide or a paper without IEC-BMR approval; no case without written consent.
4. **De-identify at capture**, not at submission.
5. **Two uses per case; four weeks from conference decision to journal submission.**
6. **PubMed-visible first.** A journal that is not in PubMed, Scopus or Web of Science gets at most one paper a year, and never a paid one.
7. **Five-minute predatory check** before any new journal.
8. **ICMJE authorship, both ways.** No gift authors; no accepting gift authorship.
9. **AI disclosed every time**, in the cover letter and the manuscript; no patient data into AI tools; he reads every reference.
10. **Delegate travel is self-funded or hospital-funded.** Industry-paid travel only as contracted faculty or documented training, in writing, routed through the hospital where possible.
11. **Every industry interaction goes in the register** (date, company, nature, value, agreement reference) and on the disclosure slide for related talks.
12. **No brand names in anything patient-facing**, including his own educational channels.
13. **Every podium role and acceptance is filed the same week**: programme PDF, certificate, acceptance email. These are the evidence for FACC, FESC and FSCAI files.
14. **Calendar review on the first of each month** by the friend/AI system: re-verify the next 90 days of "[P]" items against organisers' pages and move dates.
15. **Cut order when clinical load spikes:** conferences first, then letters and reviews; never the weekly case log, the registry entry or consent.

## Risks & things to avoid

1. **Compromised society domains.** csinic.org redirects to a gambling site and apcsi.org serves injected code. Do not enter credentials, pay fees or send documents through them; use csi.org.in and people known in person.
2. **Paying for non-indexed publication.** CRM: Interesting Cases (USD 920) and IHJ Cardiovascular Reports (USD 400) are not in PubMed. Know what is being bought.
3. **Counting case reports toward the wrong goal.** They help the podium ladder and FICC/FESC only if indexed; they do not count for NMC purposes at all.
4. **Assuming a consent waiver.** Only the committee grants it. Starting extraction before the approval letter is a reportable deviation.
5. **Mixing Apollo and Citizens data** without each institution's written permission and a clear ethics route.
6. **DPDP exposure from convenience tools.** Identifiable angiograms on a phone, in a messaging app or in an AI tool create liability for him and the hospital, more so after 13 May 2027.
7. **Industry-funded delegate travel**, however it is labelled. Clause 6.8 penalties reach removal from the register, and company disclosures to government are discoverable.
8. **"Training programme" as a loophole.** The April 2026 device-code amendment is new and untested against clause 6.8. Treat sponsored training as an exception that needs paperwork, not a routine perk.
9. **Becoming a product voice.** Company speaker programmes and proctoring are peer-facing only; any public trace conflicts with the 2026 advertising guidelines.
10. **Undisclosed AI use.** ICMJE says non-disclosure may be treated as misconduct; AI-fabricated references are the commonest way careful people get caught.
11. **Sponsor attestation of stale numbers.** The hospital web profile figures differ between listings (file 01). Only a dated log should go to a sponsor.
12. **Over-committing the calendar.** The table lists more than twenty deadlines a year; the realistic Year-1 load is 5–6 case submissions, one study, one fellowship application.
13. **Pattern dates treated as facts.** Half the 2027 rows and all 2028 rows are inferred. A missed re-check is a missed deadline.
14. **Full-time faculty declarations.** NBEMS verifies full-time status through tax documents; a declaration that does not match his actual split between hospitals would be a serious problem for him and for Apollo.
