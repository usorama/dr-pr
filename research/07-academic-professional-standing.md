# Professional and academic standing: the peer-facing reputation ladder for Dr. Avinash Paturi

**Research date:** 12 November 2026. **Scope:** early-to-mid-career Indian interventional cardiologist, about 6 years post-DM, Hyderabad.

## Executive summary

1. The fastest credible peer credentials open to him now are **FSCAI** (international pathway: 5 years post-fellowship practice plus 2,000 primary-operator procedures; apply by 28 February for induction at SCAI, 2–4 May 2027) and **APSIC Associate Fellow**; **FESC (Clinical Excellence track)**, **FACC**, **FAPSIC** and **FICC** all become reachable within 2–3 years but each needs indexed publications and sponsors, so publishing is the gating item for almost everything.
2. **FCSI is a long clock**: it needs 10 years of CSI membership plus 2 first-author indexed papers, so CSI life membership (Rs 20,000) should be taken immediately if he does not already hold it.
3. The merit-based door to the podium is **case submission**, not networking. Two live deadlines matter this quarter: **CRT 2027 interesting cases (4 December 2026)** and **EuroPCR 2027 cases/abstracts (11 January 2027)**. The Hyderabad home-ground stage is **ACVS India / TCT India Next (FACTS Foundation, Apollo-linked, each July)** and the **CSI Telangana chapter annual meeting (late February/March)**.
4. A realistic output for a busy clinician is **3–4 case reports or images, 1 original single-centre study, and 1 review or chapter per year**, under one fixed author name with an ORCID iD. **Cureus should be avoided as a primary outlet** (delisted from Web of Science in October 2025).
5. Recognition should be earned in this order: audited outcomes and registry participation, then society fellowships, then invited faculty roles. Wikipedia and a Google Knowledge Panel are downstream results, not targets; he does not currently meet Wikipedia's academic notability criteria and should not have an article created for him.

### 2. Conferences and the podium ladder

#### 2.1 How the ladder actually works

[Practitioner judgment informed by the sources below; Medium.]

1. **Attendee who asks good questions and is seen** (Year 0–1).
2. **Case presenter through open calls.** This is the only fully merit-based step. EuroPCR states that its Best Clinical Case winner presents in the Main Arena and "gets the chance to return as Faculty at the next edition", and all accepted presenters receive certificates, extended early-fee registration and visibility on PCRonline. EuroPCR 2026 received 1,984 clinical case submissions, with a limit of 3 submissions per person.
3. **Abstract presenter** with his own data (needs the single-centre study in section 3).
4. **Panelist/discussant, then invited faculty** at chapter and regional meetings. Invitations come from scientific committees, so they follow from being known to the organising secretary and from a visible record of accepted cases.
5. **Session chair, then workshop convener** for a niche he owns locally (for example imaging-guided PCI or STEMI systems).
6. **Course director** of his own annual half-day course, ideally under the CSI Telangana chapter or Apollo banner with TGMC credit hours. Realistically Year 3 or later.

ACC's FACC application explicitly accepts "syllabus/program or letter from a Society where you served as faculty or conference chair" as evidence, so every faculty role should be documented with the printed programme.

#### 3.2 What he can generate from his own practice

1. **Case reports and images** (lowest effort). Sources: complications and bail-outs, unusual STEMI presentations, imaging-guided decisions, device cases. Every conference case submission should be written so it can become a journal submission within 4 weeks.
2. **Retrospective single-centre studies with Institutional Ethics Committee approval.** Feasible topics from the hospital-listed volumes: primary PCI door-to-balloon times and in-hospital outcomes; radial versus femoral access outcomes; pacemaker implant complications at 30 days and 1 year; young-MI (under 40) risk profile including Lp(a); contrast-associated kidney injury incidence. Needs an Excel/REDCap case log from day one. [Judgment.]
3. **Prospective registry of his own procedures.** This doubles as the audited-outcomes asset in section 5.
4. **Letters to the editor** responding to recent IHJ/JACC/EHJ papers with Indian data or perspective: 300–500 words, PubMed-indexed in most journals, fast.
5. **Narrative reviews** on a chosen niche, co-written with a senior colleague.
6. **Book chapters:** [BK, Medium] CSI publishes an annual "Cardiology Update" volume released at CSICON (a 2025 edition edited by Kahali appeared in a search result listing), with chapters invited by the editor; the route is an offer to the scientific committee or editor, usually via a senior co-author.
7. **Guideline and consensus authorship:** CSI has a Guidelines Council (convener Dr Dipak Sarma). Writing-group seats go to people already active in a council, so this is a Year 3+ outcome of council work.
8. **Funding:** ICMR's Small Extramural Grant call for 2027 (investigator-initiated) closes 20 January 2027; realistic only with an institutional research office and a co-investigator with grant experience.

#### 3.3 Realistic annual output

[Judgment.] With a protected 3 hours per week and one junior co-author (DrNB resident or registrar):

- 3–4 case reports/images (one each to CVCR, EHJ-CR or JACC: Case Reports, an interventional journal, IPEJ for a device case)

#### 3.4 Author identity and profiles

The two spellings ("Avinash Paturi" and "Pathuri Avinash") will split his record. PubMed author searching is name-string based ("last name + initials"), full names are searchable only for records from 2002, and PubMed itself says author matching is computed by similarity. Google Scholar states that it "cannot reliably tell authors with similar names apart". (High; PubMed help, Google Scholar help.)

Set-up, in order (about 3 hours one-off):

1. **Choose one byline and never vary it.** It should match the name on his medical council registration and degree certificates, because society fellowship applications compare them. South Indian "surname-first" bylines are routinely mis-indexed (for example as "Avinash P"), so the given-name-first form "Avinash Paturi" is safer for indexing, but the legal-document match takes priority. This is his decision and should be made once.
2. **ORCID iD** (free, persistent, 16 digits). Add every variant as "also known as". Enter the iD at every manuscript submission; linking with Crossref lets works be pushed to the record automatically. (High; orcid.org.)
3. **Google Scholar profile.** Use a personal Google account; add a **verified institutional email** because non-institutional addresses such as gmail.com do not qualify the profile for search inclusion; add article groups for each name variant; merge duplicates; make public. An Apollo or other institutional mailbox is therefore a prerequisite. (High.)
4. **Scopus Author ID** [BK]: created automatically after the first Scopus-indexed paper; merge split profiles through the Author Feedback Wizard and connect to ORCID.
5. **Web of Science Researcher Profile** [BK]: successor to Publons; records verified peer reviews and editorial work.
6. **ResearchGate** [BK]: optional; useful for full-text requests; low maintenance.

## Risks & things to avoid

1. **Paying for recognition.** Paid awards, paid "top doctor" lists, predatory journals and pay-to-speak conferences damage peer standing more than having nothing.
2. **Over-reliance on Cureus or similar megajournals.** Indexing status has already been withdrawn once; credentials committees and peers notice.
3. **Name inconsistency.** Every paper under a different spelling is partly lost. Fix before the first submission.
4. **Unverifiable numbers.** Fellowship sponsors attest to procedure counts. Use dated logs, not the hospital web profile.
5. **Patient consent and confidentiality.** Journals such as EHJ-CR require written consent after the patient has seen the manuscript, and all authors must have been involved in care. Conference slides and social posts need the same discipline: no identifiable images, dates or faces.
6. **Publishing outcomes without institutional sign-off.** Outcome data belongs to the hospital too. Ethics approval and management consent come first; clarify authorship and data ownership with Apollo and Citizens early.
7. **Industry entanglement.** Sponsored travel, proctoring fees and speaker roles are regulated (UCPMP 2024 and medical-council ethics rules; exact text not re-verified here). Undisclosed relationships are a reputational and regulatory risk.
8. **Gift or honorary authorship.** Adding seniors who did not contribute, or being added to papers he did not work on, is an integrity risk; EHJ-CR explicitly limits authors to those involved in care.
9. **Spreading across too many niches.** Peers remember one thing per person. Two niches at most for three years.
10. **Post-nominal inflation.** Listing memberships as fellowships ("MACC", "MESC" styled as honours) or using "FACC" before conferral is noticed by peers and may breach society rules (ACC states Associates may not use the FACC designation).
11. **Burnout.** The plan assumes about 4 hours a week. If clinical load prevents that, cut conferences before cutting the outcomes log and writing block.
12. **Wikipedia self-creation or paid creation.** Likely deletion and a permanent public record of it.
