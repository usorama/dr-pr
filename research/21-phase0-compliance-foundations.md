# Phase 0: the compliance and infrastructure foundations for an independent cardiology practice in Hyderabad, and research infrastructure without a corporate hospital

**Research date:** 12 November 2026. **Builds on:** `02`, `11b`, `16`, `17` (does not repeat their analysis of the NMC advertising Guidelines, the Apollo ethics-committee route, or exit mechanics).

## Executive summary

1. **His registration is most likely a renewal problem, not a transfer problem.** The national register shows "APMC/FMR/74749", dated 25 May 2012, which is a registration of the undivided state. The Telangana Medical Council (TGMC) treats doctors "registered in combined state till 01-02-2016" as its own: its portal has a "TSMC/APMC Registered Doctor Sign In", its circular of 17 April 2025 tells any such doctor practising in Telangana to "renew with Telangana Medical Council (TGMC) immediately", and its own press release identifies a Telangana doctor by an "APMC/FMR" number. Under section 15C of the state Act a doctor who does not renew every five years "shall cease to be a registered practitioner". He must log in and check this week. Fees are small: renewal Rs 1,000, plus Rs 3,000 (MD) and Rs 5,000 (DM) if those entries are missing, plus CPD hours (18 for a 2026 renewal, 24 from 1 January 2027).
2. **The clinic law is now settled.** The Telangana Allopathic Private Medical Care Establishments Act, 2002 was repealed by Telangana Act No. 21 of 2025 (assent 16 September 2025, effective back to 27 March 2017). The central Clinical Establishments Act, 2010 governs; a solo doctor's clinic is covered; an online state portal went live on 7 April 2026; running unregistered carries a penalty of up to Rs 50,000, then Rs 2 lakh, then Rs 5 lakh; rates must be displayed in Telugu and English. The Act has no advertising provision. A consulting-and-echo clinic also needs bio-medical waste authorisation, PC-PNDT registration for the echo machine (contested but safest), and municipal trade and fire clearances. It needs no AERB licence and no drug licence unless it adds X-ray or a pharmacy counter.
3. **Data protection has a hard date: 13 May 2027.** I read the DPDP Rules, 2025 in the Gazette (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 November 2025). Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 start eighteen months after publication. His clinic is the data fiduciary. Treatment data can rest on "voluntarily provided for a specified purpose"; newsletters, cohorts, research and AI vendors need a standalone notice and consent, a written processor contract, breach reporting (Board "without delay", details within 72 hours), and one-year log retention. The three-year medical-record rule survives because erasure yields to "compliance with any law".
4. **Research without Apollo is possible but needs a home.** ICMR's 2017 Guidelines allow a researcher with no institutional attachment to use an independent ethics committee (section 4.2.5) or a host institution's committee under an MoU (section 4.2.2). The committee must be registered with the Department of Health Research. REDCap cannot be licensed by an individual or a for-profit clinic (its licence is for not-for-profit organisations), so he needs an academic partner's instance or another tool. Registration of observational studies in CTRI is voluntary.
5. **The Phase 0 checklist in this file has 36 items**: 14 before the first public post, 12 within 90 days, 10 within a year. Statutory fees are low (roughly Rs 20,000 to Rs 60,000 if PC-PNDT is already held); the larger costs are indemnity insurance, memberships (about Rs 50,000 one-off for CSI, ICC, API and IMA) and one bundled legal review **[estimate]**.

#### 1.3 What the law requires

- **NMC Act, 2019, section 33(1) proviso:** a person registered in the Indian Medical Register before the Act "shall be deemed to have been registered under this Act and be enrolled in the National Register". **Section 34(1):** no person "other than a person who is enrolled in the State Register or the National Register" may practise medicine, sign medical certificates or give expert evidence. **Section 34(2):** contravention is punishable with imprisonment up to one year, or a fine up to Rs 5 lakh, or both. **[V]** A doctor on any state register is therefore not committing the section 34 offence by working in another state; the exposure is under state law and the ethics code.
- **Telangana Medical Practitioners Registration Act, 1968 (the AP Act of 1968 as adapted by G.O.Ms. No. 68 of 3 Aug 2015), section 15C:** every practitioner "shall once in every five years renew his or her registration", producing evidence of the prescribed hours of conferences or CME; one who fails "shall cease to be a registered practitioner … and the Registrar shall remove the name", though the Registrar "may grant fresh registration" on fee and application. **Section 15B:** additional qualifications are entered on application and fee. **[V]**
- **2002 Ethics Regulations, 1.4.1:** the registration number given by the state council must be shown in the clinic and on all prescriptions, certificates and receipts; **7.13:** the signboard and prescription paper carry name, recognised qualifications, speciality and "registration number including the name of the State Medical Council under which registered". **[prior, file 02]**
- **NMC Registration of Medical Practitioners and Licence to Practice Medicine Regulations, 2023** (Gazette, 10 May 2023): a doctor moving state applies through the Ethics and Medical Registration Board's portal; the home council has 30 days; no decision means deemed approval; "the unique number of the registration shall remain the same". The regulations are silent on holding licences in several states (Medical Dialogues summary; Gazette not read). In practice, a TGMC member told Deccan Chronicle on 8 September 2026 that doctors still need an NOC from their state council to practise in another state. **[V for the news reports; Medium]**
- **Telemedicine:** a doctor on any state register may tele-consult patients anywhere in India (Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020) **[BK, High]**.

### 2. Clinic registration and licences in Telangana

#### 2.1 Which law applies

- **Telangana Act No. 21 of 2025**, the Telangana Allopathic Private Medical Care Establishments (Registration and Regulation) (Repeal) Act, 2025: Governor's assent 16 September 2025, published 17 September 2025. Section 2(1): the 2002 Act "is hereby repealed with effect from 27.03.2017". **[V, Gazette scan on PRS]** This answers the open question in file 17. The 2002 Act's own provisions, including any on advertising, no longer apply; I did not retrieve its text.
- **Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010** applies, adopted by Telangana with effect from 27 March 2017 **[prior, file 17]**, operated through state rules reported as the Telangana Clinical Establishments Rules, 2022 (Telangana Today, 18 Jun 2026; a 2022 report called them 2011 rules; the rules themselves were not retrieved). **Medium.**
- **Coverage:** the central FAQ states the Act covers establishments owned by "a single doctor" **[V]**. Hyderabad's Collector said in February 2025 that a clinical establishment includes "a small clinic or a therapy centre" (Deccan Chronicle, 19 Feb 2025).
- **Advertising:** the central Act and Central Rules contain no advertising provision **[prior, file 11b; nothing contrary found]**. The NMC Guidelines govern.
- **Jan Vishwas amendments** notified 22 June 2026: "fine" becomes "penalty" in sections 40, 43 and 46, with adjudication, a hearing and an appeal route (Medical Dialogues, 27 Jun 2026). **Medium.**

#### 3.7 Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020: the essentials [BK, High; text not re-opened]

- Only a registered practitioner may tele-consult; patients may be anywhere in India; registration number on every e-prescription, message, receipt and website.

#### 4.2 What audits and registries need

#### 6.4 GST

- **Clinical work is exempt.** Entry 74 of Notification 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) exempts "health care services by a clinical establishment, an authorised medical practitioner or paramedics" (several 2026 tax commentaries, via search; notification not read). Consultant fees paid by hospitals for treating the hospital's patients fall within it **[BK, Medium-High]**. Guidance to practitioners is to keep the clinical-establishment registration and council registration as proof of entitlement.
- **Education, wellness and advisory work is taxable at 18%.** Standalone wellness packages are taxable; an advance ruling held a public-sector wellness centre's services not exempt (Taxmann, via search). Paid online courses for doctors were held taxable by the Karnataka authority **[prior, file 24]**. Rulings on corporate health check-ups go both ways. Speaker honoraria and advisory fees from industry, paid courses, paid newsletters, sponsorship and platform advertising revenue are ordinary taxable services **[BK]**.
- **The registration trap [BK on the statute; confirm with the accountant]:** registration is required when "aggregate turnover" exceeds Rs 20 lakh a year for services, and aggregate turnover **includes exempt supplies**. A person making only exempt supplies need not register. So a doctor whose clinical receipts already exceed Rs 20 lakh becomes liable to register on the **first rupee** of taxable income (one paid corporate talk). After registration, tax is charged only on the taxable part.
- Corporates deduct tax at source (10% on professional fees **[BK]**); invoices need PAN and, once registered, GSTIN.

1. **Scope:** group education and screening logistics; "no individual diagnosis or treatment is provided in sessions"; a referral pathway that is neutral (employee's own doctor or any cardiologist).
2. **No endorsement, no advertising use:** the company may not use his name, photograph, quotes or session recordings in external marketing; internal announcements may state name, qualifications and topic only. (Guidelines on endorsements and third-party promotion; 2002 Regs 6.1, 7.) No "official cardiologist of X" wording.
3. **No patient solicitation, no referral fees, no exclusivity** (Reg. 6.4).
4. **Data:** who is the fiduciary for screening data; the employer receives only aggregate, anonymised results with a minimum group size; individual results go to the employee alone; retention and deletion; breach duties; no employee data to insurers.
5. **Consent and voluntariness:** participation voluntary; notice in English and Telugu.
6. **Fees and tax:** fee, GST as applicable, tax deducted at source, payment days, cancellation terms.
7. **Intellectual property:** he keeps his slides and materials; the company gets an internal, non-transferable licence; recording only with written consent; no editing into clips.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Posting before A1 to A3 are closed.** The registration line on every post is a statement of fact; if it is wrong or lapsed, each post is evidence.
- **Assuming "no Telangana registration visible" means a transfer is needed,** and starting an NOC from Vijayawada. Check TGMC first; a wrong application wastes fees (Rs 1,000 levy on an unused NOC) and months.
- **Signing up on TGMC as a "New Doctor".** The FAQ warns against it for anyone already on the register; it creates a duplicate profile.
- **Treating the clinic as too small to register.** The 2022 inspections sealed clinics over paperwork.
- **Buying or using a handheld ultrasound or a second probe** without checking the PC-PNDT position; the statute turns on what the machine is capable of.
- **Using the patient list for outreach.** It breaches purpose limitation and the Guidelines' bar on soliciting, and hospital-held lists are the hospital's data.
- **Patient WhatsApp groups.** Membership discloses a diagnosis to every member.
- **Pasting identifiable data into consumer AI tools, or sharing an EMR login or e-signature** with staff or the friend (NMC advisory of 13 Oct 2026).
- **Collecting cohort health readings without the research tick and ethics approval,** then wanting to publish them.
- **Promising deletion.** The clinical record must be kept at least three years; say so in the notice.
- **A first paid corporate talk invoiced without asking the accountant** about GST registration.
- **Holding memberships, domains, trademarks or the insurance policy through the hospital,** an agency or the friend's account.
- **Relying on estimates in this file for fees and timelines.** Each **[EST]** needs a quote or a portal check.
- **Assuming the draft NMR rules are law.** As of 6 November 2026 they are not on NMC's list of notices.
