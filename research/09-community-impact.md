# 09 — "Do more for society": community impact programmes a Hyderabad cardiologist can lead

**Executive summary**

1. The clearest unmet need he can fill personally is **bystander CPR + AED readiness**: Indian bystander-CPR rates are 1.3–9.8% (target 62%), a Warangal registry of 814 cardiac arrests recorded zero survivors, and Telangana's 2023 "1 lakh trained / 1,200–1,400 AEDs" plan has no published delivery figures. A "1,000 people trained properly" campaign costs roughly Rs 0.8–1.5 lakh in equipment and is achievable in 6–10 months.
2. **Do not start a trust in year 1.** A new NGO generally cannot receive CSR money until it has 12A/80G, Form CSR-1 and a three-year track record (about 3.5–4 years end to end). Partner instead: Apollo's Billion Hearts Beating, CSI Telangana chapter, Rotary, GVK-EMRI, Society for Cyberabad Security Council.
3. **Telangana already runs the big systems** (STEMI hub-and-spoke since 2019, NCD screening of ~1.5 crore people, IHCI). A single doctor adds most value by plugging into them (teaching ECG/chest-pain triage, linking screened people to free state care), not by duplicating camps.
4. **Reputation that lasts comes from a published scorecard, not from event photos.** Every exemplar (Aravind, Bang, STEMI India, Dr Ramaka Srinivas in Warangal) is known for a counted, published result. Count outputs honestly; never claim "lives saved" without a documented case.
5. Three candidate signature initiatives are set out at the end: (A) Heart-Safe Communities — 1,000 CPR-trained in the western corridor; (B) a post-angioplasty/heart-failure patient community with measured risk-factor control; (C) monthly ECG and chest-pain triage teaching for first-contact clinicians.

## Findings

### 1. CPR and AED

**1.1 The size of the gap (High confidence)**

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Indian studies report bystander CPR of 1.3% (CARO, Delhi), 2.3% (Kerala), 7.4% (Tamil Nadu), 9.8% (Manipal); two international registries found 0% in Indian arms (DA-CPR, n=2,775; PAROS, n=2,006). AHA target is 62%. | Review, PMC10568059 |
| WACAR, Warangal (Telangana), 2018: 814 presumed-cardiac out-of-hospital arrests brought to MGM Hospital; median age 60; 43.6% in the "premature" age group; CPR reported in 139 of 500 with data; **no survivors**. Unfunded; PI Dr Srinivas Ramaka. | PMC7772591 |
| Less than 2% of Indians formally trained in CPR; OHCA survival under 10%. | ORF, Oct 2024 (secondary) |
| Over 80% of out-of-hospital arrests occur in private homes. | The Week, 31 May 2026 (opinion piece citing CSI) |

Implication: training families and apartment staff matters more than training crowds at stadium events.

**1.2 Training models available in India**

- **COLS (Compression-Only Life Support)** — the Indian layperson guideline, published 2017 by the Resuscitation Council formed by the Indian Society of Anaesthesiologists. Three links: early recognition and activation (108), early chest compression (cycles of 30, ~120/min, 5–6 cm), early transfer. Designed for bystanders unwilling to give rescue breaths. (PMC5702999; High.) The current accrediting body is usually named as the Indian Resuscitation Council Federation (IRCF); its own site (cprindia.in) did not load, so course fees, instructor pathway and certificate rules are **could not verify**.
- **AHA Heartsaver** — the American Heart Association's lay course (CPR, AED, choking; optional first aid). Hyderabad fee **could not verify**; the only local price found was Rs 4,900 per candidate for an AHA-affiliated BLS/ACLS provider course at a Begumpet centre (Docthub listing, undated; Low). Paid certification is unnecessary for a mass public programme; a certificate of participation is what large drives issue.
- **National drives** — NBEMS/MoHFW online CPR awareness programme, 6 Dec 2023: more than 20 lakh participants in one sitting (vikaspedia; High). MoHFW **CPR Awareness Week, 13–17 Oct 2025**: 6,06,374 trained, ~79,000 MyGov pledges, ~7.47 lakh engaged in all (NewsOnAir, 22 Oct 2025; High). NMC asked medical colleges to join. Whether it is fixed annually was not confirmed by the primary source. A MoHFW programme specifically called "Sanjeevani" for CPR: **could not verify** (the name belongs to eSanjeevani telemedicine and to Sri Sathya Sai Sanjeevani hospitals).
- **Schools** — no national or state mandate found. AIIMS/ICMR study trained 4,500 students in 15 schools; NITI Aayog and NCERT backed a national school CPR framework in July 2024 (Devdiscourse). Best operating model found: Sikar, Rajasthan (July 2026) trained ~1,500 PT instructors and science teachers from 807 government schools as trainers and gave each school a feedback manikin (advertorial; Medium). Hyderabad paediatrician Dr Sivaranjani Santosh is campaigning for CPR/first aid in the syllabus from Class 7 (Telangana Today, 23 Dec 2025).

Metro stations and the JBS/MGBS bus terminals were described in January and December 2025 as not equipped (Telangana Today, quoting Dr B Vijaya Rao). GHMC-specific deployment: **could not verify**.

**1.5 How one doctor runs "1,000 trained" (my estimate, not a sourced figure)**

- Kit: 6 basic manikins + 1 feedback manikin + 1 AED trainer ≈ Rs 1.0–1.3 lakh one-off, or borrow from the hospital's emergency department.
- Format: 60–90 minutes, 25–30 people, one manikin per 5 people, everyone does two minutes of compressions and one AED-trainer drill. About 35–40 sessions reach 1,000.
- People: he trains 6–10 volunteer instructors once (ED nurses, cath-lab technicians, physiotherapists); he personally leads one session in four.
- Channels in the western corridor: gated-community RWAs (families, security, housekeeping), IT companies through the SCSC Healthcare Forum (SCSC lists 250+ stakeholder companies and already runs NCD screening), schools via PT teachers (Sikar model), gyms.
- Pace: two sessions per weekend-month ≈ 10 months; double sessions ≈ 5–6 months.

### 2. Screening and prevention

**2.1 What the state already does (do not duplicate)**

- **Door-to-door NCD screening:** ~1.49 crore people screened at home; nearly 18 lakh identified with hypertension and over 9 lakh with diabetes (Principal Secretary Health, Deccan Chronicle, 11 Oct 2026; High for the statement).
- **NCD clinics:** opened December 2024 in 33 Government General Hospitals plus two Hyderabad hospitals; by March 2025 over 1.2 lakh screened, 8,457 new hypertension and 20,438 new diabetes cases (The Hindu via Webjosh; primary page down; Medium).
- **Screening strategy changed to targeted** in 2025-26: 9.96 lakh screened, 8.48 lakh diagnosed with diabetes (South First, 9 Dec 2025, citing MoHFW).
- **India Hypertension Control Initiative:** Telangana had 10 of the 26 Phase I districts, all with clinic-level BP control above 50%; protocol uses amlodipine, telmisartan and a diuretic at under US$3 per patient-year; community-level control was still only 5% (PMC10328822; High, data to 2020 — stale).
- Basti Dawakhana / Palle Dawakhana linkage details: **could not verify** this round.

So what: the bottleneck is control and follow-up, not detection. A private cardiologist's camp is useful only if every positive is handed to a named free clinic and re-checked.

**2.2 STEMI systems**

- **Tamil Nadu STEMI programme** (JAMA Cardiology 2017): 4 hubs, 35 spokes, 2,420 patients. PCI rose from 29.5% to 46.5%; in-hospital mortality unchanged (5.8% vs 5.6%); **one-year mortality 17.6% before vs 14.2% after, adjusted OR 0.76 (0.58–0.98)**. ICMR-funded; STEMI India designed the protocol (PMC5814984; High). STEMI India's site claims 19% lower long-term mortality and a 4x return per rupee; its office is in Coimbatore (stemiindia.com).
- **Telangana:** the programme is "STEMI-Telangana", launched June 2019 under NHM; state nodal officer Dr Raghu Kishore Galla. Dec 2022: 5 hubs (Osmania, Gandhi, NIMS, MGM Warangal, Khammam), 74 spokes, ~2 lakh ECGs, ~7,500 critical cases, ~3,000 STEMI, ~825 thrombolysed; 42.8% of patients were daily-wage workers and 31% farmers (NewsMeter; Medium–High). Osmania's cardiology head claimed "200 lives a month" and a 50% fall in rural cardiac deaths — official claims without published data (Low). The name "Mission STEMI": **could not verify**. Current (2025–26) figures: **could not verify**.
- **Karnataka** (Puneeth Rajkumar Hrudaya Jyothi): 85 spokes with Tricog AI-read ECG, free tenecteplase (Rs 30,000–45,000 privately), 50 public AEDs planned and delayed by failed tenders (South First 2023; Deccan Herald).

**2.3 Children**

- Sri Sathya Sai Sanjeevani: totally free paediatric cardiac care; homepage counters show 28,899 surgeries and 16,258 catheter interventions; fourth centre at **Kondapak, Telangana**, which marked 500 free surgeries in 2026 (srisathyasaisanjeevani.org; syndicated ANI).
- NIMS runs free paediatric cardiac surgery under Aarogyasri and the CM's Relief Fund, including annual UK-surgeon camps (one camp: 1,000+ screened, 19 operated) (Deccan Chronicle; Telangana Today).
- Rotary Gift of Life: ~120 children's surgeries a year, Rotary paying Rs 70,000–75,000 per surgery (Rotary News, 2017 — stale).
- Telangana RBSK and school rheumatic-heart screening data: **could not find**.

He is an adult interventionalist; the right role here is referral (knowing these three free routes), not running a programme.

**2.4 Other target groups**

- **IT employees:** NIN study in *Nutrients* (2023), 183 Hyderabad IT workers, mean age ~30: ~46% had three or more metabolic risk factors; only 22% met activity guidelines; over 8 hours sitting a day (Telangana Today; High for existence, small sample). Apollo's Health of the Nation 2025 reports for Hyderabad that three-quarters of hypertensives had fatty liver (The Week).
- **Familial hypercholesterolaemia:** ~15% of north Indian premature-CAD patients had potential FH; a worker-led screen of 2,549 people found definite FH in 0.59%; one Indian cascade study found 66% of tested relatives carried the family mutation (search summaries of Indian Heart J 2019, PLoS ONE 2022; Medium). Cascade screening starts from his own under-50 MI patients and needs no camp.
- **Drivers and gig workers:** Karnataka's health minister said 20–30% of heart-attack patients were auto and cab drivers and promised separate screening (Deccan Herald headline; page blocked; Low–Medium). In Hyderabad, camps for drivers found were eye or general camps; Rapido ran a CPR session for its captains in 2022. No cardiac programme for this group was found — an open niche.
- **Traffic police:** Yashoda Hospitals screened 1,000+ Cyberabad traffic police (pulmonary and vascular) with SCSC in Sept 2026 (Telangana Today). The channel exists; cardiac risk screening was not what was done.
- **Women's heart health:** not researched (search budget). WINCARS, a Hyderabad-rooted women-in-cardiology association, is a likely partner — **could not verify** current programmes.

### 3. Institutional vehicles

**3.1 CSR rules that decide the question (High unless stated)**

- Schedule VII item (i) covers "promoting healthcare including preventive healthcare" — CPR training, screening and AEDs fit.
- Companies above Rs 500 crore net worth, Rs 1,000 crore turnover or Rs 5 crore net profit must spend 2% of average three-year net profit.
- To receive CSR funds, an implementing agency (trust, society or Section 8 company) needs 12A registration and 80G approval, **Form CSR-1**, and — unless set up by the funding company or government — **at least three years of similar activity**. A lapsed 80G breaks eligibility. (Treelife; Patron Accounting, Apr 2026.)
- CSR-1 costs about Rs 3,500–7,500 including digital signature and CA certification; 12A/80G take 1–3 months; realistic time from registering an entity to first CSR grant is 3.5–4 years (Patron Accounting; Medium).
- Schedule VII gained a thirteenth item (Social Stock Exchange instruments) in May 2026 — not relevant here.
- From background knowledge, not re-verified: CSR spending that benefits only a company's own employees does not count as CSR, so training a company's staff is paid from its wellness or safety budget, while training its security, housekeeping and neighbouring community can be CSR (Medium).

**3.3 Time and governance**

Own trust: trustees, annual audit, income-tax filings, 80G renewals, and the reputational risk of any lapse sit with him for life. Partnering costs him control over branding and nothing else. Recommendation: partner for years 1–2; decide on a trust only once there is a three-year record worth institutionalising — the same record the CSR rules require anyway.

### 4. Patient community

- **Cardiac rehabilitation is almost absent:** an audit found 23 programmes in India, all responders in three southern states, one place per 360 ischaemic-heart-disease patients a year, mostly out-of-pocket; top barrier was lack of referral (Global Heart 2020; High, stale).
- **Yoga-CaRe** (JACC 2020): 3,959 post-MI patients; yoga-based rehab improved self-rated health and return to pre-infarct activities; MACE 6.7% vs 7.4%, not significant. A credible low-cost rehab format for India (Medium–High).
- **Text-message adherence:** Cochrane 2024, 18 trials, 8,136 participants — cannot say whether texting improves adherence; certainty low to very low; no fatal-event benefit. A WhatsApp programme must therefore be framed as education and support and measured, not sold as proven (High).
- **Peer visiting:** Mended Hearts (US, founded 1951 by surgeon Dwight Harken): 285 chapters, 21,000 volunteers, 460 partner hospitals (Wikipedia; Medium). Indian equivalent found: Patients United for Heart Failure Foundation (2018, AIIMS and Fortis cardiologists) — current activity could not verify.
- **Hospitals:** Manipal, Sakra and STAR advertise heart-failure clinics, some mentioning support groups. Apollo's World Heart Day 2025 campaign was screening-led; an Apollo-run survivors' meet or cardiac support group in Hyderabad: **could not verify** — ask the Apollo Financial District marketing and cardiac-rehab teams directly.

### 5. Policy and system change

- **Proven route:** an evidence-based public post that reaches a minister (the Madivada–KTR episode, 2023).
- **Open windows:** (a) Telangana draft factory-AED rule — written comments went to the Director of Factories; (b) the undelivered 2023 public-AED plan — a polite, data-based status request is legitimate; (c) school CPR — NCERT/NITI support exists, education is a concurrent subject, so a state can act; (d) a national OHCA registry is recommended by the published review and by CSI/ISA/IRCF.
- **Legal protection for rescuers:** sources cite the Good Samaritan Guidelines 2016 as covering good-faith AED use (law-firm blog; verify before repeating).
- **Data projects:** WACAR shows one unfunded cardiologist-led registry can become nationally cited. A Hyderabad young-MI registry is feasible at two hospitals with ethics-committee approval; existing Hyderabad young-MI registries: **not searched**.
- Trans-fat/salt, tobacco, air pollution: **not verified this round**; no claims made.

### 6. Teaching the next tier

- STEMI-Telangana's own nodal officer names the gap: peripheral doctors are unsure how to read ECGs (NewsMeter, 2022).
- STEMI India trains emergency physicians, nurses and paramedics in ECG diagnosis and thrombolysis (stemiindia.com).
- A 2024 Indian Heart Journal study from Himachal Pradesh on capacity building plus tele-ECG decision support reported lower in-hospital mortality in patients treated at spokes than at the hub (search summary only; Medium).
- ECHO India is the ready-made tele-mentoring platform: 400+ hubs, 29 NHM partnerships; MoHFW used it for CPR Week (echoindia.in). No cardiology programme is listed — an opening.
- Evidence on ASHA/ANM ECG training and a list of free online ECG courses: **not researched** (budget).
- Caution: teach qualified staff (MBBS doctors, nurses, paramedics) recognition and referral. Do not train unqualified "RMPs" to treat.

**Proposed scorecard** (publish annually, two pages, with method notes)

| Level | Measure | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Output | People trained hands-on (not "reached"); sessions; instructors certified | Count only those who did compressions on a manikin |
| Quality | % passing a skills check; % retained at 6-month re-test of a sample | Report the failures too |
| Screening | Screened → abnormal → linked to a named clinic → at target at 6 months | Report the whole cascade, including drop-off |
| Patients | % of cohort with LDL and BP at goal, on four drug classes at 12 months | Consent and de-identification |
| Outcome | Documented bystander interventions, each verified | Never extrapolate "lives saved" from trainee counts |
| Money | Rupees in, rupees out, source of every rupee | Publish even if small |

## So what for Dr. Avinash

**Priority actions**

1. **Pick one flagship and hold it for three years** — recommend Initiative A (CPR). One-off 3 hours to decide; then about 3 hours a week.
2. **Borrow a vehicle.** Meet Apollo Foundation/Billion Hearts Beating, the CSI Telangana chapter secretary and the SCSC Healthcare Forum. Three meetings of one hour, arranged by the friend.
3. **Train trainers first.** One 3-hour Saturday for 6–10 nurse and technician volunteers.
4. **Keep the scorecard from day one** in a single sheet maintained by the friend (15 minutes a week).
5. **Know the three free referral routes for children** (Sathya Sai Kondapak, NIMS/Aarogyasri, Rotary) and use them — zero programme time.
6. **Start FH cascade screening in his own clinic** for every MI patient under 50 — five minutes per patient.
7. **Publish one annual impact note** each World Heart Day (29 September) — 4 hours a year.

**Three candidate signature initiatives (one doctor + one helper)**

**A. Heart-Safe Communities — 1,000 people hands-on CPR-trained in the Financial District–Nallagandla–Gachibowli belt in 12 months** *(recommended flagship)*
- Why: largest verified gap; visible without self-praise (the trainees are the story); partners and precedent exist.
- Cost: Rs 1.0–1.5 lakh kit (or borrowed); venues free.
- Time: doctor ~3 hours a week for 12 weeks, then ~6 hours a month; helper ~4 hours a week.
- First 90 days:
  - Days 1–15: choose COLS as the curriculum; get hospital sign-off; borrow or buy 6 manikins and an AED trainer; one-page session plan in Telugu and English.
  - Days 16–30: train 6–10 instructors; pilot with hospital non-clinical staff (2 sessions, ~50 people); fix the script.
  - Days 31–60: 4 sessions in two gated communities (security and housekeeping staff included); 2 sessions at one IT company via SCSC; log every attendee.
  - Days 61–90: 1 school (train PT teachers); map which sites have an AED and who holds the key; publish first count (target 250–300); schedule 6-month re-test for a sample of 30.
- Scorecard: trained, skills-check pass rate, 6-month retention, AED-ready sites, verified interventions.

**B. "Second Innings" — a patient community for his post-angioplasty and heart-failure patients**
- Why: introvert-friendly (small rooms, his own patients); cardiac rehab barely exists; outcome data are his to publish.
- Design: quarterly 90-minute meet at the hospital (physiotherapist, dietitian, one patient speaker); opt-in broadcast-only WhatsApp list (written consent, no clinical advice by chat, one message a week); two trained patient volunteers for peer visits; annual survivors' walk on World Heart Day.
- Cost: under Rs 20,000 per meeting; hospital usually hosts.
- Time: doctor 3 hours a quarter plus 20 minutes a week reviewing messages; helper 2 hours a week.
- First 90 days: consent form and data sheet approved by the hospital (days 1–20); recruit first 40 patients at follow-up visits (days 21–50); first meet and baseline LDL/BP/medication audit (days 51–75); 12 weekly messages scheduled, feedback survey (days 76–90).
- Scorecard: enrolled, % at LDL and BP goal at 12 months, % on guideline therapy, attendance. Do not claim adherence benefit from messaging — evidence is uncertain.

**C. "First ECG" — monthly chest-pain triage teaching for first-contact clinicians**
- Why: the state programme itself names ECG reading as its weak point; teaching builds peer reputation, which drives referrals without advertising.
- Design: one 60-minute online case round a month (10 real, de-identified ECGs; "thrombolyse, transfer or reassure"), for GPs, casualty medical officers and nurses in western Hyderabad and nearby districts; recordings kept as a free library; offer the series to STEMI-Telangana and ECHO India as volunteer faculty.
- Cost: nil.
- Time: doctor 3 hours a month; helper 3 hours a month.
- First 90 days: collect 30 teaching ECGs with consent/de-identification (days 1–30); invite 30 clinicians through IMA branches and referring doctors, run round 1 (days 31–60); round 2, pre/post quiz, write to the STEMI-Telangana nodal officer and ECHO India offering the series (days 61–90).
- Scorecard: clinicians attending, pre/post quiz scores, later: time-to-ECG at participating clinics.

Later option (year 2): an under-45 heart-attack registry across his two hospitals with ethics approval, modelled on WACAR.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Camps as patient funnels.** Free camps that end in angiogram referrals to his own cath lab will be read as soliciting. The Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations 2002 prohibit soliciting patients; the primary text could not be fetched this round, so check wording before any camp publicity. Hand positives to free state clinics.
- **Inflated numbers.** "Reached" and "sensitised" counts and record attempts (5,000 in a day) trade skill for headlines. Count hands-on trainees only.
- **"Lives saved" claims** without a named, verified case.
- **Screening without follow-up** — creates anxiety, cost and no benefit.
- **AED donation without an owner** — batteries and pads expire; Karnataka's cardiologist adviser stressed monthly maintenance. Every device needs a named custodian and a check log.
- **WhatsApp groups** — use broadcast lists, written consent, no individual advice, no patient-identifiable posts.
- **Premature trust** — compliance burden, and no CSR eligibility for about three years anyway.
- **Sponsor capture** — device or drug company money for training kits is acceptable only with no product promotion and full disclosure in the annual note.
- **Hospital brand conflict** — two affiliations (Apollo, Citizens); agree in writing whose name appears on what.
- **Training unqualified practitioners** to treat.
- **Over-commitment** — one flagship, not seven.
