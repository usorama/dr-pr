# 27 — Reaching the people a YouTube-and-LinkedIn plan misses: an equity layer for Telugu heart education

**Research task R4-4. Date of research: 12 November 2026.**
Confidence marks: High / Medium / Low. **[BK]** = background knowledge not re-verified this round. "Could not verify" means exactly that.

## Executive summary

1. **The digital plan misses most of the people who die of heart disease.** In Telangana only 26.5% of women aged 15–49 had ever used the internet (rural 15.8%), 40% had no phone of their own, and one in three had never been to school (NFHS-5). Nationally 36% of women own a smartphone against 58% of men (GSMA 2025). Heart-disease death rates in rural India have overtaken urban rates, and the less educated die more often despite having fewer risk factors, because their care is worse (Million Death Study; PURE).
2. **Radio is not the mass channel people assume; television and people are.** Only 2% of Telangana women and 6% of men listen to radio weekly; 75% of both watch television weekly; 17% of women and 42% of men read a newspaper weekly; 20% of women have no regular media at all. For that last fifth the only channel is a person: ANM, ASHA, self-help-group leader, family member.
3. **The public system already has the people and the patients.** Telangana has screened about 1.49 crore people at home and found about 18 lakh with hypertension; it runs monthly drug kits, Tuesday women's clinics, and a health-profile drive for 46 lakh self-help-group women. A January 2025 national field visit recorded that ASHAs there were "not yet trained" for NCD checklists. Content and training offered through the health department sit inside an explicit exemption in the NMC guidelines (cl. 7.3 Explanation).
4. **Evidence favours trained local women plus measurement, not messages alone.** ASHA-led groups in rural Andhra Pradesh and Kerala improved blood-pressure control (OR 1.6); Kerala self-help-group peer support cut systolic pressure by 4.1 mm Hg; the world's largest voice-message programme (Kilkari) changed few behaviours and under-reached women without schooling.
5. **Recommended equity layer:** six low-cost re-cuts of the same weekly lesson (audio note, public-broadcaster talks, two open-licence pictographic print pieces, a tele-mentoring session for mid-level providers inside the state system, a self-help-group "heart talk" kit co-authored with a woman cardiologist, and a print-plus-family-relay route for older adults). About 2–3 extra doctor hours a month and roughly Rs 0.5–1 lakh in year 1. He should not build his own IVR line, train unqualified practitioners, or run stand-alone village camps.

**Method note.** Twelve web searches were used (the budget). The rest came from direct downloads of primary PDFs (NFHS-5 state reports and fact sheets, GSMA report, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting community-radio lists and listenership study, the MoHFW national NCD workshop report) and from PubMed / Europe PMC programme interfaces for abstracts. Several official sites were down or refused connections during the session (rchiips.org, prasarbharati.gov.in station pages, allindiaradio.gov.in, serp.telangana.gov.in, mcrhrdi.gov.in, npcbvi.mohfw.gov.in); those gaps are flagged.

**What this means.** A Telugu YouTube video reaches, at best, the 16% of rural Telangana women who had ever used the internet in 2019–21 plus whatever growth has occurred since. Text (captions, WhatsApp text, leaflets) fails for roughly half of rural women. Audio and pictures do not.

Two consequences that contradict common assumptions:

- **Radio is a niche channel, not a mass one.** It is still worth using (free, lawful, produces reusable audio, reaches some older rural men), but the reach claim must be modest.
- **One woman in five is outside all mass media.** She can be reached only face to face.

A 2016 Odisha community study found the same pattern: television was the main source of disease information (63.6%), radio 9.2%, only 8% had received any health message on a mobile phone in a year, 36% had seen a "health wall" painting, and for people with low literacy "folk media and interpersonal communication were found to be effective" (PMID 34604587; Medium; older, another state).

District-level NFHS-5 hypertension figures exist in district fact sheets, but the host (rchiips.org) returned errors all session. GBD state-level estimates were not re-fetched; file 14 already carries the state burden figures.

**The equity point.** The poor and less schooled do not mainly lack risk-factor lectures. They lack recognition, a route to treatment, and continuity. The equity layer should therefore carry three messages only: *recognise and act* (warning signs, call 108), *know and treat your numbers* (blood pressure, sugar), *do not stop the tablets* (after a stent or a heart attack).

#### 2.3 Newspapers as the route to older readers

- 42% of Telangana men but only 17% of women read a newspaper weekly (NFHS-5). Print therefore reaches **older men and the literate household member who relays information**, not older women directly.
- Eenadu's "Sukhibhava" health section is active; contributor routes and editors are in files 04 and 08 (editorial address infonet@eenadu.net; no named health editor verified). Sakshi and Andhra Jyothy health-page routes remain unverified.
- Lawful form: writing "under his own name on matters of public health" (2002 Reg. 7.11; cl. 8.2). No paid "health page" space.

**Reading.** No existing Telugu voice channel was found that accepts outside cardiac content. Building a private IVR line is poor value for one doctor (see "What not to attempt"). #### 2.5 WhatsApp voice notes and Telugu audio platforms

- GSMA 2025 shows why audio matters: women who do get online often do so on a borrowed phone, and a large minority of phone-owning women cannot read an SMS. A voice note needs no reading, no data plan after download, and can be played aloud to a group.
- No trial of WhatsApp voice notes for cardiovascular education in low-literacy Indian adults was found (Europe PMC search; could not verify).
- **Dasubhashitam** is a subscription Telugu audiobook and podcast app (operating since 2017, Bengaluru office; Rs 999–3,600 a year). Kuku FM and Pocket FM Telugu were not verified this round. [BK] These are paid entertainment platforms for smartphone owners; they do not reach the target group and are not a priority. The same audio can be listed free on standard podcast directories at no cost (file 05).

#### 3.2 Who trains ASHAs, ANMs and mid-level providers, and is there a heart module?

- National modules exist for ASHAs, ANMs/MPWs, community health officers and medical officers on population-based NCD screening (MoHFW/NHSRC; a WHO-hosted copy of the medical-officer module and an MoHFW CHO supplementary NCD module were located but the files could not be opened). [BK, Medium] Their content is screening, risk assessment, lifestyle advice, referral and follow-up for hypertension and diabetes; acute chest-pain recognition is thin.
- IHCI trains PHC teams by cadre and has Telugu nurse and ANM guides (above). It has no ASHA-specific module on its site.
- [BK, Medium] Training runs as a cascade under the State NHM (state trainers, then district trainers, then block level), with the state health and family welfare training institute and NHSRC/State Health Resource Centre support. Outside faculty participate when the Commissioner of Health and Family Welfare / Mission Director approves, usually under an MoU with a technical partner. Could not verify a Telangana-specific rule.
- **Evidence that this cadre can do the job.** Fifteen ASHAs given a five-day course raised their hypertension knowledge from 64% to 84% and ran village groups without help (PMID 29720161). In the trial that followed, in **West Godavari and Rishi Valley (Andhra Pradesh)** and Trivandrum, ASHA-led fortnightly group education with blood-pressure monitoring for three months improved control (69.7% vs 61.7% at follow-up; OR 1.6, 95% CI 1.2–2.1; systolic −5.0 mm Hg) (PMID 31895945; High; short follow-up). By contrast, ASHA lifestyle sessions without monitoring or treatment (DISHA) and tablet-based screening without assured treatment (SMARThealth, West Godavari) did not lower blood pressure (file 19).

#### 3.3 How an outside specialist can lawfully contribute

- **Legal footing.** NMC 2026 guidelines cl. 7.3 Explanation: "All campaigns on programmes/schemes of Government of India and the state Governments, which are in larger public interest, shall be exempted from these guidelines" (file 11). Training health workers is teaching, not advertising. Materials distributed through the department should carry the department's branding, not his clinic's.
- **Route (as far as could be established).** A written offer to the Commissioner of Health and Family Welfare and Mission Director, NHM Telangana (in January 2025 this was Shri R.V. Karnan; confirm the current officer), copied to the State NCD programme officer and the District Medical and Health Officer, Ranga Reddy. Offer something specific, free and small: a monthly 45-minute tele-session for mid-level providers and staff nurses, and print-ready Telugu job aids. Ask for a permission letter; an MoU follows only if the series continues. Individuals rarely sign MoUs; a host institution usually does (below).
- **Precedents.** Private cardiologists have built state systems in exactly this way: STEMI India with Tamil Nadu and later Telangana; Dr Padmanabha Kamath's ECG network in Karnataka (file 09). Noora Health's "Care Companion Program" trains families through government hospital staff and reports 16,400+ partner facilities and 65 million people trained across four countries; its site cites cardiac-care outcomes and [BK] it began with cardiac-surgery families (organisation-reported; Medium). A search hit indicates Andhra Pradesh signed an MoU with a foundation and oriented about 10,000 CHOs virtually for a care-companion flipchart programme (Low; deck not opened).

#### 3.4 Tele-mentoring: Project ECHO India

- **Scale.** ECHO India's site states 400+ hubs, 29 NHM partners and 19 AIIMS partners. Its listed health work covers cancer, mental health, tuberculosis, tribal health and nursing; **no cardiology, hypertension or STEMI programme is listed** (High for what the site shows).
- **How to become a hub.** The site describes: a one-day in-person orientation; signing "partnership documents"; a three-day immersion training ("currently virtual"); two-day partner launch training; then monthly free virtual trainings (roles, Zoom, the iECHO partner tool). Contact: training@echoindia.in. No fees are stated. The model is written for **organisations**; an individual needs a host (a medical college, a society chapter, a public-health institute, or the NHM itself).
- **Evidence.** In-person training plus three months of video mentoring for community health officers and mid-level providers in **Telangana (66 participants)** and Tripura significantly improved knowledge, attitude and practice scores; 92% of the patients they then brought to video consultations were first-time users of that service (mental health; PMID 41104324; Medium, uncontrolled). File 19 has the wider ECHO evidence.
- **Sensible sequence.** Year 1: guest faculty inside a session someone else hosts (NHM Telangana, STEMI-Telangana, an existing ECHO hub). Year 2: his own "First ten minutes" series with a host hub, once he has a track record and a host.

### 4. Women's networks

#### 4.1 Self-help-group federations

- **Scale.** SERP (rural, under Panchayat Raj and Rural Development) launched a 2025–26 plan covering **3.55 lakh SHGs**; the state speaks of about **46 lakh SHG women** and a target of one crore members. The PR&RD Secretary said SHG activity is "expanding into health, nutrition, human trafficking awareness, and digital fraud prevention", and new groups are being formed for adolescent girls and for **women over 60** (Siasat, 9 May 2025; PTI, 4 Mar 2026; High for the statements). SERP's CEO in May 2025 was Divya Devarajan. MEPMA (urban) figures and any health module were **not found**; the SERP site was down.
- **Structure** [BK, Medium]: SHG (10–15 women, weekly or fortnightly meeting) → village organisation → mandal samakhya → zilla samakhya; in towns, slum-level and town-level federations under MEPMA. Meetings have a fixed agenda and a book-keeper, which is why an audio item of a few minutes can be slotted in.
- **Evidence.**
  - Kerala urban slums, 1,952 people with hypertension: SHG members (one per 20–30 households) gave daily support, encouragement and referral; net systolic fall 4.09 mm Hg at six months (PMID 36384950; High).
  - Systematic review of 99 studies of women's groups in India: moderate-quality evidence of benefit for perinatal, immunisation and nutrition outcomes; "the most effective interventions go beyond using groups to disseminate health information"; barriers include "limited time or focus on health" and "outcomes not relevant to group members" (PMID 33328199; High). No cardiovascular outcomes were in that review.
- **How partners work with them** (could not verify a formal procedure): through the district collector or the SERP/MEPMA project director, with the health department present. The 2026 SHG health-profile drive creates a natural opening: lakhs of newly identified hypertensive women who need follow-up.

#### 4.2 Anganwadi / ICDS and the Tuesday women's clinic

- Anganwadi centres serve mothers and young children; their staff co-delivered the DISHA lifestyle intervention that did not lower blood pressure (file 19). They are a place to hang a poster, not a cardiac channel.
- The **Arogya Mahila** Tuesday clinic at PHCs is a better fit: women are already there for screening, and a blood-pressure cuff is in the room.

#### 4.3 WINCARS and women's heart-health programmes

- **Correction to earlier files.** WINCARS (Women in Cardiology and Related Sciences) was registered as a society in 2015 (no. 559). Its **secretariat is in Visakhapatnam** (KGH Down Road, Maharanipeta), not Hyderabad; email wincarssecretariat@gmail.com (wincarsassociation.com; High).
- **Activities listed:** public awareness through "meets, webinars, quarterly conferences, CMEs, and free camps"; an annual Women Cardiac Care conference on the eve of International Women's Day; monthly e-classes, interventional rounds and a heart-failure conclave; the Prajwalika scholarship; the *Indian Journal of Cardiovascular Disease in Women* (quarterly). A "Shakti Conclave" on gender gaps was held with STEMI India (Lupin page; undated).
- No Hyderabad community programme for 2025–26 was found, and no India-wide "Go Red" programme with a named owner was verified. Women's heart health in Telangana remains unclaimed space.
- **Fit for him.** A male interventionalist should not front women's heart health alone. Co-authoring the women's kit with a WINCARS member gives it credibility, shares the credit and gives WINCARS a ready community product.

#### 4.4 Messaging that fits the evidence

From file 14 and the studies above:

1. "Women also get chest pain", plus breathlessness, sweating, nausea, unusual tiredness. Do not teach "women's symptoms are different"; teach "women wait longer".
2. Name the delay honestly: women finish the housework, wait for a man to come home, do not want to trouble the family. Give the permission: "Tell someone now. Call 108."
3. One number, one action per piece. Blood pressure checked today at the Tuesday clinic is a better "ask" than five lifestyle rules.
4. Address the relayer as well as the patient: daughters, daughters-in-law and SHG leaders.

### 5. Design for low literacy and older adults

#### 5.1 What the evidence supports

| Practice | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Health-literacy universal precautions**: simplify for everyone, confirm understanding with everyone, do not try to guess who can read | AHRQ Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit, **third edition**, 23 short tools in five areas (spoken communication, written communication, self-management, supportive systems); page last reviewed January 2025 | High |
| **Pictures with few words** | Meta-analysis of 54 RCTs: pictorial information moderately improved understanding and recall overall and "largely increased" understanding in lower-literacy groups; "icons with few words may be most helpful"; high heterogeneity | PMID 32466864; High |
| **Teach-back** ("tell me in your words what you will do") | Heart-failure discharge education using teach-back: readmission OR 0.40 (0.17–0.94), six studies of mixed quality | PMID 36411152; Medium |
| Why it matters clinically | Low literacy was independently associated with uncontrolled blood pressure (OR 1.75) in patients with hypertension and coronary disease, and with heart-failure hospitalisation (IRR 1.36) | PMID 24882088; 23478997; High (US data) |
| **Folk theatre** | Kalajatha performances for two weeks in a Karnataka district produced significant knowledge and attitude gains on malaria two months later; hygiene behaviour changed less | PMID 17173672; Medium; 2001 data |
| **Faith settings** | Mosque-based monthly sessions led by imams and trained women in rural Bangladesh cut 12-month diabetes incidence from 17.1% to 9.8% in people with prediabetes | PMID 41114978; High. No Indian temple, church or mosque cardiovascular trial was found |
| **Informal providers (RMPs)** | They give more than 70% of primary care in rural India. Seventy-two training sessions over nine months raised correct case management by 7.9 points but did not reduce unnecessary medicines or antibiotics | Das et al., Science 2016, PMID 27846471; High |

Not found: any trial of pictographic cardiac materials in Telugu; any evidence on wall calendars as a health medium; any study of pharmacy-counter cardiac education in India. These are plausible touchpoints with no outcome evidence.

#### 5.2 Telugu readability and typography

- **No validated Telugu readability formula was found** (OpenAlex and Europe PMC searches returned only speech and summarisation research). File 14's rule stands: test with listeners, not formulas.
- Working norms (general accessibility practice [BK], not Telugu-specific research):
  - Print for older readers: body text at least 16–18 point; Telugu conjuncts need generous line spacing; a Unicode open-licence face such as Noto Sans Telugu; dark text on a pale plain background; no text over photographs.
  - Screen: contrast at least 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.2 level AA); captions burned in at large size; every video also released as audio-only with a text transcript.
  - Audio: one message per piece; under two minutes; slow, everyday spoken Telugu; the single action said three times (start, middle, end); no English drug names without the Telugu description ("the blood-thinning tablet"); a second version checked by a Telangana-dialect speaker, since his own register is probably Coastal Andhra (file 25).
  - Pictures: one idea per picture; local dress and faces; a clock or sun and moon for timing (the state's own drug kit already uses three colours for morning, afternoon, night, so reuse that code); avoid abstract symbols such as a lightning bolt for pain until tested.
- **Pre-test protocol:** show each card to ten people who did not finish primary school (clinic attendants, patients' relatives, SHG members). Ask "What is this telling you to do?" Revise anything fewer than eight of ten get right. This is the AHRQ "confirm understanding" step applied to materials.

#### 5.3 Folk and storytelling formats in Telugu

- **Burrakatha** is a three-performer narrative form (lead narrator with tambura, a comic and a commentator) developed in its modern form around Guntur in 1942 for carrying political and social messages to rural audiences who could not read; it is still performed on radio and television (Wikipedia; Medium).
- **Oggu katha and dappu** announcements were used in Telangana villages for COVID-19 awareness (The News Minute, cited in search results; Medium).
- **Telangana Samskruthika Sarathi**: government-employed folk artists (583 posts reported under the previous government) tour villages with songs on state schemes; a 2024 tour in Jogulamba Gadwal promoted welfare schemes including Rajiv Aarogyasri (The Hans India, 29 Nov 2024; Medium). No NCD or heart campaign by these troupes was found.
- **Reading.** A doctor cannot run a troupe. He can supply a medically checked **story outline and five facts** to people who do: the district public-relations office, the Sarathi artists, a community radio station that produces its own drama. Low effort; reach unmeasurable; do it once, opportunistically.

Each month, one of the four weekly lessons is chosen for equity re-cutting, on rotation across three messages only: (A) warning signs and 108; (B) know and treat your blood pressure and sugar; (C) after the stent, do not stop the tablets. ### What should NOT be attempted by a single doctor

1. **His own IVR or missed-call line for the public.** Kilkari needed a ministry, a telecom back end and US$8.4 million over about four and a half years in 13 states, and still moved few behaviours. Bulk voice calls to people who have not opted in raise telecom-regulation and data-protection problems (not researched in detail; lawyer question), and callers will ask clinical questions he cannot answer safely. Voice reminders belong only inside the consented patient-follow-up system.
2. **Training RMPs or other unqualified practitioners in treatment.** The best trial showed modest gains and no reduction in unnecessary drugs; the state medical council's stance on unqualified practice makes this legally hazardous (current Telangana position not verified this round). If the health department itself runs a "recognise and refer" session, he may teach in it.
3. **Stand-alone village screening camps.** Screening without an assured treatment route does not lower blood pressure (SMARThealth, DISHA: file 19), and camps carry inducement risk (cl. 8.1(x)). Measure only where a public clinic is in the room.
4. **Training ASHAs directly, or placing his own-branded materials in government facilities, without written permission.**
5. **Paid broadcast slots or advertorial "health pages".**
6. **His own community radio licence, folk troupe, app or SMS service.** Supply content to people who already run these.
7. **Promising outcomes.** No public statement that the equity layer "prevents heart attacks". Report counts.
8. **Dialects and languages he does not command, unreviewed.** Telangana Telugu, Urdu/Deccani and Hindi versions need a native reviewer each time; a wrong word on a tablet card is a patient-safety event.
9. **Women's heart health as a solo project.** Co-author with a woman cardiologist.

## Risks & things to avoid

- **Stale access data.** NFHS-5 is 2019–21. Smartphone access has risen since, especially among younger women. The equity case rests on gradients (rural, older, unschooled), which GSMA 2025 confirms persist nationally, but the absolute Telangana numbers today are unknown.
- **Posters with a doctor's name in other people's premises.** A poster in a pharmacy or another clinic that carries his name could be read as advertisement (cl. 3.2 "manner of dissemination"). Keep the credit line small, leave out all practice details, let the displaying facility's stamp dominate, and add this to the lawyer's list.
- **Open licence versus medical accuracy.** A fully open licence lets others alter doses or add product branding. BY-NC-ND reduces this but blocks translation without permission; the mitigation is a stated "ask for the editable file" route. Either way, date every version and withdraw superseded ones.
- **Government partnership risks.** Slow permissions; officers transfer; a change of government can rename or end a programme (Palle and Basti Dawakhanas date from the previous government). Keep the first ask tiny and district-level; keep all materials usable outside the programme.
- **Contradicting the state protocol.** Anything taught to public staff must match the Telangana IHCI protocol and the NP-NCD modules. Read them first; where he disagrees, say nothing in that forum.
- **Employer and exclusivity.** Teaching government staff and appearing on public broadcasters under his own name is allowed by the NMC text, but his current hospital contract may require notice (file 17).
- **Overload.** Six items is the menu, not the minimum. If time is short the order of value is: E6 (free), E3 (durable), E4 (largest multiplier), E1, E5, E2.
- **Over-reading radio and community radio.** Do not report "coverage population" as audience.
- **Token equity.** A Telugu audio file nobody plays is not reach. Each item above has a named human distributor; if no partner confirms within a quarter, stop that item.
- **Data.** SHG tallies and clinic follow-up counts must be aggregate only; no names leave the health facility (DPDP; file 18).
