# Does having his own private practice ease the NMC advertising rules? A clause-cited reading (supplement to file 11)

**Executive summary**

1. **No rule is relaxed because the practice is his own.** The Guidelines' education permission is conditional on content that does "not promote personal practice" (cl. 5.1). That condition bites harder on an owner than the "without promoting their employer organization" condition (cl. 8.2(iii)) bites on an employee.
2. **What he gains is control over a factual footprint, not a licence to promote.** For his own clinic he may put up a signboard (2002 Reg. 7.13), display fees (Reg. 3.7.1, a duty), issue formal announcements on seven listed occasions (Reg. 6.1.1; Guidelines 8.2(ii), 9.3), publish factual clinic particulars (8.3(i), 9.1), hold his own Google Business Profile and directory entries, and hand out leaflets (8.2(iv)).
3. **What gets stricter:** his clinic is a "Hospital/Medical Institution" under cl. 3.4 and a "clinical establishment" under the central Act (which lists "a single doctor"). He is then answerable twice, as RMP to the medical council and as the establishment under clinical-establishment law (cl. 11.5), with no marketing department to blame (cl. 4.4; Reg. 7.18).
4. **Safest wording on educational content:** name, registered qualifications, "Interventional cardiologist, Hyderabad", council and registration number. No clinic name, no hospital name, no address, phone or link. Clinic details live only on static factual pages.
5. **Unknown and decisive:** what "his own private practice" actually is (owned standalone clinic, rented chamber, or visiting arrangement), whether it is registered under Telangana's clinical-establishment regime, and what his Apollo agreement says about outside practice.

> **Method note.** Based on the Guidelines text, the 2002 Regulations and the NMC Act already retrieved for file 11. One additional search and two page fetches were used for Telangana's clinical-establishment law. Same convention: **(a) Text**, **(b) Interpretation**, **(c) Unsettled**. Research, not legal advice.

## Findings

### 1. Which provisions distinguish his own practice from a hospital or employer, and what that allows

**(a) Text**

*Guidelines (6 Oct 2026)*

- 3.4: "Hospital/Medical Institution" means "a clinical establishment providing facilities and services for the diagnosis, treatment or care of illness ... where patients may be admitted and treated as in-patients **or treated as out-patients** ... and includes an institution covered under Section 2(c)(i) of the Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010/ any other State Act as applicable."
- 5.1: education is permitted "provided that such activities do not promote personal practice, do not solicit patients and are not monetized through promotional marketing."
- 5.2: public hoardings "may be used by hospitals/medical institutions and RMPs only for dissemination of factual information in the nature of infrastructure, facilities etc."
- Explanation II to 3.2: information on "availability or installation of equipment, technology, diagnostic facilities or services may be disseminated as factual information", without superiority, guarantee or comparative claims.
- Explanation IV: "Clinical Establishments while publishing any Electronic media posts should transparently disclose the names of the RMP with qualifications, registration status and SMR/NMR Registration number."
- 8.1(x): "Any lawful disclosure of charges, packages or fees shall be factual, transparent and not misleading."
- 8.2(ii): a practitioner may "make a formal announcement regarding the following: a) On starting practice. b) On change of type of practice. c) On changing address. d) On temporary absence from duty. e) On resumption of another practice. f) On succeeding to another practice. g) Public declaration of charge."
- 9.3: "RMP can issue a formal announcement which are factual, non-promotional and limited in scope regarding starting of practice, change of address, change of practice type, temporary absence, resumption of practice, public declaration of consultation fees."
- 8.2(iv): pamphlets may be issued "to the patients visiting their clinic/ hospital", without guarantees, superiority or unsubstantiated claims.
- 8.3(i): institutions "may disseminate factual, objective and verifiable information concerning their name, location, contact details, departments, facilities, equipment, services, emergency services, accreditation status, charges and other institutional information permitted by applicable law."
- 8.3(ii): such communication "shall not contain patient solicitation, inducement, comparative superiority claims, guarantees ... or promotional portrayal of an individual RMP. The form, medium or frequency of communication shall not convert otherwise factual information into solicitation or promotional self-aggrandizement."
- 9.1: clinical establishments "may advertise only factual information ... including the name of the establishment, contact details, departments functioning, diagnostic facilities, emergency services, accreditation status, other facilities offered and applicable fees."
- 9.2: self-photograph "on the letterhead or signboard of a consulting room or clinical establishment shall be governed by Regulations 6.1.1 and 6.1.2 ... and regarded as unethical conduct."

*2002 Regulations (binding; prevail in conflict, cl. 4.2)*

- 6.1.1: "A medical practitioner is however permitted to make a formal announcement in press regarding the following: (1) On starting practice. (2) On change of type of practice. (3) On changing address. (4) On temporary absence from duty. (5) On resumption of another practice. (6) On succeeding to another practice. (7) Public declaration of charges."
- 7.13: "It is improper for a physician to use an unusually large sign board and write on it anything other than his name, qualifications obtained from a University or a statutory body, titles and name of his speciality, registration number including the name of the State Medical Council under which registered. The same should be the contents of his prescription papers. It is improper to affix a sign-board on a chemist's shop or in places where he does not reside or work."
- 3.7.1: "A physician shall clearly display his fees and other charges on the board of his chamber and/or the hospitals he is visiting." 3.7.2: "A physician shall write his name and designation in full along with registration particulars in his prescription letter head."
- 1.4.1: registration number displayed "in his clinic and in all his prescriptions, certificates, money receipts".
- 6.1.2: self-photograph "in the letter head or on sign board of the consulting room or any such clinical establishment" is self-advertisement; "sketches, diagrams, picture of human system" are not.
- 7.12: "An institution run by a physician for a particular purpose such as a maternity home, nursing home, private hospital, rehabilitation centre or any type of training institution etc. may be advertised in the lay press, but such advertisements should not contain anything more than the name of the institution, type of patients admitted, type of training and other facilities offered and the fees."
- 7.18: where a physician runs a nursing home and employs assistants, "the ultimate responsibility rests on the physician." 7.19: no "touts or agents for procuring patients".

*Not in either text:* visiting cards, websites of individual doctors, Google Business Profile, clinic names, the content template or frequency of a formal announcement.

**(c) Unsettled**

1. Whether an outpatient consulting room is an "institution" for Reg. 7.12 and so may be advertised in the lay press at all.
2. Whether a formal announcement may be made on social media and how long it may stay up; whether a clinic's ordinary "we are open today" posts count as announcements or as solicitation.
3. Whether a plain identifying headshot on his own website or listing is "self-advertisement" under 9.2. (The express ban covers letterhead and signboard.)
4. Whether a clinic name may contain descriptive or laudatory words ("Advanced", "Super Speciality", "Heart Institute"). Not addressed; 8.1(ix) and Reg. 7.20 suggest restraint.

### 2. Does "without promoting their employer organization" restrict mentioning his own clinic?

**(a) Text.** 8.2(iii): talks may be given "in their own name and designation, without promoting their employer organization." 5.1: educational activities must "not promote personal practice" and "not solicit patients". 8.1(ii): an RMP shall not "promote his/her professional services through self-promotion". Reg. 7.11: no lay-press articles or interviews "which may have the effect of advertising himself or soliciting practices". Explanation I exempts information given "without any intent for commercial benefit".

**(b) Interpretation.** The employer clause is not the operative one for his own clinic; 5.1 is, and it is tighter. An employer is at least a separate entity. His clinic is "personal practice" in terms, and the commercial benefit of a mention flows directly to him, which also weakens any reliance on Explanation I. A clinic name is not a "designation". So the own-practice fact gives no extra room in educational content. Confidence: High.

**Safest lawful wording**

- **End-card, caption footer, byline on any educational item (use this everywhere):**
  `Dr Avinash Pathuri, MBBS, MD, DM (Cardiology) | Interventional cardiologist, Hyderabad | Registered Medical Practitioner, [Council] Reg. No. [____] | General health education, not personal medical advice. In an emergency call 108.`
  No clinic name, no hospital name, no address, phone, link, fee or hours.
- **Newspaper or magazine byline:** "The writer is an interventional cardiologist in Hyderabad." If the outlet insists on an affiliation, one plain mention, and prefer the neutral city form.
- **Profile bio (static, factual), safest version:** the same line as the end-card and nothing else.
- **Profile bio, defensible version:** add one plain line of fact, for example "Practises at [clinic name], [locality], and [hospital], Hyderabad." No phone, no "book", no link to an appointment page. Basis: these are the particulars Reg. 7.13 and Guidelines 8.3(i) treat as factual. Confidence: Medium.
- **Where clinic details belong:** signboard, prescription pad, the clinic website's contact page, Google Business Profile and directory entries. Someone who wants to find him can; the teaching does not carry the route.
- **If a viewer asks in comments where he practises:** one fixed reply, "Practice details are on my profile page." Do not post address, phone or a link in the thread. Confidence: Medium.

**(c) Unsettled.** Whether naming his clinic in a bio, or linking a bio to a clinic website, by itself "promote[s] personal practice" and so colours every educational post on that account. Whether "employer organization" reaches an entity he owns is academic, because 5.1 already covers it.

### 3. What gets stricter when the entity is his own

**(a) Text.** 3.4 (clinic within "Hospital/Medical Institution"). 4.3: for institutions the Guidelines are read with the applicable Clinical Establishment Act or State Act, which "shall override" in an irreconcilable conflict. 4.4: no escape through intermediaries for content "authorized, commissioned, sponsored, adopted or knowingly permitted". 8.1(xi): no use of an "employee or third party" to promote through testimonials or recommendations. 8.3(ii): no "promotional portrayal of an individual RMP". 10.1 Explanation and 11.5: the institution is dealt with under clinical-establishment law, and the RMP who "personally authorized, participated in, endorsed or otherwise been responsible" is examined separately. Reg. 7.18: "ultimate responsibility rests on the physician." Clinical Establishments Act, 2010 (as reproduced by Medical Dialogues): s.2(c)(i) covers "a hospital, maternity home, nursing home, dispensary, clinic, sanatorium or an institution by whatever name called"; s.2(c)(ii) includes an establishment owned or managed by "a single doctor"; s.40 residual penalty up to Rs 10,000 (first), Rs 50,000 (second), Rs 5 lakh (subsequent); s.41(1) operating without registration up to Rs 50,000, Rs 2 lakh, Rs 5 lakh. No advertising provision appears in the text I could see.

**(b) Interpretation**

1. **Two hats, one head.** As employee he could say "the hospital published it" and rely on a written objection. As owner there is nobody else: every signboard, listing, post, receptionist script and WhatsApp message is his authorised communication. Both enforcement tracks in 11.5 end at him. Confidence: High.
2. **Staff are his voice.** A receptionist who asks happy patients for Google reviews, forwards "testimonials", or sends offers on WhatsApp is an "employee" under 8.1(xi). A written staff instruction is needed. Confidence: High.
3. **The hospital-style latitude is small and, for a solo clinic, nearly empty.** 8.3(i) allows "institutional information". A hospital has departments, beds, equipment and accreditation to describe. A one-doctor consulting room has a name, an address, hours, fees and one doctor. Anything beyond that is "promotional portrayal of an individual RMP" (8.3(ii)), because the institution *is* the individual. He gets no more than Reg. 7.13 plus contact details, fees and plainly stated facilities. Confidence: Medium-High.
4. **Clinic posts need Explanation IV disclosure** (his name, qualifications, registration status and number) on each post. Confidence: Medium-High.
5. **A clinic social account is the weak point.** Regular posting from an account whose purpose is the clinic is hard to defend as "limited in scope", and "form, medium or frequency" can convert fact into solicitation. Recommended design: **no active clinic social account**; one personal educational account with no clinic content; clinic facts on static listings only. Confidence: Medium.
6. **Education must not funnel to the clinic.** With an owned practice the commercial link is direct, so the firewall matters more: no clinic branding, links or contact in educational assets; on the clinic website, keep patient-education pages free of booking prompts. Confidence: Medium-High.
7. **Inducement and referral rules now apply to his own pricing and arrangements:** no discounts, camps with packages, coupons or "free consultation" days (8.1(x)); no commission or fee-splitting with labs, pharmacies, aggregators or referrers (8.1(vii); Reg. 6.4); no agents or touts (Reg. 7.19); no lead-generation deals with platforms (8.1(viii)). Confidence: High.
8. **Clinical-establishment compliance becomes his.** Telangana adopted the central Clinical Establishments Act: Medical Dialogues (25 June 2022) reports a government order requiring "all kinds of healthcare facilities" to register with the District Registering Authority, with a registration fee of Rs 4,000 "in case of solo practitioner". A search summary (unverified secondary source) says the State runs it through 2022 rules and repealed the 2002 Allopathic Private Medical Care Establishments Act in 2025. I found **no advertising provision** in the central Act, so clause 4.3 gives him no State-law permission that overrides the Guidelines; what the law adds is a registration duty and fines. An unregistered or lapsed clinic registration is a larger and likelier exposure than any post. Confidence: Medium on Telangana's current instrument; Medium-High that solo clinics are covered.
9. **The employer relationship.** Publicising a personal clinic while holding a hospital attachment can breach the consultant agreement (outside practice, non-solicitation of hospital patients, brand use). Moving patients between the two for his benefit also raises Reg. 6.4 questions. Confidence: Medium; contract not seen.

**(c) Unsettled**

1. Which instrument governs clinics in Telangana today (central Act with State rules of which year; status of the 2002 State Act), what it requires to be displayed, and whether it says anything on advertising. Not verified against the gazette.
2. Whether the NMC or TGMC will treat a solo clinic's communication as "institutional" (8.3) or simply as the RMP's own (8.1). The second reading is stricter and more likely.
3. Whether a consulting room in rented or shared premises is "his" establishment or the landlord's for registration and liability.

### 5. Changes to file 11's checklist, safe formats and questions

**Checklist (file 11, section 9(i))**

- Item 11, add: "No clinic name, clinic logo, address, map pin, hours, fee or clinic link anywhere in an educational item, including the end-card, watermark, background and description."
- Item 12, rename "Employer and own practice": neither the hospital nor his clinic is promoted; designation is "Interventional cardiologist, Hyderabad".
- New item 13A (clinic communications only): "Is this a factual particular, an event-tied formal announcement, or a patient leaflet? Does it carry his name, qualifications, registration status and number (Expl. IV)? Is it a one-off, not part of a series?"

- New fixed reply: "Practice details are on my profile page."
- New quarterly task: audit the clinic's own signboard, stationery, website, Google listing and directory entries against Reg. 7.13 and Guidelines 8.3(i).

## So what for Dr. Avinash

1. **Establish the facts first** [doctor, 30 min]: what the practice is, where, under what name, whether registered as a clinical establishment, and what the hospital agreement says. Everything else depends on this.
2. **Bring the clinic's physical and printed footprint to the Reg. 7.13 standard** [helper checklist 1 h; printing as needed]: signboard, fee board, prescription pad, receipts, cards. Add the registration number and council; remove any photograph or slogan.
3. **Build the factual digital footprint once** [helper, 2–3 h]: one practice-information page, one Google Business Profile under the clinic's real name, corrected directory entries. Identical particulars everywhere. No review prompts.
4. **Write the one-page staff instruction** [30 min] on reviews, testimonials, offers and messaging.
5. **Keep the educational channel clinic-free** [no extra effort]: the end-card wording in section 2 is the rule.
6. **Use the formal-announcement route** for any change (new address, new hours, absence), once, in the fixed template [10 min per event].
7. **Add questions 18–25 to the lawyer's brief** [no extra meeting].

## Risks & things to avoid

- Reading "it is my own clinic" as permission to advertise it. The text gives none; 5.1 names "personal practice".
- A clinic social account that posts regularly. It is the form most likely to be read as solicitation by frequency.
- Staff-driven review requests and WhatsApp broadcasts. They are his acts.
- Relying on Reg. 7.12 to run newspaper or hoarding advertisements for a consulting room.
- An unregistered or lapsed clinical-establishment registration, or an APMC-only medical registration, discovered through a complaint about something else.
- Cross-promotion between the hospital attachment and the clinic, which can breach the agreement and raise Reg. 6.4 questions.
- Laudatory clinic names and keyword-stuffed listing names.

## Open questions / gaps

1. The nature, name, location and registration status of his private practice; whether any of the Round 1 listings (for example the Narsingi clinic entry) is in fact his own.
2. Telangana's current clinical-establishment instrument and rules, verified from the gazette, including any display or advertising provisions.
3. His hospital agreement's clauses on outside practice and publicity.
4. Any council or NMC statement on how solo clinics are treated under clause 8.3 versus 8.1. None found.
5. Visiting-card and website rules: the text of the 2023 Regulations (in abeyance) addressed some of this and was not re-read for this note.
